Major AI Updates
NVIDIA (Q2 FY27 earnings release, 8-K)

Nvidia doubles revenue to $96.2 billion and guides next quarter to $108 billion

Nvidia reported fiscal Q2 revenue of $96.2 billion on August 26, up 106% from a year ago, with data center revenue of $89 billion — 117% growth and 92% of total sales. Adjusted EPS of $2.46 beat the $2.10 consensus. Guidance calls for $108 billion in the current quarter, plus or minus 2%, excluding China data center revenue, against a $104.2 billion consensus, with gross margin guided to 74%. Shares rose 4.7% after hours.

Why it matters: The print answers Monday's question on whether DRAM inflation and price increases would dent demand or margins: a 74% gross-margin guide says no, for now. The ex-China framing is the number to model — any China license revival is upside not in guidance. Expect hyperscaler and power names to trade off this guide into September.
CNBC

OpenAI publishes Jalapeño benchmarks claiming efficiency lead over Nvidia Blackwell systems

OpenAI released the first measured results for Jalapeño, the inference chip it co-developed with Broadcom, on August 25. Across three open-weight models on SemiAnalysis's public InferenceX benchmark, the company reported 1.5–1.9x more compute per watt at peak throughput and 1.7–3.6x lower end-to-end latency than Nvidia GB200 and GB300 systems, with some runs verified on-site by SemiAnalysis. The chip handles inference only, and OpenAI plans to deploy it in its own infrastructure by the end of 2026.

Why it matters: Custom silicon reaching parity-plus on inference efficiency is the clearest structural threat to Nvidia's pricing power — and it landed one day before Nvidia's print. If Jalapeño deploys on schedule, the ASIC supply chain (Broadcom foremost) captures spend that would otherwise flow to GPUs. Watch whether other frontier labs publish comparable numbers.
Bloomberg

SoftBank weighs bond sale of up to $20 billion to refinance OpenAI loan

Bloomberg reported on August 26 that SoftBank is discussing a $10–20 billion bond offering with banks, in dollars and euros, that could price as early as September. Proceeds would help repay the $40 billion bridge loan backing its OpenAI investment, which is set to reach roughly $65 billion by October. At the top of the range it would be the largest bond deal from any Asian company this year; SoftBank says nothing is decided.

Why it matters: AI equity exposure is converting into rated, tradeable credit at size. A benchmark SoftBank deal would give fixed income desks the first liquid pricing reference for OpenAI-linked risk, alongside Broadcom's mooted Anthropic financing. Spread performance on this paper will signal how much AI concentration credit markets will absorb — relevant to every AI-adjacent issuer's cost of capital.
CNBC

Amazon will shut Mechanical Turk on September 30 after 21 years

Amazon confirmed this week it will close Mechanical Turk, the crowdsourced micro-task marketplace launched in 2005, on September 30, 2026. The platform stopped accepting new customers on July 30 and served more than 500,000 workers at its peak, handling data labeling, transcription, and survey work. Amazon cited an internal assessment of its programs; specialized AI-data firms Scale AI, Mercor, and Prolific now dominate the human-feedback market.

Why it matters: A concrete data point for the AI labor-substitution thesis: generic crowdwork is being absorbed by models, while human data work consolidates into specialized, higher-priced expert networks. For investors in the AI supply chain, the value has migrated from task marketplaces to firms recruiting domain experts — the segment where Mercor and Scale are raising at premium multiples.
AI in Wealth Management
Vanguard (announcement)

Vanguard agrees to buy AI-forward custodian Altruist for $4.6 billion in cash

Vanguard announced on August 26 an all-cash agreement to acquire Altruist, the AI-forward RIA custody and wealth-technology platform founded by CEO Jason Wenk — more than double Altruist's $1.9 billion valuation from April 2025. Altruist will operate as a standalone business under Wenk, with the deal expected to close later this year. Vanguard has been an investor since 2020, and former Vanguard chairman and CEO Bill McNabb has sat on Altruist's board since 2021.

Why it matters: The RIA custody duopoly of Schwab and Fidelity now faces a challenger with Vanguard's balance sheet and brand behind AI-native infrastructure, including Altruist's February AI tax-services release. Advisors custodying at Altruist gain staying power; those elsewhere gain negotiating leverage. It is also the largest mark yet validating AI-native wealth-infrastructure valuations.
InvestmentNews

AssetMark rolls out Talk Tracks AI portfolio commentary to 11,000 advisors

AssetMark, the $180 billion-platform TAMP, detailed Talk Tracks on August 26 — an AI feature that analyzes each client portfolio's return and risk drivers versus benchmark, scans news for market impacts, and generates a call-ready script. Now opt-in, it will be embedded for all 11,000 advisors by fall; 70% of advisors who tried it came back. The firm says it replaces 20–60 minutes of manual prep and adds tax-alpha commentary on tax-managed accounts.

Why it matters: AI meeting prep is shifting from a standalone vendor purchase (Jump, Zeplyn) to a bundled platform feature — pressuring point-solution pricing and changing TAMP selection criteria. AssetMark's own survey shows the gap this fills: 94% of advisors believe AI cuts prep time but 65% aren't using it consistently. Embedded-by-default is how that gap closes.
Advisor360° (press release)

Advisor360° ships AI reporting agent plus native Schwab and Fidelity workflows

Advisor360°'s August platform release, announced August 26, makes several AI capabilities generally available: a Client Intelligence reporting agent, daily-refreshed Practice Intelligence flags (RMD age, missing beneficiaries, excess cash), and VIDA agents that answer book-level questions and execute actions with advisor confirmation. Its Meeting Prep agent cuts preparation time 60%. The release also pilots native Schwab Advisor Services account opening and adds straight-through trade processing with Fidelity Institutional Wealth Services.

Why it matters: The battle for the advisor desktop is consolidating around custody-agnostic AI operating layers — the same week Vanguard bought a custodian outright. Native Schwab onboarding and Fidelity trade routing inside one platform reduces the switching costs that kept broker-dealers on legacy stacks; home offices running Envestnet or Orion comparisons should re-score connectivity.
AI in Asset Management
Hedgeweek (Goldman Sachs research)

Goldman finds hedge funds and mutual funds split on the AI trade

Goldman Sachs analysis published August 24 — covering 991 hedge funds with $5.4 trillion in gross equity positions and 504 large-cap mutual funds with $4.6 trillion — shows the two groups diverging on AI. Hedge funds added Microsoft and Amazon in Q2 while mutual funds cut both; mutual funds added AMD, Micron, and Sandisk as hedge funds trimmed. Twelve infrastructure names, including CoreWeave, Talen Energy, and Xcel Energy, were bought by both.

Why it matters: The AI trade is fragmenting by layer: consensus buying sits in power and data center infrastructure, while megacap platforms and memory are now contested. For portfolio construction, crowding risk differs sharply by segment — the both-bought infrastructure basket carries the most institutional sponsorship and the most unwind risk if capex expectations reset.
Hedgeweek (FT report)

Systematic funds post worst day in two years as momentum trades unwind

Goldman Sachs told clients that systematic long-short funds fell about 1.4% by early afternoon on Wednesday, August 19 — their sharpest daily loss in more than two years — as its global momentum index moved far outside its normal range. Morgan Stanley's pure momentum index dropped more than 4% on a day the S&P 500 rose, a first in at least five years. Systematic long-short strategies remained up roughly 1.7% for August.

Why it matters: Months of AI-led leadership have loaded momentum factors with correlated AI exposure, so rotations out of the theme now hit quant books harder than index moves suggest. Allocators should stress-test overlap between factor sleeves and dedicated AI exposure; the August 19 session is the cleanest recent scenario for that analysis.
EMXETF / EMQQ Global (press release)

EMQQ Global lists China AI Tigers LLM ETF on Nasdaq as TGRZ

EMXETF, the emerging-markets AI ETF platform from EMQQ Global founder Kevin Carter, launched the China AI Tigers LLM ETF (Nasdaq: TGRZ) on August 26. The fund targets companies in China's large language model ecosystem — the "AI Tigers" cohort around DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, and Z.ai, which listed publicly in January 2026. The non-diversified fund may use swap agreements to reach exposures, and DeepSeek and Moonshot are flagged as potential future IPO candidates.

Why it matters: This is the first US-listed vehicle purpose-built for the China frontier-model theme, giving advisors a ticker for client questions that DeepSeek raised in January. Diligence the structure before allocating: swap-based exposure, single-theme concentration, and China regulatory risk make it a satellite position, and any DeepSeek or Moonshot IPO would reshape the holdings.
