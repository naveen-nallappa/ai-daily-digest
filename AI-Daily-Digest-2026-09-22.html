Major AI Updates
Semafor

AMD tops $1 trillion market value as Meta's Muse agent sparks chip rally

AMD rose about 10% on September 21 to close above $1 trillion in market value for the first time, joining Nvidia, Broadcom and Micron as the fourth US chipmaker at that level. The catalyst was Meta's Muse personal AI agent reaching the top free-app spot on the US iOS App Store; Meta gained about 10.7%, Intel about 12% and Arm about 17%. The Nasdaq Composite closed at a record near 27,122, up 2.26%, its first record close since June.

Why it matters: Consumer agent adoption is now a chip-demand narrative in its own right, separate from hyperscaler capex, and the rally concentrated in AMD, Intel and Arm rather than Nvidia. PMs benchmarked to cap-weighted semiconductor indices should check how much second-source exposure they hold, and advisors fielding calls after a 10% single-day move should frame it as a broadening, not a new leg of the Nvidia trade.
The Texas Tribune

Texas halts all data-center environmental permits until ERCOT and water audits finish

Governor Greg Abbott directed the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality on September 21 to stop issuing permits to data centers until ERCOT and the Texas Water Development Board complete audits covering power use, water use, community impact, ownership and tax breaks. The order extends his August 3 grid-connection moratorium to behind-the-meter projects and to areas outside ERCOT's footprint. As few as 28% of data centers answered the state's water-usage survey, and ERCOT's audit is expected to finish in December.

Why it matters: Texas is the largest US data-center market, and a permit freeze there compounds the $68 billion of projects already blocked or delayed nationally in the second quarter. Infrastructure and private-credit PMs should assume Texas deliveries slip into 2027 at the earliest and favor sponsors with permits already in hand; the November election adds a political overlay that the audits alone will not resolve.
OpenAI

OpenAI asks Washington to lead global frontier-AI standards before Wednesday's UN Security Council session

OpenAI published a proposal on September 21 urging the US to lead international standard-setting through CAISI and the network of national AI safety institutes, with common measurements for recursive self-improvement, human-oversight triggers for automated AI research and shared incident reporting. It says the standards should not become licenses or pre-release approvals. The same day, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC that developers should expect no federal liability shield. Sam Altman and Dario Amodei brief the UN Security Council on September 23.

Why it matters: The labs are converging on measurement standards as the alternative to liability relief, and Bessent's comment closes the door on a shield for now. Compliance teams should expect any US standard to reuse the incident-classification language from the UN panel brief, and PMs holding pre-IPO OpenAI or Anthropic exposure should treat uncapped developer liability as a live valuation input.
Bloomberg

Alibaba unveils Zhenwu V900 chip and targets 20 GW of data centers by 2032

At its Apsara Conference on September 22, Alibaba Cloud introduced the Zhenwu V900 accelerator from its T-Head unit, claiming three times the performance of the M890 released in May and clusters scaling to 500,000 chips. Mass production and commercial release are slated for the first quarter of 2027, and existing Zhenwu chips are deployed with more than 650 customers. Alibaba also set a target of over 20 gigawatts of global cloud data-center capacity by 2032; its shares rose 2.19% on September 21.

Why it matters: Domestic accelerators with named customers and a production date weaken the assumption that China must buy Nvidia H200s, two days before a Trump–Xi summit where export controls are explicitly off the table. PMs with China exposure through cap-weighted ETFs should note that Alibaba, not the PCB and equipment layer, is where the capex is being announced.
AI in Wealth Management
InvestmentNews

Schwab's Anthropic contract bars model training on client data at every Claude tier

Alison Dooher, head of AI for Schwab Advisor Services, told InvestmentNews on September 21 that Schwab's agreement with Anthropic bars retention of any connector input or output for model training, extended to all Claude license tiers rather than only Enterprise. Account numbers are masked and Social Security numbers and birth dates are not passed; the Q4 integration is read-only, with human-in-the-loop workflows such as beneficiary updates a possible later step. Schwab is hiring a director of AI integration standards, and Anthropic's Peter Nolan speaks at IMPACT on October 27–29.

Why it matters: This is the first custodian-negotiated data-use term for a general-purpose model, and it becomes the benchmark RIAs will cite when vendors ask for training rights. Compliance heads should get the equivalent clause in writing from Jump, Zocks and Zeplyn before Q4, and operations teams should plan for a read-only launch, not the agentic account maintenance some vendors already ship.
Financial Planning

LPL explores direct Claude access for advisors as BNY and Raymond James answer Schwab

LPL chief product and technology officer Greg Gates told Financial Planning on September 21 that the firm is exploring enabling direct advisor use of Anthropic's Claude, and that its Cyan agent already uses many of the same Anthropic MCP integrations with added compliance controls. BNY's Adam Vos said the custodian is bringing its AI capabilities into advisor platforms across AI environments, while Raymond James cited $1.1 billion in annual technology spend and an expanded AWS relationship. Fidelity declined to comment.

Why it matters: A week after Future Proof, every major custodian and the largest independent broker-dealer has positioned against the Schwab deal, and LPL's answer is to expose the same Anthropic connectors through its own supervised agent rather than a third-party app. Advisors weighing affiliation models should ask whether they get Claude directly or only through the firm's wrapper, since the controls and data terms differ.
Evergreen.ai (Business Wire)

Bill Harris launches free AI advice app Evergreen.ai with no fees through January 2028

Bill Harris, former CEO of PayPal, Intuit and Personal Capital, launched Evergreen.ai on September 21, a self-directed AI financial-advice app covering financial, retirement, investment and tax strategies with no account minimum and free access through January 1, 2028 for beta registrants. The app pairs large language models for conversation with deterministic calculation engines, links accounts through Plaid and does not use client data to train public models. Evergreen Wealth, the RIA behind it, launched in September 2025 and held about $112 million in assets at December 31, 2025.

Why it matters: Harris built the last two mass-affluent digital advice businesses, and a free, RIA-registered AI planner aimed at the 73% of adults without an advisor is the clearest test yet of whether deterministic engines close the accuracy gap the Saturn benchmark exposed. Advisors should expect prospects to arrive with Evergreen plans, and firms weighing their own client-facing assistants now have a free comparison.
AI in Asset Management
Grayscale (GlobeNewswire)

Grayscale converts its Bitcoin Miners ETF into the AI Compute ETF under ticker GCPU

Grayscale announced on September 22 that its Bitcoin Miners ETF, formerly MNRS, is now the Grayscale AI Compute ETF, tracking the renamed Indxx High Performance Computing Index. Roughly half the portfolio targets companies built around GPU cloud and AI hosting, with the remainder in high-performance-computing names, including Bitcoin miners, that have disclosed or executed a shift of power and capacity to AI workloads. The fund rebalances quarterly and holds no digital assets directly.

Why it matters: A crypto-native issuer repurposing a miners fund into AI infrastructure marks the trade migrating from chips to power and floor space, the same layer Texas just froze. PMs should read the holdings before treating GCPU as an AI proxy, since converted miners keep crypto-price sensitivity, and advisors should flag the strategy change to clients who bought MNRS for bitcoin exposure.
SEC Form 8-A (iShares Trust)

BlackRock registers the iShares Future AI Beneficiaries ETF for NYSE Arca listing

iShares Trust filed a Form 8-A on September 21 registering the iShares Future AI Beneficiaries ETF for listing on NYSE Arca, incorporating the prospectus filed on September 14. The fund tracks an index of US companies expected to benefit from AI adoption, primarily through cost savings and secondarily through revenue gains, and is expected to trade as AIBF with a 0.40% expense ratio. It is distinct from BlackRock's existing ARTY Future AI and Tech and BAI AI Innovation and Tech Active funds.

Why it matters: BlackRock is packaging the "AI adopter" thesis, companies whose margins expand from using AI rather than selling it, as the diversification answer to the concentration worry NYC's pension raised this week. Model-portfolio teams should check overlap with the Magnificent Seven before treating it as a hedge, since the index methodology, not the label, decides whether it avoids the semis.
DeFi Technologies (PR Newswire)

DeFi Technologies' Valour launches an AI-run crypto hedge fund managed by Neuronomics

Valour, the DeFi Technologies subsidiary that runs more than 100 digital-asset ETPs, launched Valour Funds SPC on September 21 with its first hedge fund, Smart Crypto Fund SP, a Cayman vehicle for professional and qualified investors. Zurich-based Neuronomics AG, which is FINMA-authorised and partly owned by DeFi Technologies, sets allocations and total market exposure from proprietary AI models over price, volume and other market data, cutting positions when signals deteriorate within predefined risk budgets and concentration limits.

Why it matters: This is a model-driven exposure dial rather than a fixed basket, which is what allocators asked for after July's AI and crypto unwind. Due-diligence teams should press for live track record versus backtest, since the NBER study reported this week found AI hedge funds' edge faded after 2017, and should confirm how the execution algorithms behave when digital-asset liquidity thins.
