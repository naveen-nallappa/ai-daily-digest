FERC Orders Six Grid Operators to Speed AI Data-Center Hookups

On June 18 the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission issued Section 206 "show cause" orders to all six US regional grid operators outside Texas, requiring each to defend or reform its interconnection rules so large-load customers — explicitly AI data centers — can connect faster while preserving reliability and cost control. The orders bypass the multi-year notice-and-rulemaking process entirely. FERC Chair Laura Swett called AI grid integration a "national priority," fulfilling a 2025 Department of Energy request to accelerate hookups for hyperscale compute.

Why it matters: Grid access, not chips, is becoming the binding constraint on the AI buildout, and federal pressure to clear queues directly supports the utilities, power, and data-center names underpinning AI infrastructure theses. PMs holding regulated-utility or independent-power exposure should track which regional operators must refile and how cost allocation between data centers and ratepayers gets resolved.

SpaceX Files $60 Billion All-Stock Deal to Acquire Cursor Maker

SpaceX disclosed in a June 16 8-K that it agreed to acquire Anysphere, the company behind AI code editor Cursor, in an all-stock deal valuing Cursor at $60 billion and expected to close in the third quarter pending regulatory approval. Cursor runs roughly $4 billion in annualized revenue, $2.6 billion from enterprise accounts. The purchase exercises an option SpaceX took in April and pairs Cursor with a joint coding model trained on xAI's Colossus, slated to ship inside Cursor and a new product, Grok Build.

Why it matters: A newly public SpaceX folding a top coding tool into the xAI stack escalates consolidation among the AI mega-caps now branded "MANGOS," which sit in client portfolios via thematic ETFs. Analysts covering AI tooling should reassess Cursor's independence and pricing, while PMs weigh how vertical integration across Musk's entities concentrates platform and key-person risk.
Council on Foreign Relations

DeepSeek Ships 1.6-Trillion-Parameter V4 Trained on Huawei Chips

DeepSeek released a preview of its V4 series this week, headlined by DeepSeek-V4-Pro, a 1.6-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model available through its website, apps, and API. The notable detail is hardware: V4 was trained on Huawei Ascend 950 chips rather than Nvidia, the first major frontier-adjacent Chinese model to do so publicly. The Council on Foreign Relations judges V4 the strongest open-source option available but still behind US closed frontier models — a gap DeepSeek's own technical paper concedes.

Why it matters: Validating Huawei silicon as a training substrate weakens the assumption that US export controls keep Chinese labs perpetually behind, a core input to the Nvidia-and-suppliers AI trade. PMs with semiconductor exposure should treat Ascend's viability as a structural risk to the US chip moat, not a one-off, and watch for follow-on Chinese open-weight releases.

xAI's Grok 4.3 Goes Generally Available on Amazon Bedrock

xAI's Grok 4.3 is now generally available on Amazon Bedrock under model ID xai.grok-4.3, priced at $1.25 per million input tokens and $2.50 per million output tokens. The model carries a one-million-token context window, configurable reasoning effort levels, and xAI's claim of the lowest hallucination rate among current frontier models. The Bedrock listing lets enterprise AWS teams call Grok through existing accounts and governance without separate xAI contracts, extending xAI's push into managed enterprise distribution alongside its Grok Build marketplace.

Why it matters: Native Bedrock availability lowers the switching cost for AWS-based firms to add a third frontier provider, reinforcing that multi-model architecture is now standard rather than optional. Technology teams at advisory and asset-management firms should price Grok into vendor comparisons on cost and hallucination claims, and verify any low-hallucination assertion against their own evaluations before routing client-facing work to it.

Salesforce Launches Agentic Advisor to Defend Its CRM Turf

Salesforce unveiled Agentic Advisor on June 18, a suite of six AI capabilities built into Agentforce for Financial Services. Its Meeting Concierge, generally available now, generates pre-meeting briefings from client history, captures live conversation, and drafts summaries and follow-up tasks for advisor approval. Two further modules, Run My Day and an agentic Client Details page, are slated for July or August. The launch is an explicit defense against AI note-takers such as Jump and Zocks that have been peeling meeting workflows away from the CRM.

Why it matters: The incumbent CRM moving meeting intelligence in-house forces advisors to choose between a unified Salesforce stack and best-of-breed note-takers they may already run. Teams should compare summary accuracy and record-update reliability head-to-head before consolidating, and confirm that auto-assigned tasks and drafted notes still pass through advisor review to preserve supervision and data-governance standards.

JPMorgan Readies AI Agents That Run for Hours in Private Banking

JPMorgan will deploy upgraded AI agents later this year that operate autonomously for an hour or two at a stretch, up from two to three minutes, chief analytics officer Derek Waldron said on June 9. In private banking, the agents analyze market activity, client positions, and research overnight so bankers can focus on client conversations. JPMorgan attributes a 20% increase in private-banking gross sales to its AI tools and expects individual bankers to eventually expand coverage by as much as 50%.

Why it matters: A concrete 20% sales lift from a top private bank gives competing wealth firms a benchmark to test their own AI investments against, not just a vision statement. Advisory leaders should probe whether longer-running agents clear the security and governance bars that stalled earlier rollouts, and how overnight, agent-prepared analysis is documented when it informs advice that reaches a client.

Schwab Pegs 2026 Growth to AI and Readies Client 'Portfolio Insights'

At its May 14 investor day, Charles Schwab named AI its single biggest growth lever and raised full-year 2026 revenue-growth guidance to 14–15%, up from 9.5–10.5% in January. The custodian is rolling out "portfolio insights," an AI client experience delivering tailored analysis on holdings — performance versus benchmarks, news affecting positions, and Schwab Center research — beginning in June. Management framed AI as a way to extend personalized service to client segments that lack dedicated relationship managers.

Why it matters: When the largest RIA custodian ties a steep guidance raise to AI and ships analytics straight to end clients, advisors on Schwab's platform face client-facing tools they do not control shaping how holdings are interpreted. Advisors should preview what portfolio insights surfaces, anticipate the questions it will prompt, and align their own commentary so automated and advisor narratives do not conflict.
