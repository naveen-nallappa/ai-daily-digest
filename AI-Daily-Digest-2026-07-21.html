CuspAI raises $450 million at a $2.6 billion valuation to redesign chip materials

Alibaba previews Qwen3.8-Max, a 2.4-trillion-parameter model, at Shanghai's World AI Conference

Alibaba previewed Qwen3.8-Max on July 19 at the World AI Conference in Shanghai, describing a 2.4-trillion-parameter sparse mixture-of-experts model with a one-million-token context window and multimodal input across text, images, video and documents. The company said it ranks second only to Anthropic's Fable 5 among the systems it benchmarked, but published no model card, benchmark table or independent evaluation. Why it matters: Chinese labs are now shipping frontier-scale models on a two-day cadence, and unverified benchmark claims are becoming the norm. Firms running vendor evaluations should discount self-reported rankings that lack a model card, and test on their own retrieval and compliance workloads instead. Absent open weights and a named license, Qwen3.8-Max is not yet procurable for regulated deployment.
European Commission

EU orders Google to open Android to rival AI assistants by August 2027

The European Commission adopted two binding specification decisions on July 16 setting out how Google must comply with the Digital Markets Act. By August 1, 2027, Android phones in the EU must give rival AI assistants the same eleven system-level feature groups Gemini holds exclusively, including voice activation and cross-app actions. Separately, Google must share anonymised Search ranking, query, click and view data with rival engines, with pricing finalised and access beginning by January 2027.

Why it matters: Assistant distribution has been the moat behind Gemini's European consumer position, and a 2027 deadline puts a date on when that erodes. PMs modelling Alphabet should treat EU search-data licensing as a disclosed revenue-and-risk line rather than a footnote. Enterprises building on Gemini should confirm contractual continuity for EU users through the transition.
The White House

White House to announce voluntary frontier-model security review with labs before August 1

A voluntary framework is being finalised with OpenAI, Anthropic and Google under the June 2 executive order on advanced AI innovation and security, with an announcement expected before August 1. It lets developers give federal agencies up to 30 days of pre-release access to covered frontier models for cybersecurity and national security testing, and directs the NSA to build a classified system identifying which models qualify. The order explicitly bans mandatory licensing or pre-clearance. Meta is not in the initial deal.

Why it matters: A 30-day government window sits between a lab's internal sign-off and general availability, which shifts release timing for anyone whose roadmap depends on day-one model access. Technology teams planning fourth-quarter deployments should build schedule slack for covered models. Note too that a voluntary structure means participation, not compliance, is what a vendor can actually attest to.

VanEck Media Release

VanEck lists Australia's first AI-selected equity ETF on the ASX on July 20

The VanEck Dynamic International Equity ETF began trading on the ASX under the ticker GOAT on July 20, tracking the Akros Enhanced World ex Australia Index. Each month the model scores roughly 1,200 of the largest developed-market companies outside Australia across more than 10,000 fundamental, technical and macroeconomic signals, then holds the 150 with the highest assessed probability of outperformance. Akros, a Seoul-based index specialist, underpins about US$10 billion of index assets across 75 ETFs globally.

Why it matters: A monthly-rebalanced model that retires its own signals behaves more like an active mandate than an index, and simulated history back to 2005 is not a live track record. Allocators screening AI-driven products should demand out-of-sample performance since inception and turnover figures before granting model-portfolio slots. Ask specifically how signal retirement is governed and who reviews it.
