Nvidia cuts its OpenAI Ohio data-center guarantee to under $120 billion

Nvidia is now expected to guarantee less than $120 billion of financing for OpenAI's Pike County, Ohio campus, down from the roughly $250 billion discussed in late July, the Wall Street Journal reported August 14. The revised backstop would cover only the first phase of the 10-gigawatt project, about 800 megawatts due by 2028. Investors had pressed Nvidia on its risk exposure to outsized financing commitments. A separate arrangement covering as much as $350 billion of AI chips for the site remains under discussion.

Why it matters: The circular-financing structure that unsettled AI holders in July is being dismantled by the vendor itself, one phase at a time. PMs marking AI-infrastructure credit should note the guarantee now stands behind 800 megawatts rather than 10 gigawatts, which changes recovery assumptions on data-center paper underwritten to the full campus.

The Decoder

Alibaba released Qwen3.8-27B open weights that run on one consumer GPU

Alibaba's Qwen team published the Qwen3.8-27B checkpoint under an Apache 2.0 licence on August 14. The 27.8-billion-parameter multimodal model accepts text, images and video, ships with a native 262,144-token context window extensible to one million, and is sized to run on roughly 24GB of VRAM, the memory found on a high-end consumer graphics card. Qwen says it outperforms the larger proprietary Qwen3.7-Plus on coding and office tasks. The release marks a shift away from keeping flagship models behind proprietary walls.

Why it matters: Frontier-adjacent capability that runs on a single workstation changes the build-versus-buy calculus for firms with data-residency constraints. Compliance and technology teams evaluating advisor AI vendors should now ask whether a tool requires an external API at all, because self-hosting inside the firm's own perimeter has become a realistic answer rather than a capability concession.

DeepVest

DeepVest launched MonitorLab to run plain-English market alerts across 50 signals

DeepVest announced MonitorLab on August 11, a monitoring layer that lets advisors write market alerts in plain English rather than configure dashboards. The system continuously evaluates more than 50 technical, quantitative and market signals spanning securities, portfolios, earnings events, regulatory filings and macroeconomic data. Advisors can set triggers for indicator crossovers, drift from target allocation, benchmark-relative performance or portfolio risk thresholds. MonitorLab completes a four-part stack alongside AgentLab for research, DataLab for market data and AdvisorLab for client proposals.

Why it matters: Drift and risk-threshold monitoring is the piece most RIAs still handle on a quarterly review cycle, which is where supervision gaps surface in examinations. Firms evaluating this category should ask whether alerts are deterministic and logged, because an auditable trigger record is what converts monitoring into a defensible supervisory control.
