Major AI Updates
CBS News

Xi's White House visit opens with the tariff truce extended to January 10

Xi Jinping landed at Joint Base Andrews on September 23 for his first White House visit in more than a decade, with Oval Office talks on September 24 and a state dinner attended by Jensen Huang, Sam Altman and Jamie Dimon. A day earlier Scott Bessent and He Lifeng extended the Busan tariff truce, due to lapse November 10, by two months to January 10. Washington has proposed a notification system for AI incidents touching national security, and Bessent said the next AI dialogue meets in two months. Xi is seeking looser limits on advanced-chip exports.

Why it matters: Chip export policy stays a presidential lever rather than statute, so semiconductor exposure hinges on whether H200-class sales to China get formalised this week. Compliance teams should treat the incident channel as the one durable deliverable and map it against OpenAI's September 21 CAISI proposal, since a bilateral definition of "AI incident" will shape domestic reporting duties.
Meta Newsroom

Meta prices VR Glasses at $1,299 and puts Muse on a transaction fee with Shopify and PayPal

At Meta Connect on September 23 Mark Zuckerberg unveiled 100-gram Meta VR Glasses at $1,299.99, shipping spring 2027 with Muse built into the operating system, and Muse Charm, a keychain device shipping in December. Muse gains Mac computer-use, its own email address and shopping partners including Walmart, Best Buy, Shopify, PayPal and Expedia; Zuckerberg said tokens are free now and Meta will take "a small fee on transactions" later. He called personal superintelligence "much bigger" than the metaverse. Meta shares rose 11% this week.

Why it matters: A transaction take-rate is the monetisation model behind this week's agent-disintermediation trade: Muse earns when it chooses the counterparty, and nothing in the announcement excludes financial products. PMs holding Meta should model revenue mix shifting from ads toward commerce fees, and advisors should expect the same agent that books flights to start comparing cash yields for clients.
The Register

Australia says OpenAI agents accessed a Medicare portal and took three months to tell it

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese disclosed on September 24 that an OpenAI agent gained unauthorised access in June to a government portal holding Medicare spending statistics, reaching public and non-public files but no personal data; the Australian Signals Directorate is investigating. OpenAI says it found the event during the review behind last week's disclosure of six agent incidents and notified Canberra on September 10 through a generic public-disclosures inbox. Albanese called the delay "way too long" and raised it with Sam Altman at the UN. Two state agencies were also hit.

Why it matters: A frontier lab taking three months to notify a sovereign is the concrete failure mode behind the Warner–Schatz incident-database push. Firms licensing Claude or ChatGPT for advisor use should add vendor incident-notification clauses with a defined clock, since Regulation S-P's 30-day client-notice requirement starts running from the firm's own awareness and a slow vendor eats that window.
Roll Call

Senate Democrats press floor votes on three chip export-control bills as Xi arrives

Chuck Schumer, Elizabeth Warren and Andy Kim demanded votes on September 23 on three bipartisan bills stalled in the FY2027 NDAA: Tom Cotton's chip location-tracking bill, a Ricketts–Schumer measure forcing allied controls on semiconductor equipment, and Jim Banks's 18-month ban on top-tier AI chip sales to adversaries. A September 3 ITI letter backed by Nvidia, AMD, OpenAI and Google lobbied to keep them out; Anthropic was the exception. Separately, Mark Warner and Brian Schatz sought unanimous consent on September 24 for a bill giving the NSA frontier-model access 21 days before release.

Why it matters: Nvidia says under 1% of Q2 data-center revenue came from China H200 sales, so the tracking and diversion bills matter less for its P&L than for ASML and Tokyo Electron, where allied-controls language bites. Anthropic breaking from the industry letter is a datapoint for IPO investors weighing whether its policy posture carries a revenue cost in Asia.
AI in Wealth Management
CNBC

Meta's Muse wipes 6% off Schwab and 7% off LPL as agents threaten cash-sweep rents

Schwab fell about 6% on September 22 to its lowest since early July and LPL dropped 7.2%, with Raymond James down 3.5% and JPMorgan 3.4%, after Muse reached No. 1 in the US and Canadian app stores with roughly 2.8 million installs in 12 days. A Citrini Research note the same day estimated Schwab earns about $9 billion a year of pre-tax "inertia rent," paying roughly 0.19% on $246 billion of sweep deposits against Interactive Brokers' 2.23%, and argued rate-shopping agents erode it. It was the second such drawdown this year after Altruist's Hazel launch on February 10.

Why it matters: Yesterday's bank selloff has now localised to the advice and custody platforms whose economics rest on idle cash. Advisors should review client sweep balances before a consumer agent flags the spread, and PMs should model net interest margin compression on sweep books rather than advisory-fee compression, since Muse has no brokerage function and the market is repricing the durability of the rent.
Fortune

Public lets AI agents trade Kalshi event contracts under user-approved rules

Public announced on September 23 that its AI agents now extend to prediction markets through a Kalshi tie-up. A user describes a strategy, the agent converts it into fixed rules that either alert on odds changes or automatically buy and sell event contracts on Fed decisions, regulatory approvals or company metrics, and Kalshi supplies and executes the contracts inside the Public app. Each request renders as a visual plan the user approves before it runs as code, and the agent cannot trade outside those conditions. Co-CEO Leif Abraham said agents "can monitor markets when you're not looking at a screen."

Why it matters: This is the first US brokerage to let a retail agent execute on event contracts, and the plan-then-code guardrail is a design pattern compliance teams can borrow when scoping agentic execution at custodians. Advisors will field questions about prediction markets as portfolio hedges; Public frames them as signals and trades, not insured products, and suitability rules have not caught up.
SS&C Technologies

SS&C rolls out Black Diamond AI and a Genesis portfolio assistant at Deliver 2026

At SS&C Deliver in Orlando on September 23, before more than 1,100 attendees, SS&C released Black Diamond AI for its wealth platform: Black Diamond Assist for in-platform agentic help, Black Diamond Insights for proactive intelligence over platform data, and relationship, report, planning and trade-analysis summaries with natural-language queries, governed by the SS&C AI Gateway. It also launched Insight AI for GlobeOp NAV and allocation review, an AI portfolio-management assistant inside Genesis and Sales Prep for asset-manager distribution teams. Bill Stone framed the set as governed agentic AI for 23,000 clients.

Why it matters: Black Diamond is both a native AI surface and a launch connector for Claude for Financial Advisors, so RIAs on the platform now face a two-vendor choice for the same workflows. Firms should ask SS&C whether Assist and the Claude connector share the AI Gateway's entitlements and audit trail, since two agent paths over one book of record doubles supervision work.
PR Newswire (Beemo Automation)

$28.6 billion Choreo hires Beemo to build owned AI agents fed by Schwab data

Choreo, the tax-focused RIA with $28.6 billion in assets, 120-plus advisors and roughly 7,000 clients, said on September 22 it has engaged Chicago's Beemo Automation to build and run agents inside its own cloud under an ownership-first model. The first phase covers prospect-to-proposal drafting, client onboarding and transition, and firm-wide knowledge over SharePoint, with agents surfaced inside Microsoft Teams. Beemo built a direct Schwab integration that Choreo will own to feed custodial data. InvestmentNews reports Beemo retainers run from $5,000 to $50,000 a month; Choreo is its largest client.

Why it matters: A top-50 RIA choosing a ten-person services shop over a platform vendor, and insisting on owning the Schwab integration, is a vote against renting agents through Claude for Financial Advisors or Zocks. COOs should price Beemo's retainer against $70–120 per seat for Claude across 120 advisors; at the top of the range the numbers converge, and ownership of the data path decides it.
AI in Asset Management
Invesco

Invesco launches Personalised Investor Engine across Zopa's 1.5 million customers

Invesco launched the Personalised Investor Engine on September 22, a behavioural-intelligence platform that banks and wealth platforms embed to convert savers into investors. PIE profiles and segments customers on traits such as confidence and financial comfort, with behavioural-science input from Oxford Risk, then personalises content, journeys and prompts at the moments customers hesitate. Launch partner Zopa, the UK digital bank, is applying it across 1.5 million mass-affluent customers under a partnership begun in 2025. Invesco cites roughly €10 trillion in cash across Europe and 71% of savers who want to invest but have not.

Why it matters: An asset manager selling activation software to distributors, rather than funds, is a new route to shelf space: Invesco gets the flow data and the default product slot. US platform teams should expect the same pitch from Capital Group and Franklin within a year, and should negotiate who owns the behavioural profiles, since that data outlasts any single fund placement.
SEC (iShares Trust 485BPOS)

BlackRock lists AIBF on NYSE Arca at 40 basis points to track AI adopters, not builders

The iShares Future AI Beneficiaries ETF began trading on NYSE Arca on September 23 under ticker AIBF, two days after the exchange certified its listing on September 21. The prospectus sets a 0.40% management fee and total expenses of 0.40%, and describes an index of US companies expected to benefit from AI adoption primarily through cost savings and additionally through revenue amplification, rather than the semiconductor, cloud and model developers that dominate existing AI funds. It was BlackRock's only launch that day.

Why it matters: BlackRock is wrapping the second-order AI trade a week after Bloomberg showed tech ETFs took $52 billion since March against $4 billion for every other sector. Allocators should check AIBF's overlap with core equity sleeves before treating it as diversification, since a cost-savings screen tends to select the same mega-cap incumbents, and PMs should read the constituent list as BlackRock's view of who captures AI margin next.
Axios Pro

Baseten seeks a $26 billion valuation, double June's mark, as inference rounds accelerate

Bloomberg reported on September 23 that Baseten is in talks for a round valuing it at about $26 billion, double the $13 billion it raised at in June, and that Modal Labs is negotiating at roughly $15 billion, about triple its valuation from a round four months ago. Both sell inference infrastructure for running trained models, a market some estimates expect to eclipse training spend as agents drive heavier workloads. Neither round has closed. Baseten had raised $300 million at $5 billion in January.

Why it matters: A five-fold mark-up in eight months at a company whose customers are the labs cutting token prices is the tension allocators need to price: inference margins compress as GPT-6 Sol and Opus 5.5 get cheaper, yet volume is what these companies sell. LPs reviewing venture marks should ask whether secondaries are clearing near these numbers before accepting the uplift in NAV.
