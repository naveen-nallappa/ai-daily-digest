Major AI Updates
CBS News

Trump creates a “Super Intelligence Force” led by intelligence chief Jay Clayton

President Trump announced the Super Intelligence Force on October 4, a day after reports named Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton as AI czar. Clayton chairs it, with FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson, Pentagon technology chief Emil Michael and OPM Director Scott Kupor as vice chairs, all reporting to Trump and Chief of Staff Susie Wiles. The Wall Street Journal reported it will deliver a report on AI risks and opportunities within 120 days. No budget was disclosed.

Why it matters: A former SEC chairman now leads federal AI policy with the FTC chair at his side, so expect disclosure and consumer-protection framing rather than new licensing. Compliance teams should track the 120-day report, due around early February, before locking 2027 AI governance budgets, and watch whether agent liability lands on operators or developers.
The Atlantic

OpenAI safety leader David Robinson resigns, calling the company’s culture broken

David Robinson, who spent 3.5 years at OpenAI and led the safety reports accompanying its major launches, resigned and set out his reasons in The Atlantic on October 3. He argued that OpenAI’s trial-and-error deployment guarantees periodic failures at a growing scale, citing its agents’ breach of Hugging Face systems, and said labs need nuclear- or airport-grade safeguards. Spokesperson Drew Pusateri said OpenAI pauses training when needed to keep models within what it can safely manage.

Why it matters: An insider departure adds weight to the agent-safety record regulators are already building ahead of OpenAI’s expected IPO. Firms deploying OpenAI agents should ask for incident histories and pause criteria in vendor reviews. PMs pricing pre-IPO AI exposure should treat safety governance as a valuation and headline risk, not a footnote.
Horizon3.ai

Server flaw found by Anthropic’s Mythos was exploited within about a day of disclosure

Horizon3’s Zach Hanley used Anthropic’s Mythos to find CVE-2026-61500, a critical authentication bypass allowing remote code execution in Rejetto HTTP File Server, fixed in version 3.2.1. Mythos worked out that session keys came from a reversible random-number generator and used Microsoft’s Z3 solver to recover the seed. The Register reported on October 3 that attacks began from a China-hosted IP the evening after disclosure. Project Glasswing has produced 286 CVEs; this is the second exploited in the wild.

Why it matters: AI-found bugs are now weaponized within a day, shrinking the patch window that vendor-risk programs assume. Wealth and asset managers should ask critical vendors for patch timelines measured in hours for internet-facing systems, and check that file-sharing and legacy servers holding client data appear in asset inventories, since attackers hit these first.
Bloomberg

AI deals push Hong Kong equity fundraising to a record $47.5 billion summer

IPOs, placements and block trades in Hong Kong raised $47.5 billion over the summer, the most on record for the period, Bloomberg reported on October 4. Year-to-date proceeds exceed $92 billion, near 2021’s $112.5 billion record. Alibaba’s $10.2 billion follow-on was the largest deal, Zhongji Innolight raised nearly $8 billion, and Z.AI has raised $9.6 billion this year. Only two of the ten largest deals since July trade above their offer prices.

Why it matters: Chinese AI companies are returning to market within months of listing, adding supply that has left most recent deals under water. PMs should weigh dilution risk before buying Hong Kong AI new issues, and advisors with clients in China or Asia technology funds should check how much of each fund sits in this year’s listings.
AI in Wealth Management
RIABiz

Pontera pitches itself as a trust layer for AI agents amid Fidelity’s 401(k) ban

Pontera, which lets advisors manage clients’ held-away 401(k) accounts, is recasting itself as a “trust layer” for AI agents, RIABiz reported on October 1. Policy director Ben White argued that Pontera can validate users, scope permissions, minimize data sharing and keep activity records. Fidelity bars Pontera from its plans over credential-sharing risk. An OvationCXM survey of 520 financial firms cited in the piece found 75% using or trialing agents and 68% expecting agents to execute transactions.

Why it matters: As Schwab rolls out Charley, the fight over who may act in a client’s account is shifting from advisor logins to AI agents. Advisors managing held-away 401(k) assets should document how they access those accounts today, and compliance teams should set rules on agent permissions before recordkeepers decide the terms for them.
FUTR (press release)

USA Financial gives its 225-plus advisor offices FUTR’s AI advisor agent for prospecting

The FUTR Corporation said on September 30 that USA Financial, a network of more than 225 independent advisor offices, has made FUTR Planning and a dedicated FUTR Advisor Agent available for lead generation and financial planning. More than 400 paying advisors already use FUTR Planning, which has produced about 1 million plans since 2016. FUTR presented at USA Financial’s advisor conference from September 30 to October 2; prospect digital vaults with FutureVault follow in the fourth quarter.

Why it matters: Mid-sized independent networks are packaging AI prospecting tools as a member benefit rather than leaving advisors to buy their own. Advisors offered such tools should check who owns prospect data and plans, how leads are routed among offices, and whether automated outreach passes advertising-rule review before the fourth-quarter vault launch.
WealthAi (FF News)

WealthAi launches agents that run wealth client onboarding and KYC end to end

London-based WealthAi launched AI agents on October 2 that run the full onboarding and know-your-customer process. The agents gather client information and documents, flag missing data, generate forms, and trigger identity, politically-exposed-person, sanctions and account checks, bringing in staff only for judgment calls and sign-off. They work with WealthAi’s Client File and firms’ existing systems and handle multi-jurisdiction clients. Chief executive Jason Nabi called onboarding “absurdly manual.” No customers were named.

Why it matters: Onboarding is where agents can cut time to a funded account without touching investment advice. Operations leads should benchmark current onboarding cycle times before trialing such tools, and require that every automated screening decision is logged and reviewable, because KYC failures carry regulatory penalties whether a person or an agent missed them.
AI in Asset Management
Bloomberg

Blackstone leads $18 billion junior tranche of Broadcom’s $60 billion Anthropic chip financing

Broadcom has begun assembling $60 billion of debt to fund AI chips and infrastructure for Anthropic and other customers, Bloomberg reported on October 2. Banks are preparing to syndicate a $42 billion senior-secured Class A tranche. Blackstone leads the $18 billion Class B junior tranche, committing $9 billion of its own capital and planning to syndicate the remaining $9 billion. Anthropic is expected to become Broadcom’s largest customer in 2027.

Why it matters: A junior chip tranche of this size will flow into private-credit vehicles sold to wealth clients. Allocators should ask Blackstone and peers how much Class B exposure lands in evergreen credit funds and how recoveries are modeled if chip values fall. Credit PMs should price Anthropic customer concentration, not just Broadcom’s rating.
Bloomberg (PR Newswire)

Bloomberg closes Canoe Intelligence deal, feeding AI-extracted private-fund data into PORT

Bloomberg completed its acquisition of Canoe Intelligence on October 1, after agreeing the deal in July; terms were not disclosed. Canoe uses AI to turn unstructured private-fund documents into structured, portfolio-ready data. Joint clients can now feed Canoe’s validated data directly into Bloomberg PORT Enterprise for a combined public and private portfolio view. Bloomberg plans to extend fund discovery, benchmarking, FIGI identifiers and access through ASKB, its conversational AI interface.

Why it matters: Private-markets reporting is moving inside the terminal many PMs already use for public risk. Firms using Canoe should confirm contract and data-access terms under Bloomberg ownership. Those comparing total-portfolio tools from Addepar, Arch or BlackRock’s Aladdin should add Bloomberg PORT with Canoe to the shortlist and test it on their capital-call data.
ETF.com

ETF investors take AI chip profits as 2026 inflows beat 2025’s record

US-listed ETFs took in $150.6 billion in September, lifting year-to-date inflows to $1.54 trillion, above 2025’s full-year record of $1.49 trillion, ETF.com’s Sumit Roy reported on October 1. Fixed-income funds led with $42.4 billion as the 10-year Treasury yield topped 5.25%. AI-linked funds saw profit-taking: the Roundhill Memory ETF (DRAM) and the leveraged semiconductor fund SOXL each lost about $1.6 billion, and iShares MSCI South Korea (EWY) about $1.7 billion.

Why it matters: Money is rotating out of the most crowded AI hardware trades into Treasuries and broad bond funds as yields top 5%. Advisors should rebalance clients whose memory, semiconductor or Korea positions have drifted above target, and check leveraged chip ETF holdings in self-directed accounts, where daily-reset losses compound quickly in a reversal.
