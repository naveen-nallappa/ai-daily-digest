Major AI Updates
Alibaba (SEC Form 6-K)

Alibaba raises $10.2 billion in Hong Kong's largest-ever follow-on to fund AI

Alibaba announced on August 23 a HK$80 billion placement, roughly $10.2 billion, with 100% of net proceeds earmarked for full-stack AI spending across chips, infrastructure and model development. It is the largest primary follow-on ever by a Hong Kong-listed company and the third largest globally in 2026 behind Alphabet and Intel. The deal was upsized after oversubscription, with sovereign wealth funds among buyers. Alibaba shares fell on the dilution. The company has spent nearly half its three-year capex plan.

Why it matters: Equity, not just debt, is now funding AI capex at scale, and existing holders absorb the dilution. PMs with emerging-market or China tech sleeves should reprice per-share expectations rather than headline revenue growth, and expect more hyperscalers to test equity markets once bond desks reach issuer concentration limits on AI paper.
nVent Electric (company release)

nVent agrees to buy Maverick Power for $1.75 billion to reach AI data centers

nVent Electric said on August 24 it will acquire Texas-based Maverick Power for $1.75 billion, plus up to $550 million more tied to 2027 and 2028 performance targets. Maverick builds engineered power distribution equipment for data centers, employs roughly 900 people across Texas and Arizona, and expects about $700 million of 2026 revenue. The price is roughly 11.5 times anticipated 2026 adjusted EBITDA. nVent will fund the deal with cash and new debt, with Bank of America providing bridge financing.

Why it matters: The AI trade keeps migrating away from semiconductors into switchgear, transformers and power distribution, where multiples are still mid-cycle. Analysts running AI-exposure screens should extend coverage past chips and hyperscalers into electrical equipment, and treat this multiple as a comparable for the next round of data-center infrastructure deals.
The Information

Nvidia weighs a Perplexity investment at a valuation above $30 billion

The Information reported on August 23 that Nvidia is discussing joining an equity round valuing Perplexity above $30 billion, more than 50% above the $20 billion mark set a year ago. Perplexity's annualized revenue has climbed past $750 million from under $250 million at the start of 2026, helped by Perplexity Computer, a cloud agent that automates professional desktop tasks. Nvidia already sits on the cap table alongside Jeff Bezos, SoftBank, IVP and NEA. Chief executive Aravind Srinivas is weighing a 2028 listing.

Why it matters: Nvidia is simultaneously supplier, lender and shareholder to a widening set of AI companies, which makes its reported demand harder to read as independent. PMs holding Nvidia should track the vendor-financed share of the customer base, and discount application-layer valuations that rest partly on chip-vendor capital rather than end-market pull.
Bloomberg

Hugging Face explores a sale that could value it above $13 billion

Hugging Face has hired a bank to gauge bidder interest in a sale that could value it at $13 billion or more, Business Insider reported on August 23. The New York company hosts open-source models and datasets used across the AI developer community and was last valued at $4.5 billion in a 2023 round backed by Salesforce, Google and Nvidia. Talks are early and no bidder has been named. A sale would put neutral model-distribution infrastructure under a single strategic owner.

Why it matters: Open-weight model distribution is consolidating alongside compute, which narrows the assumption that firms can freely swap model providers. Technology committees building vendor-independence into AI policies should confirm whether their fallback models depend on a single hosting platform, and revisit contingency plans if a hyperscaler ends up owning it.

Salesforce (company blog)

Salesforce ships Run My Day to advisors as incumbents crowd AI notetakers

InvestmentNews reported on August 18 that platform incumbents are moving onto ground held by standalone AI startups. Salesforce's Agentic Advisor, announced June 18 inside Agentforce for Financial Services, brought Meeting Concierge to general availability in June, followed by Run My Day and an enhanced client details page in July and August. RightCapital separately launched Iris, which scans client data for gaps and planning issues. The squeeze falls hardest on AI notetakers Jump and Zocks, which created the category before incumbents responded.

Why it matters: Functionality advisors currently buy separately is being folded into CRM and planning contracts they already pay for, which changes renewal math mid-cycle. Before adding notetaker seats, firms should inventory what their existing vendors now ship natively and weigh whether a point solution's remaining edge justifies a second data-sharing agreement.
Hadrius (company release)

Hadrius raises $27 million to build agentic compliance infrastructure for advisors

Hadrius announced $27 million in combined seed and Series A funding led by CRV, including a $22 million Series A, with Y Combinator, Pathlight Ventures and the founders of Altruist, Jump AI and FINNY participating. The company says it now serves more than 500 financial institutions, cutting false positives by 95% and manual compliance work by 70%. Greenboard, a direct competitor, disclosed $20 million in total funding on May 12, including a $15.5 million Series A led by Base10 Partners.

Why it matters: Compliance was the advisor workflow where AI adoption trailed notetaking and planning, and two funded vendors are now selling into it head-on. CCOs evaluating these systems should press on how the software documents its own suppression decisions, because a tool that clears 95% of alerts needs an audit trail an examiner will accept without reconstruction.
Feathery (company release)

Feathery raises $30 million to automate onboarding and proposals at 300 firms

Feathery closed $30 million in a Series A led by Portage Ventures, joined by Index Ventures, Allstate Strategic Ventures, Clocktower Ventures, Erie Strategic Ventures and Bain Capital Ventures. The platform automates client onboarding, proposal generation, account opening, account maintenance and advisor transitions for more than 300 insurance and wealth firms, processing tens of millions of submissions monthly. Wealth clients include Sequoia Financial, Allworth Financial and Mission Wealth. Proceeds fund a cross-client data network and engineering hires.

Why it matters: Repapering and account opening offer the cleanest AI payback in wealth management because the work is high-volume, rules-bound and gates revenue recognition. Firms modeling 2027 acquisitions should treat transition cost as an automatable line rather than headcount, and diligence exactly how a vendor's cross-client data network segregates their client records.

CNBC

Six managers join Nvidia to source $500 billion of AI infrastructure financing

Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR agreed to work with Nvidia to source $500 billion for AI infrastructure, creating dedicated capital pools at scale for Nvidia customers. The structures treat compute capacity as collateral, delivered through private placements and bonds from special-purpose entities, and are partly designed so smaller AI companies can borrow to buy compute. Jensen Huang said he approached only those six firms and none declined. Deals are expected to reach market within months.

Why it matters: Compute is being underwritten as a collateral class whose residual values rest on GPU depreciation schedules never tested through a downcycle. Allocators reviewing private credit and infrastructure sleeves should ask managers directly how they mark GPU collateral and what recovery rate sits behind it, before these SPE bonds start clearing into diversified portfolios.

PMs who cut AI exposure into the selloff should size re-entry against a survey showing far fewer managers hiding in the same names, with Nvidia's print landing Wednesday.
