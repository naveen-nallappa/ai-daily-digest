The White House

Trump Order Asks Frontier Labs for Voluntary Pre-Release Model Reviews

President Trump on June 2 signed an executive order, "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security," directing federal agencies to build a framework for the secure deployment of frontier AI models. A central provision asks developers to voluntarily give the government early access to new models for up to 30 days before wider release to trusted partners. The order follows months of internal debate weighing national-security cyber risks from advanced models against the administration's preference to avoid mandatory safety reviews that could slow innovation.

Why it matters: A federal pre-release review channel, even a voluntary one, starts to formalize how frontier risk is governed in the US. Analysts covering OpenAI, Anthropic and Microsoft should track which labs opt in, since participation could become a procurement signal for government and regulated buyers; compliance teams at financial firms should watch whether "reviewed" status becomes a vendor-diligence checkbox for deploying frontier models.

Bipartisan House Draft Would Freeze State AI Laws for Three Years

Representatives Jay Obernolte (R-CA) and Lori Trahan (D-MA) released a 269-page discussion draft on June 4 of the Great American Artificial Intelligence Act, billed as the most comprehensive federal AI framework Congress has put forward. Its headline provision is a three-year preemption of state AI laws governing frontier-model development, which would freeze California's AI bills and Colorado's AI Act — set to take effect June 30 — among others. The draft pairs preemption with federal standards, and faces an uncertain path through committee.

Why it matters: A federal freeze on state rules would reset the compliance map firms are currently building toward, including Colorado's imminent deadline. Legal and compliance leads at wealth and asset managers should avoid over-investing in single-state controls until the preemption question resolves; the draft signals Washington, not statehouses, may set the binding AI rulebook financial firms ultimately design around.
AWS

OpenAI's GPT-5.5 and Codex Go Generally Available on Amazon Bedrock

AWS said on June 1 that OpenAI's GPT-5.5 and GPT-5.4 models and its Codex coding agent are now generally available on Amazon Bedrock, a month after a limited preview. GPT-5.5 is priced at $5 per million input and $30 per million output tokens, with GPT-5.4 at $2.50 and $15. Every call inherits the enterprise governance controls already used across AWS, including IAM permissions, VPC isolation, KMS encryption and CloudTrail logging. The launch puts OpenAI's frontier models on the largest cloud platform for the first time.

Why it matters: OpenAI distributing through AWS loosens the perception that frontier models are tied to a single cloud, broadening enterprise access under familiar compliance plumbing. CIOs at advisory and asset-management firms can now standardize on Bedrock governance while sourcing top models; software vendors reselling AI features should expect clients to ask why they cannot simply build the same capability on Bedrock directly.
