Major AI Updates
The Information

Nvidia agrees to acquire open-source model hub Hugging Face for $12.9 billion

Nvidia has agreed to buy Hugging Face for $12.9 billion, The Information reported late on August 26, with Bloomberg confirming talks the next day. Hugging Face, founded in 2016, is the largest public repository for open-weight models and was generating roughly $150 million of annual revenue, up from about $100 million two months earlier. The company turned down a $500 million Nvidia investment at a $7 billion valuation late last year, saying it did not want a dominant shareholder. Neither party has commented publicly.

Why it matters: Nvidia is buying the distribution layer for open-source AI, not more silicon — the place enterprises pick models before they pick hardware. For portfolios, it extends the Nvidia thesis from a chip cycle into a software moat, and it hands antitrust reviewers a concrete question about neutrality of the industry's default model registry. Watch for regulatory pushback in the EU.
NVIDIA Newsroom (joint announcement)

AWS commits to deploying two million more Nvidia GPUs across 2027 and 2028

AWS and Nvidia said on August 27 that AWS will deploy two million additional Blackwell Ultra, Rubin, and Rubin Ultra GPUs across its global infrastructure in 2027 and 2028. The commitment follows the more than one million GPUs AWS announced at GTC in March, capacity the companies say sold out ahead of plan, and takes total Nvidia capacity introduced at AWS this year above three million. The expansion also brings Nvidia Vera CPUs to AWS and includes 100,000 GPUs for US government AI factories.

Why it matters: This is the demand signal behind Wednesday's $108 billion Nvidia guide, extended two years forward and sourced from a hyperscaler that has been building its own Trainium silicon. Anyone underwriting a 2027 capex deceleration now has a dated, named commitment working against that view. Power and cooling suppliers are the second-order beneficiaries.
CNBC

Anthropic commits $45 billion over six years for 460 megawatts at Nscale

Anthropic has agreed to spend $45 billion over six years renting capacity at Nscale's West Virginia campus, Bloomberg reported August 26. The contract covers roughly 460 megawatts running on Nvidia Vera Rubin systems that begin coming online late in 2027, and is the largest single contract in Nscale's $51 billion revenue backlog. Anthropic moved in weeks after Microsoft walked away from a 1.35-gigawatt commitment at the same site, adding to deals already signed with AMD, Google, Broadcom, and SpaceX.

Why it matters: Anthropic is locking multi-year compute cost ahead of an IPO, which converts an operating uncertainty into a fixed obligation investors can model. For allocators tracking the AI credit complex, Nscale's backlog is now concentrated in one counterparty — worth checking before buying data-center paper or the equity of anyone financing that campus.
CNBC

116 companies sign open letter urging a global surge in AI cyber defense

OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, and Amazon led 116 signatories on an open letter published August 27 warning that AI-enabled attacks will become far more widespread as models improve. Signatories include CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Cloudflare, Cisco, and Oracle alongside financial institutions Capital One, Mastercard, and Visa. The letter asks every organization to raise its security baseline, deploy a mix of low-cost and frontier models in defense, and calls for coordination among local, national, and international governments.

Why it matters: Three card networks and a large consumer bank signing a frontier-lab document is the tell: financial firms now treat AI-enabled intrusion as a near-term operating risk, not a research topic. Expect vendor due-diligence questionnaires and cyber insurance renewals to start asking what models sit in your defense stack — a question most advisory firms cannot yet answer.
AI in Wealth Management
Royal Bank of Canada (Q3 2026 results)

RBC targets up to C$1 billion of AI enterprise value by fiscal 2027

RBC reported third-quarter profit of C$6.02 billion on August 27, against C$5.41 billion a year earlier, with wealth management net income up 32% to C$1.44 billion and pretax margin four points wider at 29.3%. On the call, management set an ambition to generate C$700 million to C$1 billion of enterprise value from AI by the end of fiscal 2027, funded inside the existing technology envelope. Named use cases span advisor productivity, back-office processing, and alpha generation in asset management.

Why it matters: RBC has put a dated dollar target on AI rather than a productivity anecdote, which gives analysts something to hold management to next year. Advisors evaluating platform moves should ask competing firms for the same disclosure: a quantified benefit, a deadline, and whether it is funded incrementally or absorbed into the current budget.
WealthManagement.com

Osaic names Sayee Bellamkonda its first chief AI and technology officer

Osaic said on August 27 that Sayee Bellamkonda joins as chief artificial intelligence and technology officer from Vialto Partners, where he was chief digital and technology officer, reporting directly to CEO Jamie Price. The role adds an AI title to the vacancy left by former CTO Ed Obuchowski and follows the departure of Osaic's CFO and head of wealth management solutions last month. Reverence Capital closed a recapitalization in April that raised more than $2 billion, with Bain Capital entering as a new investor.

Why it matters: The chief AI officer title is becoming standard at independent broker-dealers, arriving days after LPL hired a chief technology and information officer from Wells Fargo. For advisors weighing an Osaic affiliation, the practical question is whether fresh capital and a new AI owner translate into shipped tools before the next recruiting cycle, or another year of roadmap.
Wells Fargo (Advisor Gateway announcement)

Wells Fargo turns a $1.5 billion technology rebuild into an advisor recruiting push

Wells Fargo is courting hundreds of advisors after a five-year wealth overhaul that included $1.5 billion of technology spending, Bloomberg reported August 27. Independent advisors using the bank's infrastructure have brought in $17 billion of assets so far this year, on top of $24 billion from newly hired employee advisors. The centerpiece is Advisor Gateway, launched in the second quarter, whose Proposal and Portfolio Analytics module runs on BlackRock's Aladdin Wealth with a generative Auto Commentary feature, alongside a plain-language AI Teammate assistant.

Why it matters: AI tooling is now a recruiting instrument, pitched alongside payout and transition money. Advisors hearing this pitch should separate what is live from what is announced, and note that Aladdin Wealth is licensed infrastructure any competitor can buy. The differentiator is workflow integration and the underlying client data, not the risk engine itself, so ask for a live demo on a real book.
AI in Asset Management
ETF.com (weekly flows)

Nvidia's print pulls $3.7 billion into QQQ and $2.3 billion into chip ETFs

Nvidia added more than $400 billion of market value on August 27 after its results, and the flow data followed. The Invesco QQQ Trust took in $3.67 billion of net creations, the largest single-day inflow of any US ETF, while the SPDR S&P 500 ETF saw $3.82 billion of redemptions. VanEck's SMH, iShares' SOXX, and Direxion's leveraged SOXL together drew close to $2.3 billion. QQQ carries roughly a 9.4% Nvidia weight against about 8% in the S&P 500.

Why it matters: Clients are funding concentration deliberately, rotating out of the broad index and into the vehicle with more of one stock. That is a single-name risk decision dressed as an allocation, and it belongs in the next review: look through both tickers to the combined Nvidia weight before adding a thematic semiconductor sleeve on top.
Bloomberg

JPMorgan opens lender talks on a $5 billion package for Volta's AI data centers

JPMorgan has begun early outreach to lenders on a $5 billion debt package funding Volta Infra Holdings' data-center buildout, Bloomberg reported August 27. Volta was founded this year by two former Brookfield infrastructure executives, raised $300 million of venture funding at a $2.4 billion valuation earlier in August, and has signed a $10 billion capacity agreement with Anthropic. Its first major site is a 121-megawatt campus in Norway leased from bitcoin miner Bitdeer.

Why it matters: A months-old company is raising $5 billion against a single anchor tenant, and much of that paper will land in private credit funds sold to wealth clients through interval and evergreen structures. Ask managers for tenant concentration, lease term versus debt maturity, and residual value assumptions before the next capital call.
Bloomberg

JPMorgan says AI issuance is now priced at whatever investors will tolerate

Matthias Reschke, JPMorgan's head of European investment-grade finance, said on August 27 that AI-related issuance is testing the bond market's absorption capacity, with new-issue premiums the clearest evidence. "We don't doubt that we will be able to place it, it's all a question of price," he said. The comments follow JPMorgan's August 7 decision to raise its technology bond supply forecast and its earlier projection of record issuance for 2026.

Why it matters: Concession, not demand, is where AI risk is showing up in high-grade credit — a repricing that reaches core bond allocations rather than a thematic sleeve. Clients holding index-tracking IG funds are accumulating AI exposure passively; a look-through on technology and data-center issuer weights is a reasonable fourth-quarter review item.
