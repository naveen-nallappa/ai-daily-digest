CoreWeave doubles revenue to $2.58 billion as backlog reaches $104 billion

CoreWeave reported second-quarter revenue of $2.58 billion on August 11, more than double the $1.21 billion it posted a year earlier and slightly ahead of consensus. Revenue backlog reached $104 billion, lifted by a $21 billion agreement to supply AI cloud capacity to Meta through 2032 and a multi-year compute deal with Anthropic. Net loss widened to $626 million from $290 million as net interest expense more than doubled to $640 million. Shares rose roughly 11% after hours.

Why it matters: The AI compute buildout is increasingly financed with debt, and CoreWeave shows what that costs. Credit and equity PMs holding neoclouds or their paper should track interest expense against contracted backlog rather than headline revenue growth, because a $104 billion backlog only services $640 million of quarterly interest if customers draw capacity on the contracted schedule.
Bloomberg

Riot Platforms signs 20-year, $9.1 billion Anthropic lease at Rockdale campus

Riot Platforms disclosed a 20-year, $9.1 billion lease covering 191 megawatts at its Rockdale, Texas campus with what it described as a leading frontier AI lab; Bloomberg identified the tenant as Anthropic. Capacity arrives in two phases, 96 IT megawatts in December 2027 and 95 more in June 2028. Two five-year extension options could lift the total to roughly $16.1 billion. Anthropic becomes Rockdale's second tenant after AMD signed in January 2026.

Why it matters: Power-adjacent real assets are repricing fast. Managers holding digital-infrastructure, utilities or mining equities should re-underwrite them on contracted AI revenue and interconnect position rather than hashrate. Counterparty concentration deserves equal attention: a single 20-year lab tenant converts a commodity miner into something closer to a credit-sensitive landlord, with the tenant's funding runway as the real risk.

Nitrogen launches AI insurance analyzer with Schwab as first account-opening partner

Nitrogen launched Insurance Center at LPL's Focus conference on August 11, an AI tool that reads a household's plan, retirement income projections and long-term care details to produce a coverage score benchmarking existing policies against calculated need. Scores between 80 and 120 are treated as on track. LPL has approved the tool for its advisors. Nitrogen also unveiled Nucleus Account Opening to automate proposals into new accounts, with Charles Schwab as its first account-opening partner.

Why it matters: A custodian wiring itself into a third-party AI engine is the pattern worth watching: account opening increasingly starts in the advisor's software, not the custodian's portal. Firms evaluating this should confirm the coverage score is documented and reproducible, because an AI-generated insurance conclusion becomes a best-interest exhibit the moment a client acts on it.
WealthManagement.com

Seventy-eight groups urge senators to strip the AI sandbox from the CLARITY Act

Seventy-eight organizations, including the Consumer Federation of America, Americans for Financial Reform and the Public Investors Advocate Bar Association, wrote to Senators John Thune and Chuck Schumer opposing the CLARITY Act's proposed regulatory sandbox. The provision would let firms with fewer than 25 employees and under $10 million in revenue test AI tools inside a CFTC-SEC sandbox that could supersede state registration, qualification and licensing requirements. Thune has scheduled a procedural vote for when the Senate returns in September.

Why it matters: The carve-out would let AI advice tools operate outside state oversight and, critics argue, outside Regulation Best Interest. Compliance officers should track the September vote and map which internal AI pilots would qualify. The employee and revenue caps also create an incentive to spin AI projects into small affiliates, a structure worth flagging in vendor diligence.

Bloomberg

BlackRock signs labor pact with building trades to staff AI data centers

BlackRock's Global Infrastructure Partners and the AI Infrastructure Partnership signed a non-binding memorandum of understanding with North America's Building Trades Unions on August 10 to line up skilled labor for data-center and power projects. NABTU represents more than three million workers and runs over 1,900 apprenticeship facilities across the United States and Canada. The AI Infrastructure Partnership, a joint venture with Microsoft and others, targets $30 billion of equity and has raised $12.5 billion so far.

Why it matters: Labor and interconnects, not chips, increasingly determine whether a data center is delivered on schedule. Allocators underwriting AI infrastructure funds should ask sponsors how construction labor is secured, since project labor agreements compress schedules and reduce cost variance. It also signals sponsors are actively managing the siting and permitting opposition that has stalled projects.

Technology hedge funds post worst month since 2008 as AI trade reverses

HFR data released this week showed the HFRI EH: Technology Index fell 7.0% in July, its worst month since January 2008, as AI-related equities reversed sharply. The HFRI Fund Weighted Composite Index declined 1.1%, its first monthly loss since March, trimming the year-to-date gain to 6.2% after a second quarter that returned 6.4%. Why it matters: A 20-point spread between the best and worst deciles in a single month suggests hedge fund allocations have become a bet on AI positioning rather than on strategy labels. Allocators should re-run factor attribution on managers hired for diversification and confirm the sleeve is not simply levered AI beta wearing an uncorrelated label.
