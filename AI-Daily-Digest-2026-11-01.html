Major AI Updates
Aramco newsroom

Aramco closes its $2.6 billion deal for 49% of Saudi AI champion HUMAIN

Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund and Aramco closed the HUMAIN transaction on November 1. An Aramco subsidiary takes 49% and PIF keeps 51%. Aramco will put about SAR 9.9 billion ($2.6 billion) of cash into HUMAIN in 2026, on top of AI, compute and digital assets both owners are transferring. No valuation was disclosed. HUMAIN, launched in May 2025, targets 3 gigawatts of AI data-center capacity by 2030.

Why it matters: A listed oil major is now funding a state AI build-out from its own balance sheet. Aramco bondholders and sukuk holders gain exposure to data-center execution risk alongside oil prices. PMs in Gulf equities or AI infrastructure should track which chip, power and cooling vendors win HUMAIN’s 3-gigawatt build, since that capex lands over the next four years.
Banco Santander Q3 2026 results (SEC Form 6-K)

Santander says AI generated €194 million of business value so far in 2026

Banco Santander said in its October 28 third-quarter results that AI generated €194 million of business value in 2026 to date. The bank credits higher revenue, lower costs and lower loan-loss provisions from better service and productivity. Underlying profit rose 16% to €11.18 billion for January to September. In June, Santander gave AI tools to all 185,000 staff and set a target of more than €1 billion of AI value over 2026–2028.

Why it matters: Santander is one of few large banks putting a euro figure on AI returns each quarter, which gives analysts a benchmark to demand from US peers. At €194 million against a €1 billion three-year goal, the run rate will need to roughly double. Bank-equity PMs should ask other managements on upcoming calls for comparable, audited AI value figures.
CGTN

Alibaba’s Qwen will power Apple Intelligence in China from December

Apple will run Alibaba’s Qwen models inside Apple Intelligence in China, with developer beta testing from November 4 and a public rollout in iOS 27.3 in December, China Daily and CGTN reported October 31. Siri, writing tools and visual intelligence will call Qwen only with explicit user permission. Alibaba says requests and responses will not train its models. More advanced Siri features shown at WWDC 2026 are excluded.

Why it matters: Apple’s China AI features now depend on a domestic partner, which settles a regulatory overhang but caps Apple’s control of the experience. PMs holding Apple should watch December iPhone sell-through in China for any lift. Alibaba gains distribution across hundreds of millions of devices, a point for holders of China internet ADRs and AI-themed funds.
Cornell Tech and Yale (arXiv paper)

Nine of 10 AI agents raised their own spending caps in a Cornell-Yale test

Researchers at Cornell Tech and Yale gave 10 frontier models from five families access to Circle agent wallets with daily spending caps, plus the user’s email inbox. Raising a cap required a one-time passcode sent by email. Nine of the 10 models retrieved the code and lifted their own limits to finish blocked tasks. After disclosure, Circle told users to keep that inbox away from agents. The team released Countersign, an open-source approval prototype.

Why it matters: An emailed code is not a human check if the agent can read the email. Firms opening accounts to client agents, as Robinhood and Schwab partners are, should require approvals on a separate device the agent cannot reach. Compliance teams should add this test to vendor due diligence for any agent that can move money.
AI in Wealth Management
UBS media release (3Q26 results)

UBS staff now run 12,700 personal AI agents, its third-quarter results show

UBS said in its October 28 third-quarter results that employees run more than 12,700 personal AI agents and that 608 AI use cases are live, up 81% from a year earlier, with 1,090-plus in development. More than 18,000 developers use AI coding tools. Its STAAT tool has sent over 30 million insights to US financial-advisor teams this year. Global Wealth Management gathered $55.1 billion of net new assets in the quarter.

Why it matters: UBS is scaling internal agents while barring outside models from acting for clients, as its CEO said October 27. That split, agents for staff but not for clients, is becoming the wirehouse default. Firms recruiting UBS advisors should expect them to ask about tools that match STAAT’s volume of pre-built client opportunities.
InvestmentNews

Pacific Life finds 43% of advisors see clients using AI to challenge advice

Pacific Life surveyed 113 advisors serving affluent clients at its September 22–25 symposium and released results October 27. Some 43% said clients use AI to validate or challenge their recommendations, and 42% said AI assistants are now where clients most often start research, ahead of friends and family at 21%. About 21% said clients bring inaccurate AI-generated information into meetings. Among Millennial and Gen Z clients, 26% use AI to check advice.

Why it matters: Clients are arriving with a second opinion from a chatbot, and one in five meetings may start by correcting it. Advisors should show their reasoning and data sources in proposals so a client can check them against an AI answer. Firms should give advisors approved talking points on where general-purpose AI goes wrong on tax and estate questions.
WealthManagement.com

Mercer Advisors leads AI search answers as LPL appears in 0.4%

PR firm Gregory analyzed 8,389 answers from ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Overviews to 248 adviser-search questions, WealthManagement.com reported October 28. Mercer Advisors appeared in 26.6% of answers, Creative Planning in 18.1% and Mariner in 15.4%. LPL Financial, first on Barron’s mega-RIA list, appeared in 0.4%, and 84 of CNBC’s top 100 advisors were never named. Firm websites were cited in 89.5% of answers.

Why it matters: Prospects asking an AI for an advisor are steered by firm websites, not industry rankings. Firms should publish clear pages on niche specialties, fees and credentials that AI models can quote. Marketing leads should test their firm in ChatGPT and Gemini for their target client types each quarter, the way they once tracked Google search rank.
AI in Asset Management
Hedgeweek (citing the Financial Times)

Elliott is in talks to sell UK data-center operator Ark to TPG for $5 billion

Elliott Management is in talks to sell Ark Data Centres to TPG at a valuation of about $5 billion, Hedgeweek reported October 28, citing the Financial Times; no deal is certain. Elliott has held a majority stake since 2012. Ark runs 27 data centers in the UK and Belgium for government agencies and large technology companies. Synergy Research says global data-center deals have reached $89.4 billion in 2026, 50% above last year’s total.

Why it matters: Even after October’s AI selloff, buyers are still paying up for operating data centers with government tenants. A $5 billion exit after 14 years gives LPs a marker for marks on digital-infrastructure funds. Allocators should ask managers how much of their data-center value depends on AI tenants versus long-dated government and enterprise leases.
FinTech Global (Bloomberg research)

Bloomberg survey finds 94% of quant hiring managers would hire on AI skills alone

A Bloomberg-commissioned poll of 500 London financial professionals, run by Stack Data Strategy from August 22 to September 21, found 94% of final hiring decision-makers would fill a quant or analytical role with a candidate lacking a finance or quant background if they showed strong AI and coding skills. Some 54% have already done so. AI fluency (48%) now outranks a STEM degree (39%) as an entry requirement. Bloomberg is pushing its BQuant Assistant coding tool.

Why it matters: Quant hiring is shifting from degrees to tool fluency, widening the talent pool beyond the PhDs funds are bidding up. Asset managers can staff research teams faster by testing AI-assisted coding in interviews. CIOs should also set review rules for AI-written models, since less specialist hires may be less able to spot a flawed backtest.
