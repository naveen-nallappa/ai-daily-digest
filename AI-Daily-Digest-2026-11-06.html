Major AI Updates
CNBC

Nasdaq drops 5.5% and chip stocks 12% as midterm results hit the AI trade

The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5% on November 5, the S&P 500 lost 4.4% and the Dow 3.4%, Reuters reported, as all 11 S&P sectors declined. The Philadelphia semiconductor index dropped 12%, its second-biggest fall on record. Nvidia slid 9%, Micron, AMD and Marvell each lost about 17%, and SanDisk 22%. Investors cited fears that Democrats, after sweeping midterm wins, will use control of Congress to slow the data-center build-out. Chip stocks rebounded on November 6.

Why it matters: The selloff tested how concentrated client portfolios have become in a handful of AI names after a two-year run. Advisors should pull household-level exposure to semiconductors and AI-linked ETFs before clients call, and revisit rebalancing bands for accounts that drifted past targets. PMs should treat data-center policy as a political risk factor for capex-dependent names, not just a local zoning issue.
Google

Google commits $17 billion to its first Wyoming data center near Cheyenne

Google said on November 5 it will invest $17 billion over the long term in a data center in Laramie County, Wyoming, its first in the state. Power will come partly from a 702-megawatt AEP fuel-cell project using Bloom Energy cells with carbon capture, with CO2 stored at Tallgrass’s sequestration hub. Google said it will pay for 100% of the power it uses. A University of Wyoming poll found only 30% of residents support more large AI data centers, WyoFile reported.

Why it matters: Hyperscalers are pairing new campuses with self-funded power to blunt the ratepayer backlash seen in Ohio and Texas this week. Infrastructure and utility PMs should note fuel cells and carbon capture winning contracts that once went to grid upgrades. Advisors holding Bloom, AEP or midstream names through infrastructure funds should expect project announcements, not rate cases, to drive those positions.
OpenAI

OpenAI limits GPT-6 Cyber, rated Critical for cyber capability, to vetted defenders

OpenAI published a system card on November 5 for GPT-6 Cyber, a security variant of GPT-6 Astra that it rates Critical in cybersecurity, its top risk tier. Access is limited to approved Daybreak Red organizations, with identity checks, single sign-on, multi-factor authentication and API IP allowlisting. The model scored 96% on exploit-development tasks and 97% on red-teaming evaluations. OpenAI also said an autonomous pipeline found 948 privilege-escalation flaws in the Linux kernel.

Why it matters: Frontier labs now gate their most capable cyber models by customer, so firms without approved access face attackers who may hold equivalent tools. Bank and asset-manager security chiefs should ask core vendors whether they are in such programs and how fast they patch AI-found flaws. Compliance teams should expect examiners to ask how firms test against AI-driven attacks.
AI in Wealth Management
Bank of America Investor Relations

Bank of America says over 25,000 wealth professionals now work with AI tools

Bank of America’s November 5 presentation to the BancAnalysts Association of Boston said more than 25,000 wealth professionals are equipped with AI to assist clients, against about 20,000 on its Meeting Journey tool in the second quarter. More than 200,000 employees have AI tools, and the bank counts about 130 generative AI use cases and over 1,800 AI patent filings. Erica has more than 24 million active users and over 550 million interactions this year.

Why it matters: Merrill and the Private Bank now report AI coverage across nearly their whole adviser base, matching Morgan Stanley’s near-universal adoption claims. Rival wirehouse and IBD leaders should expect recruiting talks to include which AI tools come with a seat. The deck gives no wealth productivity figure, so analysts should press for hours saved per adviser or asset lift at the next earnings call.
Anthropic

Anthropic opens Claude Personal Finance beta with read-only Plaid bank links

Anthropic began rolling out a Personal Finance beta on November 5 for US subscribers on its Pro, Max and Ultra plans. Users link up to 16 bank and card accounts through Plaid, and Claude reads balances and up to 24 months of transactions but cannot move or invest money. Anthropic said the tool is not designed to give investment advice and that hub conversations are excluded from model training. It follows OpenAI’s ChatGPT Finances, launched in May.

Why it matters: Both leading AI labs now give clients a spending and net-worth view outside the adviser relationship. Advisors should assume prospects arrive with AI-generated cash-flow analyses and be ready to show where planning adds value beyond aggregation. Firms should also update guidance on clients sharing account data with consumer AI tools, since Plaid links sit outside the firm’s own privacy controls.
InvestmentNews

Mariner says its Hugo AI agents uncovered $22 billion of unknown client assets

Mariner CEO Marty Bicknell said at Schwab IMPACT in Boston that Hugo, the firm’s AI agent workforce, has uncovered $22 billion of client assets its advisors did not know about, InvestmentNews reported on November 4. Hugo runs on Humanity Labs, which agreed in July to deploy 700 AI agents for Mariner. Mariner serves about 70,000 households and targets 500,000 by the end of 2028. Bicknell said 80 of its 200 client associates want to become advisors.

Why it matters: Held-away asset discovery is the first agentic use case at a large RIA with a dollar figure attached, though the $22 billion is unaudited. Advisors at rival firms should expect aggregators to use similar agents to find assets to consolidate. Firms evaluating agent vendors should ask what share of discovered assets became managed AUM, the number that will prove the return.
InvestmentNews

Mercer Advisors buys AI-native tax firm Fifteenth to expand equity-compensation planning

Mercer Advisors, a Denver RIA with $114 billion in client assets, said on November 5 it acquired Fifteenth, an AI-native tax and accounting firm whose platform handles equity compensation and complex cases such as stock options and tender offers. Fifteenth serves more than 1,000 professionals, mostly tech-company employees. Co-founders Ankur Dahiya and Laura Moreno join Mercer, and terms were not disclosed. Tax professionals make up about one in 10 Mercer staff, and the firm expects one in eight by year-end.

Why it matters: Large RIAs are buying AI-built tax capability rather than building it, as tax planning becomes a main differentiator for wealthy tech clients. Advisors competing for equity-compensation households should check whether their tax partners can model option exercises and tender offers at scale. Vendors selling tax software to RIAs now face a buyer that owns a competing platform.
AI in Asset Management
Hedgeweek

AI-focused hedge fund VARA loses 33% in October as Asia funds face margin calls

Value Aligned Research Advisors, a Princeton, New Jersey, hedge fund focused on AI, lost 33% in October after a 44% drop in July, according to an investor letter reported by Bloomberg on November 5. Its Class C shares were still up 13% for the year. In Asia, Hong Kong’s Aspex fell 16% in October, and Goldman Sachs prime brokerage estimates Asia equity hedge funds lost 9.4%, cutting year-to-date gains to 14%, Hedgeweek reported.

Why it matters: Concentrated AI funds have now shown drawdowns of a third in a single month, twice this year. Allocators should check leverage, liquidity terms and counterparty headroom in thematic AI managers before redemptions cluster. Advisors placing clients in AI-themed hedge funds or feeder vehicles should confirm position limits and revisit suitability for clients who bought after the summer rally.
Hedgeweek

Citadel gains 1.7% in October as Balyasny and Millennium lose ground

Citadel’s flagship Wellington fund rose 1.7% in October and is up 15.3% this year, and D.E. Shaw’s Composite gained 0.7%, Hedgeweek reported on November 6, citing Bloomberg. Balyasny lost 3.4% and is down 2.7% for the year, ExodusPoint fell 2.4% and Millennium 2.3%. Millennium is pulling more than $1.5 billion from external managers Troluce and Axebrook. Quant manager Quantedge gained 7.2% in October and is up 60% this year.

Why it matters: Crowded AI longs hurt pod shops whose risk models treated those trades as diversified. Allocators to multi-manager funds should ask how much gross exposure sat in the same semiconductor names and how fast pods were cut. The gap between Citadel and Balyasny shows manager selection matters more than the multi-strategy label in clients’ alternatives sleeves.
Hedgeweek

XN tells investors it plans to buy more Anthropic shares in the expected IPO

Gaurav Kapadia’s XN told investors it intends to be a large participant in Anthropic’s anticipated initial public offering despite the AI selloff, according to a letter seen by the Financial Times and reported by Hedgeweek on November 6. XN first invested in 2025 and has joined three funding rounds. Its stake was worth about $5.3 billion at the May round that valued Anthropic at $965 billion. Anthropic has reportedly targeted an IPO valuation above $1.5 trillion.

Why it matters: Crossover funds that built private AI-lab stakes are signaling they will anchor the IPO book rather than sell into it. PMs should prepare for index-inclusion questions once a listing above $1 trillion nears eligibility. Advisors fielding client requests for IPO allocations should keep position sizes small, given the volatility AI shares showed this week.
