Mercer Survey Finds 55% of Asset Managers Now Embed AI in Investment Processes

A majority of asset managers now use AI directly in their investment process, according to Mercer's 2026 AI in Asset Management survey of 131 global managers, published this week. The survey found 55% have integrated AI into at least one strategy's investment process, 27% are running pilots or proofs of concept, and 18% report no integration. Why it matters: The data gives allocators a concrete benchmark: a manager with no AI integration is now in a shrinking minority. Manager-research and due-diligence teams should ask where AI actually sits in a manager's process — research and signals versus real portfolio construction — and treat vague "we use AI" claims skeptically given how few managers embed it in execution.

Janus Henderson Survey: 79% of Investors Want Advisors to Disclose AI Use

A Janus Henderson investor survey released May 19 found that 79% of investors with $250,000 or more in assets would be upset if their advisor used AI without disclosing it, yet only 33% said their advisor has discussed how AI is used in the practice. The survey of 1,000 investors also found 85% hold the advisor ultimately responsible for AI-generated advice. Comfort with advisor AI is selective: 87% are fine with AI-built educational materials, but support drops for drafting client emails or investment recommendations.

Why it matters: The disclosure gap is a live compliance and trust risk: many advisors using AI have not told clients, and most clients expect to be told. Practice-management and compliance leads should add a plain-language AI-use disclosure to client onboarding and annual reviews now, and scope AI to lower-sensitivity tasks like educational content rather than direct recommendations until client trust catches up.
