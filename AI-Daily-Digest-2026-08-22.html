Nvidia pays $6 billion for a non-exclusive licence to Poolside's model factory

Bloomberg reported on August 20 that Nvidia will pay $6 billion under a non-exclusive licence covering Poolside's Model Factory, the system behind its Laguna family of open-weight coding models. Nvidia is separately investing $1 billion at a $12 billion pre-money valuation and extending offers to 109 Poolside engineers who built Laguna. Poolside's three co-founders stay in place and the company continues to operate independently. The licence fee is expected to reach Poolside investors by the end of 2027.

Why it matters: Structured licensing plus mass hiring delivers acquisition economics without merger review, and it creates a liquidity path for private AI positions that never reaches an IPO or a sale. Allocators marking venture and growth-stage AI stakes should expect more distributions arriving as licence proceeds, where timing, tax treatment and valuation marks all differ from a conventional exit.

Broadcom seeks more than $60 billion of debt for Anthropic chip financing

Bloomberg reported on August 20 that Broadcom is negotiating with lenders on a senior secured tranche of roughly $60 billion to $70 billion, alongside a junior piece near $30 billion that could take the package to about $100 billion. Blackstone and Apollo Global Management are among investors in discussions, extending a $35 billion partnership the three firms unveiled in June to expand Anthropic's compute. Anthropic does not buy the chips: investors finance the hardware and lease it to the company.

Why it matters: AI compute is increasingly funded off balance sheet through leases underwritten by private credit, so the exposure lands in direct-lending and asset-backed sleeves rather than in tech equity. Allocators reviewing private credit managers should ask what share of the book is chip- or data-centre-linked, and how residual values on three-year-old accelerators are being marked.
The Information

AT&T cut advanced AI task costs by 56% using model routing

The Information reported on August 20 that AT&T reduced the cost of coding and other advanced AI tasks by as much as 56% using LiteLLM routers, which grade task complexity and send simpler queries to cheaper models. Measured output quality declined about 2%. The carrier wants employee spending on Anthropic and OpenAI models held flat, and plans to lift the open-weight share of employee queries from roughly 40% today to between 60% and 70% in the coming years.

Why it matters: A named enterprise has now put a number on frontier-model substitution, which cuts against the assumption that inference revenue scales linearly with adoption. Anyone underwriting AI-lab revenue or holding concentrated positions in model providers should treat routing and open-weight adoption as a live margin risk rather than a distant one.

New York overtakes the Bay Area as the top US tech-talent market

A CBRE report released on August 21 put New York's tech workforce at about 394,300 jobs against the San Francisco Bay Area's 375,730 — the first time New York has led in 13 years of the study. AI-related roles now account for nearly a third of US tech job listings and grew 45% year over year across the US and Canada. Finance-sector AI hiring in New York and Bay Area job cuts drove the crossover, with both markets adding more than 20,000 AI positions since mid-2025.

Why it matters: Financial firms are now the marginal bidder for AI engineers, which raises the build-versus-buy threshold for wealth and asset managers staffing internal AI teams. Firms budgeting 2027 headcount should benchmark against New York finance compensation rather than national tech averages, or expect vendor partnerships to win the comparison on cost by default.

NewEdge Capital Group

NewEdge reports advisor results two months into a firmwide Claude rollout

NewEdge Capital Group, which supports more than 450 advisors and affiliated representatives across three business units, said on August 21 that giving advisors direct access to Anthropic's Claude in June has produced retirement roadmaps, plain-English translations of annuity and insurance contracts, and client webinars built in minutes rather than hours. The firm buys enterprise licences centrally in tokens while advisors purchase their own, and is now assembling a best-practices library, webinars and advisor forums.

Why it matters: This is one of the first RIA aggregators to publish specific use cases rather than pilot metrics, and the split cost model is the notable design choice — central licensing for governance, individual purchase to force adoption. Firms scoping a rollout should copy the enablement layer, since unguided model access reliably produces low usage and inconsistent output.

AI notetakers Jump and Zocks push into CRM territory with new workflows

An August 20 analysis noted that Jump and Zocks have reached roughly 40,000 advisor users in about two years and are now expanding well beyond transcription. Jump launched account-opening and onboarding workflows triggered directly from a captured new-client meeting. Zocks launched Client Queries, which answers natural-language questions across a book of business — for example, which clients hold at least $500,000 in held-away assets and are due for a review this quarter.

Ai Funds (company release)

An ETF with an AI model as its portfolio strategist began trading August 21

Ai Funds launched the Ai Funds High Conviction US Equity AI-Managed ETF, ticker HIAI, on Cboe BZX on August 21. A proprietary Bayesian model called BAILA acts as investment strategist: it selects roughly 40 to 60 names from the 1,000 most liquid US equities, sizes positions, sets overall market exposure and can rotate to cash, with weekly reviews. Milliman Financial Risk Management, which oversaw $273.2 billion as of June 30, serves as sub-adviser.

Why it matters: Due-diligence frameworks built for discretionary managers or rules-based quant products do not map cleanly onto a model that resets its own market exposure weekly. Analysts reviewing HIAI or the products that follow it need answers on model-change governance, retraining cadence, and what a style-drift check means when the strategist is not a person.

PMs holding concentrated AI positions should check whether recent price action in those names reflects forced supply, and size any addition against how much of the overhang has actually cleared.
