Major AI Updates
OpenAI

OpenAI halves GPT-6 token prices hours after Anthropic cuts Opus 5.5 by 20%

OpenAI released GPT-6 Sol and Luna on September 22 at $2/$10 and $0.10/$0.50 per million input and output tokens, down from $4/$20 and $0.20/$1.20 for the GPT-5.6 equivalents. OpenAI says these are permanent list prices, not promotional, and credits inference efficiency and prompt caching. Anthropic shipped Claude Opus 5.5 the same day at $4/$20 with cache reads at 20 cents, which it says works out about 40% cheaper than Opus 5 on a typical workload.

Why it matters: Per-seat AI budgets set this spring assumed today's token costs; a permanent halving resets the build-versus-buy math on advisor copilots and research agents. Firms negotiating enterprise licences before Anthropic's September 30 credit deadline should ask for price-step-down clauses rather than locking a rate, and PMs modelling lab revenue should treat unit price, not usage, as the variable now moving.
Security Council Report

Altman and Amodei brief the UN Security Council alongside DeepSeek and Moonshot

France, holding September's Council presidency, convened a high-level briefing on AI and international security on September 23. Briefers are Yoshua Bengio for the UN scientific panel, Sam Altman in person, Dario Amodei by video and Hugging Face's Clément Delangue. Chinese developers DeepSeek and Moonshot were invited to make statements. Altman is promoting global AI standards; Amodei is proposing independent evaluators embedded in frontier labs, coordination among democratic states and limits on the pace of recursive self-improvement.

Why it matters: This is the first time US and Chinese frontier developers address the Council together, which makes an incident-reporting standard more plausible than a licensing regime. Compliance teams drafting AI policies should watch whether the Council language matches OpenAI's September 21 CAISI proposal, since a single classification scheme would let firms write one incident playbook instead of one per jurisdiction.
DIGITIMES

DeepSeek commits to Huawei Ascend 950 chips for training its largest models

DeepSeek chief executive Liang Wenfeng told investors in a closed-door meeting that large-scale training on Huawei's Ascend 950 series begins as early as late 2026, calling the break from the Nvidia ecosystem the company's biggest bet. DeepSeek is weighing at least 160,000 Ascend 950DT processors in a roughly 1 GW data center in Ulanqab, Inner Mongolia, and is spending about CNY 30 billion on compute. Liang has estimated 200,000 Ascend chips match roughly 50,000 Nvidia GB300s.

Why it matters: A 4:1 chip ratio means Chinese labs buy power and floor space rather than silicon, which is where the marginal cost now sits. PMs holding China AI exposure should separate memory and networking suppliers, which gain from the higher chip count, from Nvidia's China revenue line, which loses the training workload even if inference stays.
Benzinga

Nvidia turns to smaller sites and power deals as grid limits strand AI hardware

Nvidia is shifting away from giant campuses toward smaller facilities, software that extracts more work per watt and deals that steer electricity to customers running its chips, according to reporting on September 22. A buyer holding a signed power agreement and a data hall can now take delivery of GPUs faster than it can obtain a grid connection. The move follows the AI Energy Management Alliance Nvidia launched with Google and Emerald AI on September 16, which now spans 18 companies.

Why it matters: The binding constraint has moved from chip supply to interconnection queues, which is why Texas's permit freeze matters more than any single order book. Infrastructure and private-credit allocators should underwrite sponsors on secured power rather than announced megawatts, and equity PMs should expect utilities and electrical-equipment names to track AI capex more closely than the accelerator makers do.
AI in Wealth Management
WealthManagement.com

Savvy Wealth opens its custodial stack to outside RIAs with 90-second onboarding

Savvy Wealth, the AI-native RIA running $9 billion across more than 150 advisors, launched the Savvy Custodial Platform on September 23. It bundles fully digital onboarding in as little as 90 seconds, asset transfers in a few clicks, white-labelled client experience and integrated CRM, billing, trading and reporting, and is now available to unaffiliated RIAs. It is not a legal custodian: Savvy Wealth Management LLC is the introducing broker-dealer, with clearing, execution and custody at Fidelity's National Financial Services.

Why it matters: An AI-native RIA selling its own plumbing to competitors is the same playbook Altruist ran before Vanguard paid $4.6 billion for it, and it arrives while Schwab's Claude connector is still read-only until Q4. RIAs evaluating a move should price the introducing-broker structure carefully, since NFS holds the assets but Savvy controls the interface, the data and the switching cost.
WealthManagement.com

Milemarker ships a firm-level MCP server spanning 140 custodians and legacy systems

Milemarker launched Milemarker Firm MCP on September 22, a Model Context Protocol server giving an advisory firm one governed AI connection across its own data, with more than 300 features covering data exploration, reporting, entitlements, governance, workflows and app building. It draws on the 140-plus custodians, CRMs, portfolio and planning systems already integrated with the Snowflake-native platform, plus legacy systems that have no API. It works with Claude, ChatGPT, Codex and Gemini, and firm data stays in the firm's own Snowflake environment. Every client firm has access with no waitlist.

Why it matters: Vendor-published MCP servers each expose one system; this one sits at the firm boundary, which is the layer a CCO can actually supervise and entitle. Firms weighing Claude for Financial Advisors against a build should note that model-agnostic access removes the lock-in risk in a single-vendor connector set, and that legacy data with no API is where most reconciliation work still lives.
WealthManagement.com

$36 billion SEIA rebuilds its data foundation with Invent to power SEIA Brain

Signature Estate & Investment Advisors said on September 22 it has consolidated enterprise data from multiple custodians, its advisory and broker-dealer businesses and its in-house TAMP onto a single governed foundation built with Invent. The unified environment underpins SEIA Brain, a proprietary AI framework for marketing, sales, finance, operations and compliance staff to query data and automate routine workflows. Authorized users can now build, publish and export dynamic reports without third-party reporting specialists. Neither firm would disclose the cost.

Why it matters: Invent's pitch is vendor-agnostic plumbing rather than an all-in-one agentic suite, which matters for hybrids running a broker-dealer and a TAMP alongside multiple custodians. Operations leaders should treat data consolidation as the gating item before any copilot pilot, and should press vendors on whether SEC-registered workflows stay deterministic where probabilistic output is not defensible.
AI in Asset Management
BlackRock

BlackRock paper argues AI agents will settle their own purchases in stablecoins

BlackRock published "The Machine-Native Economy" on September 23, arguing that autonomous agents paying for data requests, services and compute make AI a leading driver of digital-asset demand. It names stablecoins as the first beneficiary, citing Coinbase's x402 protocol as one route for agents to pay for API calls, and flags that conventional payment networks are adapting too. On compute it is more cautious: tokenized claims could be traded or pledged as collateral, but liquid markets for standardized contracts do not yet exist.

Why it matters: The largest asset manager is putting a research frame around agentic payments while its own clients still lack a way to price compute exposure outside equities. Allocators should read this as a pipeline signal for tokenized-compute products rather than an allocation call, and advisors fielding stablecoin questions now have an institutional source that separates the near-term payments case from the speculative compute one.
Bloomberg

Bank shares sell off on agent disintermediation as the Nasdaq 100 sets a record

The Nasdaq 100 closed at a record on September 22 and has gained for five straight sessions, up 22% year to date, but Bloomberg reported on September 23 that the move carried a twist: investors are marking down businesses that agents can intermediate, from banking to travel booking, even as the chip and platform complex rallies. Shopify led the index with a 7.0% gain after a partnership putting Shop Pay inside Meta's Muse agent. Bank shares fell on the same session.

Why it matters: This is the first session where the market priced agents as a revenue threat to financial distribution rather than a cost saving, which cuts directly at brokerage, payments and advice fee pools. PMs running financials overweights should stress-test which revenue lines survive an agent choosing the counterparty, and advisors holding bank-heavy value sleeves should be ready to explain a factor rotation that no longer tracks rates.
Pensions & Investments

FINQ's fully AI-managed ETFs post 23% returns against the S&P 500's 11.6%

FINQ's AIUP and AINT, the first SEC-registered US ETFs in which selection, weighting and rebalancing are handled entirely by an autonomous system with no discretionary override, listed on NYSE Arca on February 5, 2026. Through August 31 AIUP returned 23.51% and the dollar-neutral AINT 23.83%, against 11.61% for the S&P 500. The model ranks every S&P 500 constituent daily on market, financial and textual data. AI-managed funds remain small and expensive, with the cheapest at 58 basis points and most above 100.

Why it matters: Seven months of live outperformance is a track record allocators can actually diligence, unlike the backtests that sank the earlier Watson-era funds. Due-diligence teams should ask what happens at a model version change, since there is no portfolio manager to interview and the prospectus, not a person, defines the process; fee committees should weigh 100 basis points against systematic managers charging less for comparable turnover.
