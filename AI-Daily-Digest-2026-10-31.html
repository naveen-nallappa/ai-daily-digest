Major AI Updates
Financial Times

OpenAI trims its funding target to $20 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation

OpenAI has cut the round it is marketing to about $20 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation, from $30 billion at $1.4 trillion, the Financial Times reported late on October 30. The reset follows investor pushback during a selloff that has taken the Nasdaq 100 down about 10% from its October 6 record. OpenAI has already pushed its IPO plans to 2027, while Anthropic is preparing a November Nasdaq listing that could raise up to $100 billion.

Why it matters: An 11% cut to the leading private AI valuation is a read-through for every late-stage AI holding. Advisors with clients in private AI funds, interval funds or tender-offer vehicles should expect marks to lag public prices and ask managers about valuation policy. For Anthropic IPO requests, size positions for repricing risk rather than the last round’s headline value.
The Globe and Mail

Canada takes a C$1.6 billion stake in Cohere’s C$7 billion Series E round

Ottawa will invest up to C$1.6 billion in Cohere through the Canada Development Investment Corporation, part of a Series E of up to C$7 billion announced October 29 that values the Toronto AI lab at about C$36 billion. Germany’s Schwarz Group leads the round, with Nvidia, Inovia and Bell Ventures participating. The federal money is staged against milestones, and Cohere commits to invest C$1.7 billion in Canadian computing infrastructure and create 1,000-plus jobs by 2030.

Why it matters: Governments are now buying equity in model makers, not just funding compute. That makes sovereign AI a policy-backed investment theme, with a state shareholder that can shape strategy and cap upside. PMs running AI or Canadian equity sleeves should track which listed data-center, power and chip suppliers win Cohere’s C$1.7 billion compute build over the next four years.
Reuters

Nasdaq falls 2.8% as AI cash-burn fears sink chip stocks

The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.84% and the S&P 500 1.83% on October 30, with the chip index down about 7%, Reuters reported. Intel dropped about 29%, its worst day on record, after forecasting a cash burn this year on a weak PC market, manufacturing problems and expansion spending; AMD fell about 10%. The Nasdaq 100 lost over $1 trillion in the session and more than $2 trillion in October. S&P Global also moved Meta’s credit outlook to negative, citing its AI spending.

Why it matters: The selloff has spread from the hyperscalers that spend to the chipmakers that sell to them, and now into credit ratings. Advisors should check how much client equity risk sits in semiconductors through index funds and AI ETFs. Fixed-income PMs holding Big Tech bonds should treat a negative outlook as an early signal of wider spreads on new issuance.
Reuters

Nvidia discusses guaranteeing Nscale debt for an Anthropic data center

Nvidia is in talks to provide billions of dollars in guarantees backing Nscale’s borrowing for a West Virginia data center that will serve Anthropic, Reuters reported October 30, citing The Information; terms are not final. Anthropic agreed in August to spend $45 billion on compute at Nscale’s Monarch campus. Bloomberg reported on October 19 that the two were negotiating a second deal of about $45 billion for a 460-megawatt building using Nvidia’s Vera Rubin chips.

Why it matters: A chipmaker guaranteeing its customer’s debt is vendor financing, and it ties Nvidia’s balance sheet to Anthropic’s ability to pay. Credit and private-credit PMs should look for guarantees like this in data-center deals and price the circular exposure. Advisors fielding Anthropic IPO questions can point clients to its compute commitments as a key risk factor in the S-1.
AI in Wealth Management
Ameriprise IR (Q3 2026 earnings call)

Ameriprise starts an early release of AI meeting-prep and service agents

Ameriprise CEO Jim Cracchiolo said on the October 29 third-quarter call that the firm is beginning an early release of an AI meeting-prep agent and an AI service assistant for advisors. He said Ameriprise has delivered tens of millions of AI-generated insights and that nearly three-quarters of practices use them. Practices using the current tools can gain more than 30 hours of capacity a week, and broader data integration and guided workflows are planned for 2027.

Why it matters: Ameriprise is moving from notetaking to agents that do prep and service work, built on its own data lake rather than a vendor’s stack. Independent firms recruiting Ameriprise teams should benchmark their tech against that 30-hour claim. Firms evaluating Jump, Zocks or Salesforce agents should ask vendors for adoption data as specific as three-quarters of practices.
InvestmentNews

Schwab survey finds 49% of RIAs invest in AI but only 7% use agents

Schwab Advisor Services’ annual independent advisor outlook study, released at IMPACT on October 29, found 49% of RIAs investing in AI tools for their workflows and 7% using agentic AI. Eighty-one percent expect AI to handle part of research and analysis within three years, and 67% expect it to cut administrative time. Two-thirds of those expecting an AI impact want to keep it behind the scenes, and 38% rank AI regulation as their top advocacy issue.

Why it matters: The gap between 49% and 7% shows most RIAs are buying assistants, not handing work to agents. That is the market Schwab’s Claude seat credit and Ester rollout are aimed at. Firm owners should decide which tasks to hand to agents, and how to supervise them, before custodians and regulators set those terms for them.
WealthManagement.com (Schwab IMPACT)

Mariner’s Humanity Labs deal puts 700 AI agents to work at $50,000 each

Humanity Labs CEO Andrei Pop said at Schwab IMPACT on October 29 that its five-year deal with Mariner covers 700 full-time AI agents at $50,000 a year each, about $35 million a year. The agents start with back-office work such as onboarding, account opening, compliance reviews, reporting and billing, then move to middle- and front-office tasks. Pop did not say how many are live, and said Humanity Labs has fewer than 10 such deals.

Why it matters: Pricing AI as headcount gives operations leaders a direct comparison with hiring, at about $50,000 a role with no benefits. Aggregators should model whether deals like this widen the margin gap with smaller acquirers. Compliance teams should ask how an AI worker doing compliance reviews is supervised and documented before signing a similar contract.
AI in Asset Management
T. Rowe Price Q3 2026 results (SEC 8-K)

T. Rowe Price says AI is moving into the core of how it invests

CEO Rob Sharps said on the October 29 third-quarter call that T. Rowe Price’s Anthropic deal gives portfolio managers and analysts Claude and Cowork and developers Claude Code, and that “AI is moving into the core of how we invest.” CIO Eric Veiel said the firm will review middle- and back-office processes over the next 12 to 24 months. T. Rowe reported $1.86 trillion in assets and $28.3 billion of third-quarter net outflows.

Why it matters: Under outflow pressure, T. Rowe is betting AI improves research before it cuts costs, and that 20 years of proprietary research and trading data is its edge. Consultants and gatekeepers should ask active managers which AI work draws on data rivals lack. Expect efficiency claims to show up in 2027 and 2028 budgets, not 2026 margins.
InvestmentNews

ICI publishes a framework for putting AI agents into fund operations

The Investment Company Institute’s Financial Innovation Advisory Committee issued “AI and the Future of Asset Management Operations” on October 29, setting out an intelligent operating model for fund back offices. Use cases include catching settlement breaks before trade details go out, pulling data across trading and shareholder-servicing teams during large redemptions, and checking questionable prices before NAV is struck. It cites a 2026 Mercer survey finding 55% of asset managers use AI in at least one investment process.

Why it matters: The fund industry’s trade body now tells members to measure AI by fewer settlement exceptions and operations that scale without proportional headcount. COOs can use the framework to set targets for vendors and outsourcers. Boards and CCOs should expect SEC examiners, already focused on AI, to ask how agents touching NAV and settlement are tested and overseen.
Hedgeweek

Citadel, Bridgewater and Man Group flag AI agent risks at a Bloomberg conference

Executives from three of the largest hedge funds described how they fence in AI agents at a Bloomberg conference in New York, Hedgeweek reported October 28. Citadel interim CTO Andrew Janian said agents stay in designated environments and fundamental teams use AI to challenge ideas, not generate theses. Bridgewater’s Aaron Linsky said keeping autonomous agents from creating security or operational risk is unresolved. Man Group CIO Greg Bond warned against staff “cognitive surrender” to AI outputs.

Why it matters: The firms furthest along now spend as much effort on controls as on capabilities. Allocators running operational due diligence on quant and multi-manager funds should ask where agents can act, who can override them and how their outputs are checked. The same questions apply to any asset manager adding agentic research tools to its investment process this year.
