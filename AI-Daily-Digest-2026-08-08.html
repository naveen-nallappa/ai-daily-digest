Nvidia and SK Group sign $500 billion-plus AI infrastructure partnership

Nvidia and South Korea's SK Group signed letters of intent on August 3 for a partnership valued at more than $500 billion spanning AI data centers and next-generation memory. SK Telecom will build a 2-gigawatt AI cloud in South Korea using Nvidia's Vera Rubin systems paired with SK hynix HBM4 chips, with the first data center slated to begin operations in 2027. The agreement, unveiled at a San Francisco meeting between Jensen Huang and President Lee Jae-myung, ties Nvidia's compute roadmap to a captive supply of high-bandwidth memory.

Why it matters: Vertically bundling GPUs with a dedicated HBM supplier signals that memory, not just chips, is now the binding constraint on AI buildouts. Portfolio managers with semiconductor exposure should weight the memory supply chain, not only Nvidia, and treat multiyear compute-and-memory pacts as evidence the capex cycle extends well into 2027 rather than peaking this year.

Celestica, the contract manufacturer that builds servers and networking gear for hyperscalers, priced a $3 billion public equity offering on August 5, selling 9,677,419 shares at $310 apiece. Why it matters: When suppliers issue equity at a discount to fund capacity, it confirms order books are running ahead of balance sheets across the AI hardware chain. Investors holding data-center names should watch for dilution and margin pressure as the buildout is increasingly financed on the supply side, and treat these raises as a real-time gauge of how durable hyperscaler demand actually is.
Google DeepMind

Google DeepMind ships Gemini Robotics 2 with whole-body robot control

Google DeepMind released Gemini Robotics 2, extending its robotics models from tabletop manipulation to coordinated whole-body control, dexterity, and multi-robot teamwork. Three models ship together, including an on-device version that runs without an internet connection and adapts to new dual-arm platforms using fewer than 200 real-world examples. Demonstrated on Apptronik's Apollo 2 humanoid, the system hit a 92% success rate unscrewing a light bulb but far lower rates picking objects off the floor, underscoring how uneven physical AI remains.

Why it matters: General-purpose humanoid control moving on-device shortens the path from lab demo to warehouse deployment, a theme investors are already pricing into industrial-automation and robotics names. PMs building thematic exposure should separate capability claims from reliability data, since the gap between a 92% and a sub-50% task success rate is exactly what determines whether these robots reach commercial payback.

Bridgewater's AI-run macro fund surpasses $4.5 billion in assets

Bridgewater's AIA Labs macro strategy, live since late 2023 and run with AI as the primary decision-maker, has grown to more than $4.5 billion in assets after generating returns the firm says are uncorrelated to its human-led funds. The system combines Bridgewater's proprietary models with frontier models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and others, while human staff oversee risk and trade execution. Its growth tracks a broader shift, with an industry body reporting that roughly 47% of mid-to-large hedge funds now run generative AI in production.

Why it matters: A marquee macro shop scaling a fund where AI makes the calls, not just assists, moves autonomous investing from experiment to allocatable product. Investors considering AI-driven strategies should probe whether returns are genuinely uncorrelated or simply repackaged factor exposure, and demand clarity on how much discretion the models hold versus the humans still signing off on risk.

Man Group hands trade-idea generation to its AlphaGPT research agent

Man Group's quant equity unit detailed AlphaGPT, an agentic AI workflow that brainstorms investment hypotheses, turns them into code, and backtests potential signals, mimicking how a human researcher develops new strategies. The tool keeps humans in the loop for oversight while letting the roughly $170 billion manager process far more data and evaluate more ideas per researcher. Rivals including Vanguard and T. Rowe Price described similar agent-based tools that compare AI-generated consensus views against their own in-house research.

Why it matters: Pushing agents into signal generation, not just back-office tasks, targets the highest-value part of the quant workflow and could reshape research-team economics. Allocators should ask managers how AI-generated signals are validated and guarded against overfitting, since a tool that spins up thousands of backtested ideas also multiplies the risk of mistaking data-mined noise for durable alpha.
