Major AI Updates
OpenAI

OpenAI brings its frontier models and Codex to Google Cloud’s Model Garden

OpenAI said on October 21 that Google Cloud customers will be able to buy its frontier models and Codex agentic workflows through Model Garden, billed via Google Cloud Marketplace. Enterprises can apply existing Google Cloud spending commitments to the purchases. General availability is planned “in the coming weeks,” with access by request until then. OpenAI models will also come to Google’s Antigravity and Gemini Agent, while Microsoft remains OpenAI’s primary cloud partner.

Why it matters: OpenAI, Anthropic and Google models now sit on the same Google Cloud contract, which weakens single-cloud lock-in as an argument for any one model vendor. Firms running on Google Cloud can trial OpenAI models without a new procurement cycle. Vendor-risk teams should check that data-handling terms carry over, since the commercial contract runs through Google.
OpenAI disclosure

AI-found PHP flaws put advisor websites and client portals at risk as attacks begin

PHP shipped fixes on October 21 for two critical flaws, rated 9.2 of 10, that let attackers take over web servers without logging in. Security firms Calif, using OpenAI’s GPT-5.6 Sol, and Wiz found the bugs with frontier AI models. PHP runs about 70% of websites, including WordPress. Decoy servers recorded exploitation attempts the same day, and parts of Wikipedia and several government portals went offline as a precaution.

Why it matters: AI is now finding internet-scale flaws faster than many vendors patch them. Advisor websites, client logins, document upload forms and scheduling tools often run PHP through a vendor. Under Reg S-P, firms should ask each provider in writing whether it has patched, file the answers as vendor oversight, and check unpatched servers for compromise.
Tesla Q3 2026 update (SEC)

Tesla lifts R&D 69% to $2.76 billion and targets 400 MW of AI training compute

Tesla reported third-quarter revenue of $28.0 billion on October 21, flat from a year earlier, while R&D rose 69% to $2.76 billion. Tesla attributed the increase to its AI, Optimus and Cybercab work. Its update says Optimus production has begun on high-volume lines in Fremont, and Tesla plans to reach 400 MW of AI training compute by year-end. Capex was $4.7 billion, bringing the nine-month total to $13.0 billion against guidance of more than $25 billion for 2026.

Why it matters: Tesla is now valued and run partly as an AI-compute spender, so its margins depend on AI spending as well as car sales. Hitting the 2026 capex guide implies roughly $12 billion in the fourth quarter. PMs holding Tesla through index or growth sleeves should model it alongside AI infrastructure names, not only autos.
AI in Wealth Management
WealthManagement.com

$24 billion Visionary Square pays for FINNY’s AI prospecting across 140 partner firms

Hybrid RIA Visionary Square, with $24 billion in assets under supervision and more than 250 advisors across 140 partner firms, said on October 22 it will cover FINNY subscription costs for its partner firms. FINNY uses AI and proprietary data to match advisors with prospects by niche, similarity to existing clients and money-in-motion signals. Its F-Score ranks the people most likely to fit. FINNY raised a $17 million Series A led by Venrock last December.

Why it matters: Platforms are starting to fund AI growth tools centrally as a recruiting and retention benefit, not just productivity software. Advisors comparing affiliation offers should ask which AI tools come paid. Note FINNY’s August pricing option, which takes a share of AUM from clients it finds, and check how that sits with your firm’s solicitation and fee-disclosure rules.
Stifel Q3 2026 release

Stifel CEO says advisers now use AI for meeting prep and follow-up

On Stifel’s October 21 call, CEO Ron Kruszewski said advisers use AI tools for meeting prep, meeting follow-up and analysis, and that the firm uses AI to settle trades. He described a minority stake in Meow Technologies, announced October 15, as giving Stifel “an AI platform” with family-office capabilities; Stifel Bank will white-label Meow’s treasury platform for venture clients from early 2027. Wealth management revenue hit a record $980.3 million, up 8%.

Why it matters: Stifel had been the AI skeptic among regional brokerages. It now describes the same meeting-prep use case that Morgan Stanley and LPL have rolled out, though it named no tools or adoption figures. Recruiters and advisers comparing firms should ask Stifel which vendor sits behind it and how widely it is deployed before counting it as a platform feature.
WealthManagement.com, RIA Edge

63% of RIAs use AI, but only one in 10 builds it into strategy

Panelists at WealthManagement.com’s RIA Edge conference in Dana Point, held October 19–21, cited surveys showing 63% of RIAs now use AI, double the 2024 level, but only one in 10 uses it as a fully integrated part of business strategy. Current uses are mostly notetaking, onboarding and research. Brookwood Investment Group CEO Kimberley Raimondo called failure to launch “a leadership issue,” and AdvisorEngine’s Tim Foley warned against pilots that do not fit existing workflows.

Why it matters: Most firms have bought point tools without an operating plan, which is where agents for CRM and investment management will stall. Firms should name an owner, agree with compliance on risk limits, and choose the two or three workflows to change before adding vendors. Acquirers doing diligence on RIAs should ask for that plan.

Wall Street Journal

Anthropic’s draft S-1 shows two customers supply about 20% of revenue

A draft Anthropic prospectus reviewed by The Wall Street Journal, reported October 20, shows first-half 2026 revenue of $16.3 billion, up from $1.1 billion, and a net loss of $80.7 billion, about $72 billion of it non-cash charges tied to convertible financing. About 20% of revenue came from two customers. Anthropic has about $450 billion of mostly non-cancellable cloud contracts, including $111 billion with Google Cloud and $110 billion with AWS.

Why it matters: The customer concentration and fixed compute commitments are the key IPO risks, more than the headline loss, which is mostly accounting. PMs sizing an allocation should model revenue if either large customer shifts spending. The Google and AWS commitments also give visibility into hyperscaler backlog for holders of Alphabet and Amazon.
AI Street

Apollo’s Redding Ridge cuts CLO portfolio builds from days to minutes with agents

Jack McDonald, COO of Apollo’s roughly $50 billion CLO manager Redding Ridge Capital, told the AI Engineer conference in New York that Rowspace agents now draft proposed portfolios of 200–400 credits in minutes, 80–90% complete, against two to three days in Excel before. A weekly trade and settlement reconciliation that took two or three people several hours is now checked daily. He said the gain came from data plumbing, not chat.

Why it matters: This is one of the clearest production numbers yet from private credit, and it comes from data infrastructure work, not a chatbot. Managers should budget for consolidating conflicting data feeds before buying agents. Allocators doing operational due diligence should ask about error rates and audit trails, which the talk did not cover.
AI Street

Citadel Securities and Bridgewater detail in-house AI agents at AI Engineer

At the AI Engineer conference in New York, reported October 20, Citadel Securities’ COO for AI João Wenzel described a “world model” built on three years of project records. In a backtest, 18 of 100 green projects it flagged turned yellow or red within three months, against 10 of 100 unflagged. Bridgewater’s AIA Labs is building agents that propose research questions from Vault, its archive of more than 100 million documents from 135 sources.

Why it matters: Large funds are aiming agents at management oversight and research agendas, not just code and summaries. Their advantage comes from proprietary archives that rivals cannot buy. Managers without that data depth should focus on organizing internal research notes and decision records now, since that archive determines how useful agents become later.
