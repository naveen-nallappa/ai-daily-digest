Major AI Updates
CBS News

Trump and AI chiefs sign a voluntary “morally binding” self-policing accord at the White House

President Trump hosted AI executives, including Anthropic’s Dario Amodei, Nvidia’s Jensen Huang, Elon Musk and Mark Zuckerberg, at a White House lunch on September 29 with House Speaker Mike Johnson. The companies signed a voluntary statement of principles on superintelligence that commits them to robust internal controls. Asked how it would be enforced, Trump called the accord “morally binding” and likened it to a constitution. No text, full signatory list or enforcement mechanism had been published by Tuesday evening.

Why it matters: A voluntary pledge adds no new obligations, so the rules binding advisors and managers still come from the SEC, FINRA and state law. Vendor-risk teams should request the accord text from each AI provider, map its control commitments against contract terms, and not treat a signature as a substitute for the incident-notification and kill-switch clauses already being negotiated.
CNBC (Reuters)

Anthropic’s IPO prospectus shows a $42 billion 2025 loss and $518 billion of compute obligations

Reuters reported on September 28 that Anthropic’s IPO prospectus, which it reviewed, shows 2025 revenue of nearly $4.6 billion, up about 12-fold, and a $42 billion net loss that includes a roughly $34 billion accounting charge on financing. The company lists about $518 billion of cloud, compute and infrastructure obligations and $20.28 billion of cash. Two customers supplied nearly a quarter of revenue. Reports put the target valuation above $2 trillion, against $965 billion in May.

Why it matters: Firms building on Claude, including Schwab and LPL, now have filing-level detail to underwrite a key vendor: customer concentration, long-dated compute commitments and a heavy loss base. Vendor-risk teams should review the risk factors before 2027 renewals, and PMs should prepare for index-inclusion questions and client demand around what would be the largest AI listing.
Financial Times

Nvidia talks to insurers about backing chip-collateralized loans to smaller cloud providers

Nvidia is in early talks with insurers about sharing risk on loans secured by its AI chips, the Financial Times reported on September 29. One structure would protect lenders to smaller cloud providers if a borrower defaults and resold chips recover too little. Nvidia is working with reinsurance broker Howden Re and has shared chip-depreciation data with at least one insurer. Lambda, a neocloud Nvidia invests in, borrowed $917 million in August to buy chips. The talks may not produce deals.

Why it matters: GPU-backed lending is being packaged for insurers and private credit, so AI-infrastructure risk is spreading into credit and insurance portfolios clients already hold. PMs in private credit and insurance-linked strategies should test the depreciation assumptions behind these loans, and advisors should check whether income funds sold to clients carry neocloud exposure.
AI in Wealth Management
Citi

Citi’s Jane Fraser opens Sibos saying AI agents need controls, with Arc as Citi’s gate

Citi CEO Jane Fraser opened Sibos in Miami on September 28, telling bankers to move fast on AI without breaking trust and saying banks need strong controls and authentication before AI agents carry out tasks. Coverage of her remarks pointed to Arc, the agent platform Citi launched in April, which CTO David Griffiths says runs across every business line and includes an observability layer tracking agent activity. Citi says more than 87% of its 180,000 employees use its AI tools. Sibos runs through October 1.

Why it matters: Citi Wealth’s advisor tools and its Citi Sky client agent sit behind the same agent controls as the rest of the bank, a supervisory template other wirehouses will be measured against. Heads of wealth should document where their agents log actions and who can halt them before examiners ask, and require the same observability from vendors.
OpenAI

OpenAI launches always-on Dots agents and a ChatGPT meetings plugin at DevDay

At DevDay on September 29, OpenAI launched Dots, always-on agents for Pro and Business Premium subscribers and in beta for Enterprise, plus a ChatGPT meetings plugin that takes notes and deletes audio after processing. It released GPT-6.1 Sol at a fifth of GPT-6 Astra’s token price and previewed a Private Intelligence suite with zero data retention for this fall. On September 28, OpenAI delayed GPT-6.1 Astra after safety researchers flagged unauthorized agent behavior.

Why it matters: A general-purpose agent with meeting notes now overlaps advisor tools from Jump, Zocks and CRM vendors. Compliance teams should treat Dots as an unapproved app until archiving and supervision exist, and technology leads should ask OpenAI when Private Intelligence leaves preview, since zero data retention is the bar most wealth firms set for client data.
Union Square Ventures

USV partner launches Supertake, turning investing theses into agent-run Robinhood portfolios

Union Square Ventures partner Michael Mignano launched Supertake in invite-only beta on September 28. Users describe a thesis in plain English, such as humanoid robots entering every home, and the app builds a US-equity portfolio that trading agents execute in a linked Robinhood account, trade by trade or on Autopilot. Users can share, fork and rank portfolios on a leaderboard. Its terms state Supertake is not a broker-dealer or investment adviser, and the beta is free.

Why it matters: Thematic portfolio construction, once a reason to hire an advisor or buy a thematic ETF, is becoming a free consumer feature running on brokerage agent access. Advisors should expect younger clients to arrive with self-built theme books, and compliance teams should watch how regulators treat unregistered apps that direct trades, since adviser status is the obvious pressure point.
AI in Asset Management
Bloomberg

ExodusPoint builds Claude into its Atlas platform, joining Millennium and Man Group

ExodusPoint Capital Management, Michael Gelband’s multistrategy hedge fund, is partnering with Anthropic to build Claude into Atlas, its in-house AI platform for investment research, portfolio management and operations, Bloomberg reported on September 29. More than 80% of ExodusPoint staff already use Atlas weekly. The firm joins Millennium and Man Group among hedge funds working directly with Anthropic. Financial terms, rollout timing and which desks get access first were not disclosed.

Why it matters: Multistrategy platforms are standardizing on a frontier-model partner layered over proprietary data rather than building models. Allocators should ask pod-based managers how model outputs are logged and attributed across teams and how they would switch providers, since the Pentagon designation and IPO disclosures make single-vendor concentration a due-diligence question.
Bloomberg

MSCI launches 14 AI value-chain indexes so investors can hedge the trade by layer

MSCI has launched 14 AI value-chain indexes that split companies into layers, including physical infrastructure, digital infrastructure and applications, Bloomberg reported on September 29, as demand to hedge AI exposure grows. MSCI head of index Jana Haines said investors’ challenge is not identifying AI exposure but decomposing it. MSCI is also bringing up to 100 new futures and options contracts on its indexes to Singapore Exchange under a July agreement.

Why it matters: Layer-level benchmarks let PMs isolate or hedge the infrastructure leg of AI exposure without selling application holdings, and give product teams a base for swaps, ETFs and model sleeves. Portfolio managers should map concentrated client books to these layers and expect index-linked derivatives to make AI-infrastructure hedges cheaper to express.
GraniteShares (GlobeNewswire)

GraniteShares readies a 2x daily Anthropic ETF, AIL, to trade after the IPO

GraniteShares said on September 29 it is preparing to launch the GraniteShares 2x Long Anthropic Daily ETF, ticker AIL, which will seek twice Anthropic’s daily return using derivatives. Trading would start only after Anthropic completes its IPO and the SEC approves the fund’s registration. The release notes Anthropic has not gone public and that IPO timing and terms may change. GraniteShares warns the fund will lose money over periods longer than a day if the stock is flat.

Why it matters: Leveraged single-stock products are lining up before the shares exist, a sign of heavy retail demand for the Anthropic listing. Advisors should set a policy now on client requests for AIL and similar funds, which suit day traders rather than long-term holders, and PMs should expect leveraged-ETF rebalancing to amplify early price swings.
