Anthropic newsroom

Anthropic commits 5% of its shares to a new Public Benefit Fund

Anthropic said on October 18 it will place 5% of its shares in the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, a wholly owned LLC, subject to stockholder approval. The board may add 2% more for every 50% rise in company value above a baseline it has not yet disclosed. Including co-founder pledges and the employee equity match, about 20% of shares would go to public-benefit uses. Fund equity cannot be used for lobbying or political giving, and governance details are due in the coming months.

Why it matters: For investors weighing a future Anthropic listing, this is a dilution and governance term to model: a share commitment that grows as the valuation rises. Advisors fielding client questions about pre-IPO or IPO allocations should ask how the fund will vote its shares and where the baseline is set, since both affect per-share value.
GeekWire

Microsoft adds live risk scores that can freeze or shut down rogue AI agents

Microsoft said on October 19 that its Project Perception security system will give each AI agent a continuously updated risk score, based on the data it can reach and whether it drifts from its assigned task, GeekWire reported. As risk rises, the system can remove write access, require human approval for high-stakes steps, or shut the agent down. Discovery now covers 60 agent types on employee computers, up from 25, including Claude Code and Codex. The features are in private preview ahead of Ignite on November 17–19.

Why it matters: Most large wealth and asset managers run on Microsoft 365, so agent kill switches may soon arrive in tools they already license. Compliance and technology teams should seek preview access or an Ignite briefing, inventory agents on advisor and analyst laptops, and map the human-approval step to existing supervisory procedures before agents touch client records.
SemiAnalysis

SemiAnalysis forecasts about 43 gigawatts of new data-center capacity in 2027

SemiAnalysis said on October 19 it expects about 43 gigawatts of new global data-center capacity to come online in 2027, with more than 24 GW across about 40 campuses already in advanced construction. Binding, non-cancellable leases signed so far in 2026 total just under $1 trillion, and pre-leases typically run 15 to 20 years. Altimeter’s Brad Gerstner called the forecast too aggressive and put 2027 at about 25 GW. SemiAnalysis expects US grid headroom to turn negative by late 2027.

Why it matters: Long, non-cancellable leases fix hyperscaler and neocloud obligations even if AI demand slows, which is why credit markets are pricing AI infrastructure risk. PMs should treat the gap between 25 and 43 GW as the swing factor for utilities, power equipment and data-center REITs, and test how much expected earnings growth rests on the higher number.
AI in Wealth Management
WealthManagement.com

Trust & Will launches a Nevada-chartered trust company run by software agents

Online estate-planning provider Trust & Will is launching the Trust & Will Trust Company, a Nevada-chartered retail trust company that can act as a professional corporate trustee, WealthManagement.com reported on October 16. It targets households under $10 million, has no account minimum, and charges fees based on account value and asset type. Recordkeeping, trust tax filings, distributions and beneficiary reporting will be delivered by software agents backed by a human fiduciary. The initial rollout is mostly direct to consumers, with no launch date given.

Why it matters: Agent-run administration could bring corporate trustee services to the mass affluent, where traditional trust companies set minimums in the tens of millions. Advisors can add it to their trustee menu for smaller trusts, but should check how distribution decisions get human review, who bears liability for agent errors, and whether data flows back to the advising RIA.
FINRA Regulatory Policy Agenda

FINRA puts AI guidance on its fourth-quarter agenda after a September roundtable

FINRA’s October 2026 regulatory policy agenda, published October 12, lists guidance on using AI tools under its rules, with timing set for “Q4 2026 and following.” The item follows a September roundtable, and FINRA says it is still collecting input from firms and other stakeholders. The agenda names no rule numbers and does not single out generative AI or agents. The same agenda holds e-delivery as the default for customer communications until the SEC acts on Regulation E-Delivery, proposed July 16.

Why it matters: Guidance, not a new rule, is the likely first step, and it will shape exam expectations for 2027. Broker-dealers should map each AI tool to the rules it touches, such as communications, supervision and books and records, and document that inventory now. Firms with views on agentic trading or AI notetakers still have time to weigh in before FINRA drafts.
Financial Planning

One advisor estimates nine in 10 new prospects consult AI before meeting him

Financial Planning asked nine independent advisors on October 16 how they handle clients who cite ChatGPT. James Norris Eppes of Eppes Wealth Management estimated that nine in 10 new prospects have already asked AI about their finances, and said AI sometimes sends business his way. James Mayo of IronFjord Wealth discusses AI-sourced ideas but will not verify AI-generated plans or math. Juan Hernandez-Ariano of WealthCreate cited Roth versus pre-tax 401(k) choices for borrowers on income-driven loan plans as a case generic answers miss.

Why it matters: Clients now arrive with a chatbot second opinion, so recommendations will be stress-tested before and after meetings. Firms should give advisors a standard approach: welcome the research, explain which client-specific facts change the answer, and keep a file note. Compliance should decide whether reviewing a client’s AI output is advice that needs documenting.
AI in Asset Management
OpenAI customer story

Benefit Street Partners uses Codex to give 300-page credit agreements a 20-minute first pass

Benefit Street Partners, Franklin Templeton’s credit manager, described its use of OpenAI’s Codex in a case study published October 16. Portfolio manager Anant Kumar built a tracker that uses public SEC filings to compare how business development companies mark the same loan, in about 10 to 12 hours of work. A credit agreement analyzer gives a first pass on documents often over 300 pages in about 20 minutes, followed by an hour or more of human review. The analyzer now runs on GPT-6 Astra in a company-hosted portal.

Why it matters: Comparing BDC marks on the same loan is a direct read on valuation dispersion in private credit, a live concern as AI-related debt reprices. Allocators should ask private credit managers whether they run similar mark comparisons and how AI-flagged covenant terms reach investment committees. The hour of human review shows the tool speeds analysis rather than replacing credit judgment.
SiliconANGLE

Tenstorrent raises $1.5 billion at $6.2 billion with Fidelity, T. Rowe and Morgan Stanley

AI chip startup Tenstorrent raised a $1.5 billion Series E at a $6.2 billion valuation on October 19, SiliconANGLE reported, up from $2.6 billion after its December 2024 Series D. Trace Capital and Innovation X led the round, with Fidelity Management & Research, T. Rowe Price, ARK Invest, Morgan Stanley and Protagonist participating. The company, led by chip designer Jim Keller, will use the money to ramp production of its Galaxy servers, fund RISC-V research and hire. Individual commitments were not disclosed.

Why it matters: Large mutual fund managers are still buying private AI hardware stakes after the chip index’s eight-session losing streak. Advisors holding Fidelity or T. Rowe growth funds should check private-company weights and how those positions are marked, since a late-stage round at a higher price can lift reported values while public chip peers fall.
Defiance ETFs press release

Defiance relaunches a momentum ETF that uses a language model to score news

Defiance ETFs renamed the QRAFT AI-Enhanced U.S. Large Cap Momentum ETF as the Defiance LLM Catalyst Momentum ETF on October 19, changing its NYSE Arca ticker from AMOM to AIED. The actively managed fund holds about 50 large-cap US stocks. It pairs a price-momentum screen with a large language model that reads company-specific news in real time and flags unusually positive or negative developments. It charges 0.75% and refreshes its data every four weeks. Assets were not disclosed.

Why it matters: LLM news signals are now a retail ETF selling point, so clients will ask about them. Before using such funds, advisors should ask how the signal was tested out of sample, how turnover affects taxes, and whether a 0.75% fee is justified against plain momentum ETFs that cost a fraction of that.
