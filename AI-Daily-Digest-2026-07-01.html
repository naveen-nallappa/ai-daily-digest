Qualcomm to Buy AI-Software Startup Modular for $3.9 Billion in Data-Center Push

Qualcomm agreed on June 24 to acquire AI-software startup Modular in an all-stock deal valued at about $3.92 billion, issuing up to 19.2 million shares and targeting a close in the second half of 2026. Modular's vendor-neutral software lets AI models run across Nvidia, AMD, and other chips without rewriting code, positioning Qualcomm against Nvidia's CUDA moat. Separately, Tenstorrent CEO Jim Keller on June 30 denied reports of an $8–10 billion Qualcomm takeover.

Why it matters: Qualcomm buying the software layer, not just silicon, signals the AI-accelerator fight is shifting to developer tooling and portability. PMs holding semiconductor names should watch whether CUDA alternatives gain real traction, since a credible cross-vendor stack would compress Nvidia's pricing power and reshape the margin assumptions underpinning today's chip valuations.

Cerebras Books $20 Billion, 750-Megawatt Multi-Year Compute Deal With OpenAI

Cerebras disclosed on June 23 a multi-year agreement to supply OpenAI 750 megawatts of AI inference capacity valued at more than $20 billion, running 250 megawatts a year through 2028 with an option for an additional 1.25 gigawatts by 2030. OpenAI also lent Cerebras $1 billion in working capital and holds warrants tied to the contract. OpenAI plans to run its GPT-5.6 Sol model on Cerebras at up to 750 tokens per second starting in July.

Why it matters: The deal underlines that inference capacity, not just training, is now the binding constraint on frontier AI, and that model makers are financing their own suppliers. Allocators underwriting the compute supercycle should scrutinize these circular vendor-financing structures, where a customer's loans and warrants prop up the very supplier booking its multibillion-dollar revenue.

Why it matters: A rocket company monetizing data-center capacity shows how far the compute land-grab has spread beyond incumbent clouds. For investors, the signal is that AI-infrastructure demand is deep enough to support new entrants on both sides of the trade, reinforcing multi-year revenue visibility for power, chips, and data-center real estate underlying many portfolios.
