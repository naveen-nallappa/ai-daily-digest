Major AI Updates
Meta (company blog)

Meta launches Muse Wonder, its first frontier-class model from Superintelligence Labs

Meta released Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full on October 12, its most capable models for personal and professional agent tasks, about 14 months after rebuilding its AI unit. Muse Wonder is available now through the Meta Model API and Muse Code; Wonder Full is limited to Muse Code subscribers. Meta claims state-of-the-art results on Harvey’s Legal Agent Benchmark and SWE-Marathon, and Decrypt reported API pricing of $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens.

Why it matters: A fourth US lab now sells frontier-tier models at the same list price as Anthropic’s top tier. Firms negotiating enterprise AI renewals have a credible alternative to quote, but should wait for Meta’s promised safety report and independent finance benchmarks before routing client data or advice workflows to it.
Microsoft Azure (company blog)

Microsoft unveils Maia 300 chip as its primary inference system from 2027

Microsoft detailed its Azure Maia 300 accelerator on October 12, built on TSMC’s N2P process with 288GB of HBM4 memory, 2.5 times the memory bandwidth of Maia 200. Microsoft claims up to seven times Maia 200’s rack-level token throughput and more than 2.5 times the performance per watt. Cloud and AI chief Scott Guthrie said the Maia family will scale to multi-gigawatt deployments starting next year, with Maia 300 deploying in 2027. Maia 200 already runs Copilot and OpenAI models.

Why it matters: A hyperscaler naming its own chip as the primary inference platform is a direct revenue risk for Nvidia’s inference business from 2027. PMs overweight AI semiconductors should test how much of their thesis rests on inference share, and favor names with custom-silicon exposure such as foundry, memory and packaging suppliers.
DatacenterDynamics, citing Nikkei

NTT Data nears a $6.3 billion deal to build Anthropic’s first Japan data center

NTT Data is in final-stage talks to build a data center of up to 500 megawatts in Osaka Prefecture for Anthropic, worth up to ¥1 trillion ($6.3 billion), Nikkei reported on October 13. Signing is targeted by March 31, with partial operations from 2028. Neither company commented. Separately, Anthropic hosts selected institutional investors at its San Francisco headquarters on October 14, Bloomberg reported, ahead of an IPO that could trade before Thanksgiving. No public S-1 has been filed.

Why it matters: Anthropic is locking in long-dated capacity before its IPO, which will make compute obligations a central diligence item. Advisors fielding client requests for IPO allocations should set expectations now: likely heavy institutional demand, limited retail allocation, and a prospectus whose compute commitments deserve as much attention as revenue growth.
Forbes

Character.AI files for Chapter 11 with Disney as $20 million opening bidder

Character.AI filed for voluntary Chapter 11 protection in Delaware on October 12 to run an asset sale, Forbes reported. Disney is the stalking-horse bidder at $20 million for a non-exclusive perpetual license to some intellectual property, and other bidders can top it. The sale is expected to close between November and December. The app keeps running for now but could shut down if no acceptable buyer emerges. Disney hired former Character.AI CEO Karandeep Anand as its CTO in September.

Why it matters: Consumer AI apps without their own models or distribution can fail fast despite large user bases. Venture and growth investors, and advisors placing clients in private AI funds, should check how much portfolio value sits in application-layer companies that depend on rented models and face copyright exposure.
AI in Wealth Management
Wells Fargo Q3 2026 earnings release (SEC 8-K)

Wells Fargo says its new advisor AI tool is the first of several for wealth

CEO Charlie Scharf said on Wells Fargo’s third-quarter call on October 13 that the bank “just launched an AI-powered tool for our financial advisors that streamlines routine work,” the first of several planned capabilities in Wealth and Investment Management. He also cited new portfolio analysis and proposal tools built on BlackRock’s Aladdin Wealth. Wealth revenue rose 12% to $4.1 billion and client assets 11% to $2.5 trillion. Scharf said agents built at scale give big banks an underappreciated advantage.

Why it matters: Wells is signaling a sequenced advisor-AI roadmap rather than one tool. Recruiters competing for Wells advisors should expect AI capability to feature in retention pitches, and FiNet independents on the platform should ask which upcoming capabilities extend to their channel and on what timeline.
JPMorgan Chase Q3 2026 earnings release (SEC 8-K)

Dimon says JPMorgan will not let “just any outside agent” into the bank

Jamie Dimon said on JPMorgan’s third-quarter call on October 13 that the bank uses agents internally but is “not going to allow just any outside agent to come inside the company,” citing security and liability when customers share credentials. CFO Jeremy Barnum said AI benefits are not yet visible in quarterly earnings and that some will be offset by token costs. Asset and wealth management revenue rose 14% to $6.9 billion, with client assets up 15% to $7.9 trillion.

Why it matters: The largest US bank is drawing a hard line on third-party agents just as fintechs and AI labs launch personal agents that move money. Advisors and fintechs building agent workflows that touch JPMorgan accounts should plan on approved APIs, not credential sharing, and expect other incumbents to follow.
Reuters

Fed and two other US regulators question banks on AI agents and Chinese models

Three top US bank regulators, including the Federal Reserve, are asking banks whether they use China-developed AI technology, whether they use AI agents for cybersecurity, and whether they can rein in autonomous systems, Reuters reported on October 13, citing sources. The Bank of England and the European Central Bank are pushing banks to strengthen recovery plans. The scrutiny follows CrowdStrike’s finding that a China-based actor used a Chinese AI agent and Claude Code to hack South Korean banks.

Why it matters: Supervisors are moving from model-risk theory to specific exam questions. Bank-owned wealth units and their vendors should be ready to show an inventory of AI models by origin, a named owner for each agent, and a tested kill switch, since state examiners already say an inventory alone is no longer enough.
AI in Asset Management
BlackRock Investment Institute

BlackRock cuts US stocks to neutral but keeps AI as its preferred equity theme

The BlackRock Investment Institute cut US equities to neutral from overweight on October 12, calling it a short-term risk-management move that partly funds an upgrade of Treasuries. Short-term Treasuries went to overweight and long-term Treasuries to neutral, the first change from underweight since October 2020, with the 10-year yield near 5.25%. BlackRock said its conviction in AI fundamentals remains intact and it still prefers AI-related companies and listed infrastructure within US stocks.

Why it matters: The largest asset manager is trimming US equity risk without abandoning AI. Advisors running BlackRock models should expect rebalancing toward Treasuries, and PMs can read the call as a rotation within equities toward AI infrastructure rather than a broad exit from the AI trade.
InvestmentNews

IMF says 14 AI deals carry $650 billion of debt against uncertain revenue

The IMF’s Global Financial Stability Report, released October 13 in Bangkok, found that 14 disclosed deals since 2023 sent about $300 billion of equity and $650 billion of debt into financially weaker AI firms in exchange for $1.4 trillion of future revenue commitments. Hyperscalers’ share of global investment-grade issuance rose to 10% from under 1% since 2024. IMF official Jason Wu warned of a “triple whammy” if those commitments fail. Hedge funds now hold 9% of US Treasuries.

Why it matters: AI credit risk is now concentrated enough to show up in official stability reports. Fixed-income PMs should check index weight in hyperscaler and AI-lab-linked bonds, and allocators should ask private-credit managers how much of their book depends on AI revenue commitments that the IMF calls uncertain.
Business Insider

Two Sigma says AI let 82 interns finish core projects weeks early

Most of Two Sigma’s 82 summer interns finished their core projects weeks earlier than past classes and moved on to stretch goals, head of talent acquisition Evan Anger told Business Insider on October 12. The $80 billion quant firm added training on large language models led by senior leaders. Because AI has narrowed gaps in coding and math, Two Sigma now weighs judgment and communication more heavily, and 85% of eligible interns received full-time offers.

Why it matters: A top quant shop is changing what it hires for, not just how fast juniors work. Asset managers and research teams should update analyst interviews to test whether candidates can explain and check AI-built work, since Anger warned that output a hire cannot explain is a liability.
