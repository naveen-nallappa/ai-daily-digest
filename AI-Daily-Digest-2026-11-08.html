Major AI Updates
AJU Press

Kospi falls 6% and the Nikkei enters a bear market as the AI selloff hits Asia

South Korea’s Kospi closed 6.02% lower at 5,059.69 on November 6 after an opening drop of more than 8% triggered a 20-minute trading halt, AJU Press reported. SK hynix fell 9.97% and Samsung Electronics 6.4%, and foreign investors sold 2.77 trillion won ($2.04 billion). Japan’s Nikkei 225 lost 5.96% to 57,434.77, more than 20% below its June peak. The Kospi’s two-session loss reached nearly 11%.

Why it matters: Samsung and SK hynix sit near the top of most emerging-market and global semiconductor funds, so the AI drawdown is now an international-allocation problem, not only a Nasdaq one. Advisors should check how much client emerging-market and Japan exposure overlaps with US AI holdings. Seoul’s three-month margin-collateral waiver for brokers signals worry about forced selling.
Reuters (via GV Wire)

OpenAI closes $11 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation, below its first ask

OpenAI has closed $11 billion of new funding, the first tranche of a round that values it at $1.25 trillion, Bloomberg News reported on November 6, according to Reuters. Reports citing the Wall Street Journal said OpenAI had first sought a $1.4 trillion valuation. The close came a day after the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5% and the Philadelphia semiconductor index 12.25%, and as Anthropic prepares to market its IPO.

Why it matters: A lower private mark for the sector leader resets the reference point for every late-stage AI holding. Advisors with clients in private AI feeder funds, tender-offer vehicles or interval funds should expect quarter-end marks to drift down, and should check whether managers price these stakes off the latest round or off public comparables.
METR

METR finds Anthropic’s AI research progress has run 1.9 times faster since October 2025

METR, an independent evaluator, published a November 5 assessment of AI-driven research acceleration at Anthropic after a review that ran from August 12 to October 29. It estimates Anthropic’s internal capability index has grown 1.9 times faster since October 2025 (95% confidence interval 1.5 to 2.8 times) and guesses about 80% of that is due to AI. Anthropic redacted a majority of the evidence METR found most informative, citing competitive concerns.

Why it matters: This is the first outside estimate that a frontier lab’s own AI is speeding up its model development, and it lands as Anthropic markets its IPO. Analysts valuing AI labs should treat faster model cycles as both a growth driver and a depreciation risk for compute. Heavy redactions also mean investors cannot test the claim themselves.
AI in Wealth Management
BNN Bloomberg (Canadian Press)

Wealthsimple launches Penny, an AI assistant that can move money and place trades

Wealthsimple unveiled Penny at its Toronto product event on November 5, one of a dozen launches. The assistant can cue e-transfers, flag duplicate card charges, pay invoices, answer RRSP questions and buy or sell stocks and ETFs, but acts only after the client confirms. From early 2027 clients will be able to connect accounts to ChatGPT or Claude at access levels they set. Wealthsimple has more than four million users and C$169.2 billion in assets.

Why it matters: Penny joins Robinhood’s agents as a retail platform letting AI execute, not just advise, behind a confirmation step. Advisors serving younger clients should expect them to compare advice with an assistant that trades in two taps. Compliance teams should watch how Canadian regulators treat confirmed AI-initiated trades, a likely template for US suitability questions.
Wealthbox

Wealthbox puts 24 pre-built AI agents on every CRM plan

Wealthbox said on November 4 that AI Agents are now included on all of its CRM plans, starting with 24 pre-built agents such as Daily Planning, Follow-Up Automation, Client Re-engagement for clients not contacted in 90 days, Sentiment Analysis and an Upcoming RMD Screen. Agents run on a schedule or when CRM data changes, read stored PDFs, spreadsheets and scans, and ask for approval before changing records. Admins get access first, and a training webinar runs November 12.

Why it matters: Agentic CRM automation is becoming a standard feature for small RIAs, not a paid add-on. Firms on Wealthbox should decide which agents may write to client records and log approvals for books-and-records purposes. Firms paying separately for notetaker follow-up workflows should check where Wealthbox now duplicates them before renewals.
Arch

Mercer Advisors says Arch’s AI cut its alternatives operating costs by 40%

Mercer Advisors, with $114 billion in client assets, signed a multiyear expansion with Arch, an AI-powered operating system for private investments, Arch said on November 3. Since early 2025 Arch has saved Mercer more than 7,000 hours a year and cut the cost of running client alternatives by 40%, the vendor said. Arch collects capital-call, distribution and K-1 documents, sends K-1s to CPAs and feeds data into Mercer’s Aspen Investing platform.

Why it matters: Alternatives paperwork, not portfolio construction, is often what stops RIAs from scaling private-markets allocations. A 40% cost cut at a $114 billion firm gives other RIAs a benchmark for vendor talks. Operations heads should ask how K-1 timing and capital-call errors are tracked, since these figures are vendor-reported and unaudited.
Financial Planning

48% of advisors using AI say admin work fell, but most did not cut their hours

Of advisors who use AI, 48% say their administrative workload has fallen, but about seven in ten say they have not worked fewer hours, according to the Financial Advisor Confidence Outlook survey reported by Financial Planning on November 5. Advisors put the saved time into professional development (29%), existing clients (28%) and deeper planning (28%), and 31% say they now serve more households. Advisors remain less likely to use AI for planning itself.

Why it matters: The data supports framing AI as capacity, not cost: time saved is going to clients and growth rather than shorter weeks. Practice leaders should track households per advisor and planning hours, not just hours saved, when judging AI vendors. The planning gap also shows where advisors still want human judgment and stronger data controls.
AI in Asset Management
WisdomTree (SEC 8-K)

WisdomTree says AI saves 1,500 hours a month and powers a market-beating strategy

WisdomTree President Jarrett Lilien said on the October 30 third-quarter call that the firm has tracked more than 1,500 hours saved per month through AI, equal to at least 10 full-time employees, and that the effort is not a headcount-reduction program. CEO Jonathan Steinberg said one AI-driven long-short strategy has beaten the market by more than 900 basis points this year. Will Peck, head of WisdomTree OnChain, tied tokenized funds to AI agents handling more transactions.

Why it matters: Mid-size ETF sponsors are now quantifying AI savings on earnings calls, giving investors a productivity measure to set against fee pressure. Analysts covering asset managers should ask peers for comparable hours-saved or expense figures. Fund selectors should ask for audited live track records before allocating to AI long-short strategies that cite a single year of outperformance.
Hedgeweek

Tiger Global, Whale Rock and Atreides lose 8% to 10% in October’s AI selloff

Tiger Global’s flagship hedge fund fell 8.5% in October and is roughly flat for the year, Hedgeweek reported on November 6, citing Bloomberg. Whale Rock’s flagship lost about 8% but is up about 34% this year, and Atreides’ $3.4 billion flagship fell 9.5%, leaving it up about 43%. Dan Sundheim’s D1 lost 4.4% and is up 18.8%, while Coatue gained 0.2% and is up 13% for the year.

Why it matters: Tech-focused long-short funds gave back months of gains in a single month. Allocators should compare October drawdowns with each manager’s stated net exposure to see whose hedges worked. Advisors using these funds through feeder platforms should check redemption gates and notice periods before year-end liquidity requests arrive, especially for clients who bought after the summer rally.
Bloomberg

Anthropic IPO buyers discuss $1.1 to $1.3 trillion, below the $1.5 trillion target

Potential investors in Anthropic’s IPO are discussing a valuation of roughly $1.2 trillion to $1.3 trillion, with some as low as $1.1 trillion, Bloomberg reported on November 6; no decision has been made. The Wall Street Journal had reported a $1.5 trillion target, and three large investors told the paper the price would likely need to fall. Bloomberg earlier reported the roadshow could start the week of November 9, aiming for a listing before Thanksgiving.

Why it matters: Long-only managers and crossover funds hold the pricing power on the largest AI listing, and their pushback will set marks for private AI holdings across the industry. PMs should settle allocation limits before books open. Advisors fielding client requests for IPO shares should set expectations that price, timing and allocation size may all change.
