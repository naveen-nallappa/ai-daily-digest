Trump Says US Will Explore Taking Equity Stakes in AI Companies

President Trump said on June 5 that his team will "look into" the US government taking equity stakes in leading AI developers, and that he plans to discuss the idea directly with AI executives. The approach would extend the precedent set by Washington's 10% stake in Intel. One option under consideration would have companies hand over shares voluntarily, with earnings potentially funding dividend payments to American households. Sam Altman first pitched an equity-sharing arrangement to Trump in early 2025.

Why it matters: Government ownership talk lands days before OpenAI and Anthropic price the largest AI listings ever attempted, injecting a policy variable into their valuation math. PMs holding AI mega-caps should price in the governance overhang a sovereign shareholder creates; advisors fielding pre-IPO questions should note that dilution and dividend mechanics remain entirely unresolved.

OpenAI Rolls Out "Dreaming V3" Memory Overhaul to ChatGPT

OpenAI began rolling out its Dreaming V3 memory architecture to US ChatGPT Plus and Pro users on June 4, with Free-tier access expected within weeks. Instead of waiting for explicit "remember this" commands, the system runs background synthesis after conversations, automatically cataloguing preferences, projects and time-sensitive context — and retiring stale facts. OpenAI cut the compute cost of memory synthesis roughly 5x, making free-tier memory economically viable, while paid tiers get double the storage. Why it matters: Persistent, self-updating memory is the feature that turns chatbots into client-facing assistants that "know" a household — exactly the capability wealth platforms are building toward. Compliance teams evaluating AI tools should add memory governance — what is retained, where, and for how long — to vendor diligence now, before profile-building features arrive embedded in advisor tech.

Arizona Utility Proposes 45% Electricity Surcharge on AI Data Centers

Arizona Public Service, the state's largest electric utility, has proposed a 45% rate increase targeted specifically at data centers, arguing AI infrastructure operators should pay their "fair share" of grid expansion costs, the Wall Street Journal reported. The proposal would rank among the largest single-sector utility rate increases in US history. Arizona, Texas and Virginia have all experienced grid stress tied to accelerating data-center construction over 2025–2026, and other state utilities are expected to study similar surcharges.

Why it matters: Power pricing is becoming the swing variable in AI unit economics, and sector-specific surcharges would compress margins for hyperscalers while boosting regulated-utility revenue. PMs should stress-test data-center and AI-capex exposure against rising power tariffs, and watch operators with owned generation — nuclear restarts, SMR contracts — as the relative winners if surcharges spread.

Study Finds Three in Five Investors Reject AI-Driven Investment Advice

Corporate Insight's AI in Financial Services study, released June 2 and built on a 2,000-person February survey plus testing across 21 verticals, finds 74% of consumers use AI yet nearly three in five don't want it involved in investment decisions. Schwab's Portfolio Insights is cited as the first client-facing generative AI feature at scale among traditional brokerages, while fintechs Origin, Composer, Public and eToro already offer AI-driven recommendations and semi-autonomous execution. Why it matters: The study identifies three concrete trust levers — disclosing when AI is used, an easy path to a human, and human review of AI output — that should shape any client-facing rollout. Why it matters: RACK gives advisors a single-ticket, picks-and-shovels alternative for clients who want AI exposure without adding to crowded mega-cap positions. PMs comparing it against broad AI thematic funds should note the 50% revenue-purity screen, which skews holdings toward industrials and energy — a meaningfully different factor profile that pairs with, rather than duplicates, existing tech sleeves.
