NVIDIA Newsroom

Nvidia loses about $190 billion in value as its board investigates Jensen Huang

Nvidia’s board said on November 8 that a committee of independent directors, with outside counsel, will investigate after reports that CEO Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. The board said it has no reason to believe national-security or company secrets were compromised. Nvidia shares closed 4.3% lower on November 9, erasing about $190 billion of market value, Reuters reported. Shareholders led by Mark Kistenmacher filed a proposed class action in federal court in San Jose the same day.

Why it matters: Nvidia is the largest weight in most US equity and technology indexes, so a key-person and governance probe now sits inside nearly every client portfolio. Advisors should check combined exposure for clients who hold Nvidia directly alongside S&P 500, Nasdaq-100 and semiconductor funds. PMs should watch the inquiry’s scope, since the new lawsuit centers on what investors were told.
Anthropic

Anthropic releases Claude Fable 5.2 at $8 per million input tokens

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 on November 9. They share one underlying model: Fable is open to all users with cyber and biology safeguards, while Mythos is limited to verified cyber and life-sciences programs. Pricing is $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output, with a faster mode at double. Anthropic reported 89.3% on OSWorld 2.1, against 87.2% for Opus 5.5, and named Bridgewater, Salesforce and Stripe as private-beta users of its Enterprise Frontier Safeguards.

Why it matters: Fable 5.2 arrives as Anthropic prepares its IPO roadshow, giving investors a fresh read on product cadence. Firms piloting Claude for research or advisor tools should rerun cost models: at $40 per million output tokens, agent-heavy workflows get expensive fast, and Anthropic still recommends Opus 5.5 as the default. Bridgewater’s beta shows hedge funds testing tighter controls on frontier models.
Financial Times

Anthropic commits about $3.4 billion for 1.7 gigawatts of Caterpillar gas generators

Anthropic agreed in late September to pay about $3.4 billion to take over OnePWR Solutions’ orders for gas generators totaling 1.7 gigawatts, placed with Caterpillar roughly two years earlier, the Financial Times and CNBC reported on November 8. Anthropic bought the orders and their place in Caterpillar’s production queue, not OnePWR itself. Piper Sandler described the deal as a $3.4 billion sale of OnePWR’s power-generation platform. Neither company had confirmed it.

Why it matters: AI labs are now buying their way to the front of the power-equipment queue, which supports order books at generator and turbine makers even as chip stocks sell off. PMs should treat on-site gas power as an AI-capex beneficiary distinct from semiconductors. The deal also adds to the spending commitments Anthropic’s IPO investors must underwrite.
AI in Wealth Management
Reuters (via The Standard)

UBS CEO Sergio Ermotti says AI investors were pricing perfection before the correction

UBS Chief Executive Sergio Ermotti said on November 9 that AI investors and markets had been pricing perfection for technology stocks until the sharp correction of the past few weeks, Reuters reported. Speaking at a UBS conference in Sydney, he said US midterm election results and the global debate over AI’s future could delay infrastructure delivery, especially data centers. His remarks came days after the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5% on November 5.

Why it matters: The head of the world’s largest wealth manager is telling clients the AI trade was priced for no setbacks. Advisors should expect UBS and peers to tilt house views toward diversifying away from AI concentration, and should prepare their own rebalancing conversations. Data-center delays would also hit the infrastructure and utility holdings many clients bought as a safer AI play.
WealthManagement.com

Only 42 of the 455 largest RIAs have named an AI or innovation leader

Only 42 of the 455 largest independent RIAs by assets have someone with an AI or innovation leadership title, according to Caddi research reported by WealthManagement.com on November 6. Caddi built the list from Form ADV data and LinkedIn job postings and profiles. Of firms with a clear AI leader, 71.4% have hired for AI tools, against 11% of firms without one. Seventeen of the 24 AI-lead titles started in 2026, and 56.6% of AI-hiring firms made no compliance or security hire.

Why it matters: Ownership, not budget, appears to separate RIAs that put AI to work from those still evaluating it. Firms without a named lead should assign one before signing more vendor contracts. The compliance gap matters more: firms adding notetakers and agents without archiving or security staff are building books-and-records and supervision risk into every rollout.
InvestmentNews

FIS survey finds 37% of Americans ready for AI to manage their finances

Some 37% of US adults say they are ready for AI to manage their finances and 49% are not, according to FIS’s 2026 Consumer Pulse Survey with Kantar, reported by InvestmentNews on November 6. The September 16–30 survey of 2,000 adults found 57% comfortable with AI flagging or freezing a suspicious charge and 55% with paying a pre-approved bill, but only 40% with applying for a loan or credit card. Some 22% said they had personally been targeted by an AI impersonation scam.

Why it matters: Clients accept AI that protects money before AI that moves it, which favors fraud alerts and bill-pay over autonomous trading. Firms planning client-facing agents should lead with safeguards and confirmation steps. The scam figure matters most for advisors: impersonation attempts are now common enough that every firm needs a call-back rule before acting on client money-movement requests.
AI in Asset Management
Bloomberg

Hedge funds sell tech at a near-record pace and cut leverage most since 2020

Hedge funds net sold technology stocks in the week to November 6 at a pace roughly matching the record in Goldman Sachs prime brokerage data going back to 2016, Bloomberg reported on November 9. Tech was the most heavily sold sector globally and in the US, and funds sold US industrials at the fastest pace since April 2020. Gross leverage at US fundamental long-short funds fell 8.7 points to 207.5%, the largest drop since March 2020, and net leverage fell 3.7 points to 43.3%.

Why it matters: De-grossing on this scale means funds are cutting longs and shorts together, which can exaggerate moves in crowded AI names in both directions. Allocators should ask long-short managers how much of October’s loss came from crowded positions. Advisors can frame the selloff for clients as forced position-cutting by funds, not necessarily a verdict on AI earnings.
WealthManagement.com (Bloomberg)

Capital Group, Fidelity and BlackRock funds hold over $15 billion of Anthropic stock

Mutual funds run by Capital Group, Fidelity and BlackRock hold Anthropic shares worth more than $15 billion combined, according to Bloomberg data and filings reported by WealthManagement.com on November 5. Capital Group holds at least $9 billion, including more than $7 billion across its Growth Fund of America and Fundamental Investors funds. Fidelity holds at least $2 billion, including about $1 billion in Select Technology Portfolio, and BlackRock at least $500 million. Anthropic’s last private round valued it at $965 billion.

Why it matters: Many clients already own Anthropic through ordinary active funds, often without knowing it. Advisors fielding requests for IPO shares should first check existing exposure through these funds. The IPO price will also reset how the funds mark their stakes, so holders of Capital Group and Fidelity growth funds could see NAV moves tied to a single listing.
