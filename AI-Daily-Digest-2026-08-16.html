Fortune

Anthropic investors are targeting a $2 trillion valuation for an October listing

Half a dozen Anthropic investors told the Financial Times they are working toward a roughly $2 trillion valuation when the company lists in October, Fortune reported August 13. The figure rests on internal projections of $100 billion to $120 billion of revenue by the end of 2026. At that level the offering would surpass SpaceX and rank as the largest initial public offering on record. Senior Anthropic executives have not set a valuation target, and the company is not yet profitable.

Why it matters: Clients will start asking about pre-IPO and day-one allocations this quarter, and the anchor circulating is an investor target rather than anything in a filing. Advisors should keep the two separate in writing. At $2 trillion with no net income, matching Nasdaq 100 multiples implies roughly $60 billion to $80 billion of annual profit — a number worth stating before any suitability conversation.
Anthropic

Anthropic disclosed an unreleased Model 2 that outscores Mythos 5 internally

Anthropic's August 2026 Risk Report, published August 15, describes an internal system it calls Model 2 that scores 62.8% on the company's CoBench evaluation against 50.3% for the publicly released Mythos 5. Anthropic says it has no current plans to release the model externally. The report also moves its qualitative estimate of catastrophic harm from misalignment in high-stakes settings from "very low" to "low," and states that its most concrete task-based capability evaluations have saturated.

Why it matters: A frontier lab is telling the public that its own measurement tools no longer track capability gains. Firms drafting AI governance policy should stop treating vendor benchmark scores as a control and instead require use-case-level testing and documented human review thresholds, because the supplier itself is signalling that headline eval numbers have lost resolution.
Bloomberg

Bond investors are weighing $70 billion of off-balance-sheet AI chip backstops

Bloomberg reported August 15 that roughly $70 billion of residual-value guarantees tied to AI chip financings sit off the balance sheets of the companies providing them. Nvidia is positioned to supply tens of billions of dollars of that support, letting borrowers lean on its credit rating to hold funding costs down. The typical structure places chips inside a special-purpose vehicle that borrows against a user contract, with the hardware re-leased or sold if the user stops paying.

Why it matters: Credit analysts pricing data-center paper are effectively underwriting a chipmaker's promise that never appears in its own filings, and those guarantees would come due in precisely the downturn that compresses the guarantor's earnings. Fixed-income PMs holding AI-linked asset-backed positions should ask managers to quantify guarantor concentration across the book rather than treating each deal's rating as independent.
Google Blog

Google shipped Gemini 3.7 Flash three weeks after its predecessor

Google released Gemini 3.7 Flash on August 13, three weeks after Gemini 3.6 Flash, positioning it as a workhorse model for coding, agent workflows and knowledge tasks. The company did not train it from scratch, rebuilding the prior version through algorithmic changes and user feedback instead. Introductory pricing runs at $0.75 per million input tokens through December 31, 2026. Google's flagship Gemini 3.5 Pro, originally promised for June, remains unreleased.

Why it matters: A three-week refresh cycle on the low-cost tier means any AI feature a vendor demos this quarter will sit on a model that is replaced before implementation finishes. Technology committees should require vendors to state which model version a tool runs on, and to specify who bears the retesting and cost burden when that version changes underneath them.

Charles Schwab Form 10-Q

Schwab booked a $633 million multi-year software licence in second-quarter capex

Charles Schwab's second-quarter 10-Q reports capital expenditures of $792 million against $136 million a year earlier, driven primarily by a $633 million multi-year software licence recognised with a matching liability in long-term debt. First-half capex reached $965 million versus $292 million. Full-year spending now runs above the guided 3% to 5% of net revenues. The filing names artificial intelligence, digital assets and private company securities among the competitive trends shaping the business. Schwab has not identified the vendor.

Why it matters: A licence large enough to carry its own long-term debt liability is a platform commitment rather than a pilot, and it lands at the custodian holding more than $4 trillion of advisor assets. RIAs on Schwab should expect the Advisor Center capability set to move on a vendor's release schedule and should plan training and supervision reviews around that cadence.
eToro Q2 2026 Earnings Call

eToro says AI rewrote its entire app, replacing 500 person-years of development

eToro told analysts on its second-quarter call that it rebuilt its application from scratch using AI, replacing what it estimates was more than 500 person-years of prior development, and placed an agent named Tori at the centre of the new experience. Official Grok and Claude connectors now let clients authorise AI agents to trade autonomously under read-only or portfolio-scoped permissions. Net contribution rose 9% to $229 million and funded accounts rose 18% to 4.28 million.

Why it matters: Third-party agents transacting inside a brokerage account is production plumbing at a listed broker now, not a conference demo. Compliance teams should settle in advance how they will supervise an agent-initiated order: what constitutes the client's authorisation record, how permission scopes are evidenced after the fact, and who is accountable when an agent trades outside its mandate.

MoneyLion veterans raised $10 million for a wealth-data semantic layer

Astraeus, founded by former MoneyLion global chief technology officer Phill Rosen and former MoneyLion wealth head Jon Stevenson, went live on August 6 with more than $10 million from Fintech Collective, F-Prime, Walkabout Ventures and Plug and Play Ventures. The New York company sells a semantic layer that unifies clients, advisors, accounts, products, fees and regulatory requirements without replacing incumbent systems. It is being sold to advisory firms, wealthtech platforms and private equity owners rather than to individual advisors.

Why it matters: Every agentic tool an RIA buys assumes clean, joined data underneath it, and most firms discover the gap only after a pilot stalls. Firms scoping AI projects for 2027 budgets should sequence a data-mapping exercise ahead of vendor selection, and press each vendor on which fields it expects to already exist and who owns reconciling them.

Alternative Credit Investor

HPS says the AI buildout is reshaping the private credit opportunity set

HPS Investment Partners, the private credit manager BlackRock acquired in 2025, said on August 14 that AI infrastructure financing is widening the opportunity set toward large corporate and investment-grade borrowers. The firm argued this buildout differs from the telecom fibre glut because much of the capacity has committed users before construction finishes, with hyperscalers and investment-grade counterparties standing behind many transactions. Outstanding private credit loans to AI-related borrowers have grown from near zero to more than $200 billion.

Why it matters: The pitch to allocators has shifted from yield pickup to counterparty quality, which is a testable claim rather than a theme. Diligence on any credit sleeve marketed on AI exposure should establish what share of the book depends on a single hyperscaler tenant, and how the take-or-pay contracts behave if that tenant renegotiates mid-term.
InvestmentNews

AI voice cloning hit Point72, Citadel, Millennium and Two Sigma in coordinated attacks

Point72, Citadel, Millennium and Two Sigma were targeted in a coordinated wave of AI voice-phishing attempts, Bloomberg first reported. Point72 told investors its initial review found no client information taken, and Two Sigma said it blocked the attempt with no impact to data or systems. FINRA has been in contact with member firms about the breaches. Several private equity managers were hit in the same campaign. Align Managed Services said AI has lifted attacker reach from roughly 50 targets to 1,000.

Why it matters: Operational due diligence questionnaires still ask about phishing training rather than voice-clone verification on wire instructions and data-access requests. Allocators should add a direct question on out-of-band callback procedures, and managers should assume any voice authorisation embedded in a treasury or IT workflow is now forgeable at scale and cheaply.
NCPERS

Most public pension sponsors lack in-house expertise to oversee AI tools

A National Conference on Public Employee Retirement Systems research brief released in early August found 63% of public pension sponsors cite a lack of in-house technical expertise to evaluate or oversee AI tools as their top barrier to expansion, while 43% name cybersecurity vulnerabilities from AI systems or vendors as their leading risk. Some 58% said they are optimistic about AI's effect on defined benefit administration over the next decade. Equable Institute separately estimates 10% or more of public plan assets carry AI exposure.

Why it matters: The same asset owners reporting an oversight-skills gap are already carrying double-digit portfolio exposure to AI businesses. Managers pitching AI-themed sleeves into public plans should expect trustee questions that performance data will not answer, and should arrive with a plain-language read on concentration, vendor dependence and what a capex slowdown does to the position.
