Nvidia Prices $25 Billion Bond, Its Largest Debt Sale Ever

On June 15, Nvidia priced a $25 billion investment-grade bond — its first since 2021 and largest on record — drawing roughly $85 billion in orders. The seven-tranche deal, upsized from about $20 billion, spans maturities from 2028 to 2056, with the 30-year note landing at just 65 basis points over Treasuries. Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley led the sale, which helps fund Nvidia's strategic stakes, including up to $10 billion to Anthropic and roughly $30 billion in OpenAI's latest round.

Why it matters: Even the AI cycle's biggest cash generator is now tapping debt markets to fund its web of model-maker stakes, deepening the sector's circular financing. PMs holding Nvidia or AI-infrastructure credit should weigh how investment-grade issuance at decades-long tenors ties chipmaker balance sheets to the same customers buying their GPUs.

G7 Summit in Évian Centers on AI Access and US Dominance

The G7 summit running June 15–17 in Évian-les-Bains put artificial intelligence atop the agenda, with leaders debating a framework that would extend access to American AI systems only to trusted allies. CEOs including Sam Altman, Dario Amodei and Demis Hassabis joined heads of state, while US export controls on Anthropic's frontier models featured prominently. Reports suggest any joint language on AI governance will be softened as Washington resists multilateral rules that could blunt its industrial edge.

Why it matters: AI is hardening into an instrument of trade and national-security policy, not just a product cycle. Advisors and PMs with international or semiconductor exposure should track how allied access tiers and export controls could fragment AI supply chains — shifting which regions and vendors can deploy frontier models and repricing the geopolitical risk embedded in tech allocations.

First State Financial Rolls Out Mili AI Across 50-Plus Locations

On June 15, First State Financial Management — the wealth arm of Missouri's $4.5 billion First State Community Bank — named Mili as its AI platform for advisors across more than 50 statewide locations. Advisors run client meetings end to end on Mili: preparation from firm records, real-time notes without bots or recordings, automatic CRM updates, and follow-ups drafted in each advisor's voice. The two firms plan to extend the platform into annuity paperwork and required-minimum-distribution workflows.

Why it matters: AI advisor copilots are moving past wirehouses and large RIAs into community-bank wealth units, a tier that rarely leads on technology. Advisors evaluating meeting-automation tools should note the shift toward no-recording capture and write-back into existing CRM and planning systems, and press vendors on data handling before deploying client-conversation AI under bank compliance regimes.

Tradeweb Launches TARA, an AI Research Assistant for US Credit Traders

On June 15, Tradeweb launched TARA, a conversational AI assistant embedded in its institutional platform that lets US credit market participants query trading activity, market flows, execution performance, liquidity and pricing in natural language. TARA pairs Tradeweb's proprietary intraday and historical data with its Ai-Price pricing engine to deliver tailored insights. A pilot is live for select US credit clients, with broader availability expected in July and global credit and government bonds to follow later in 2026.

Why it matters: Embedding natural-language analytics directly in the execution venue moves AI from back-office research toward the point of trade for fixed-income desks. PMs and traders should assess how venue-native assistants change pre-trade workflows and best-execution documentation, and whether reliance on a platform's proprietary pricing engine concentrates dependence on a single provider's data.
