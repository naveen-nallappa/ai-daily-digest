Apple Opens iOS 27 to Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini as Default AI Engines

At WWDC on June 8, Apple unveiled an "Extensions" framework in iOS 27 that lets users set Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or Grok as the model behind Siri, Writing Tools and Image Playground, ending its single-provider arrangement. The redesigned Siri itself runs on Google Gemini under a multi-year deal worth roughly $1 billion per year, with most queries staying on-device or on Apple's private servers. The features are in beta now, with broad release expected around September.

Why it matters: Default placement on a billion iPhones is the largest distribution prize in consumer AI, and an open marketplace turns model choice into a recurring user decision rather than a locked default. For firms building client-facing tools, Apple's on-device-first routing sets a privacy benchmark advisors can cite when vetting where client data is processed and which provider sits behind a given feature.

Google Launches Gemini 3.5 Live Translate Across 70 Languages

Google introduced Gemini 3.5 Live Translate on June 10, a real-time audio model that translates spoken conversation across up to 70 languages with low latency. The capability is rolling into Google Translate and Google Meet and ships as an API for developers to embed in their own products. Google positioned the model for live meetings and cross-border calls rather than document translation, where the company already fields separate tools.

Why it matters: Embeddable real-time translation lowers the bar for firms serving multilingual households or cross-border clients without staffing every language in-house. Compliance teams should note that live audio translation creates a new class of recorded, machine-generated client communication; supervision and books-and-records policies written for text and human interpreters will need to cover translated meeting audio.

State Street Uses an LLM to Scan Filings and Build an Innovation ETF

State Street Global Advisors, which manages more than $5 trillion including roughly $1.9 trillion in ETFs, has partnered with an AI analytics provider that runs a large language model across thousands of regulatory filings to identify companies tied to themes such as drones, biotech and electric vehicles. The system tracks how often and in what context terms appear, then sorts each company into one of 25 innovation buckets that feed construction of the manager's innovation ETF, augmenting human portfolio decisions.

Why it matters: Thematic ETFs built on LLM-parsed disclosures change what due diligence looks like — allocators evaluating these products should ask how the buckets are defined, how often they re-classify, and where a human overrides the model. As more issuers adopt language-model screening, two thematic funds with similar labels can hold very different baskets depending on the prompt and taxonomy behind them.
