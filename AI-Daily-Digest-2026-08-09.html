OpenAI pauses work on its Astra model over critical cyber capabilities

On August 7, OpenAI said it "cannot rule out" that its unreleased Astra model has reached the "critical" cyber threshold in its Preparedness Framework — meaning it could identify and develop zero-day exploits without human intervention. The company is pausing internal Astra activities that don't yet meet strengthened security controls and is expanding safety testing. It may be the first time a frontier lab has slowed one of its own models specifically over offensive-cyber risk, days after touting Astra's machine-verified math breakthroughs.

Why it matters: A lab throttling its most capable model reframes AI safety from hypothetical to operational and signals capability is outrunning control. Security and risk teams at financial firms should treat frontier models as dual-use, tightening governance around agentic coding tools before deployment, and watch whether "critical cyber" designations become a recurring release gate that delays the enterprise features they are counting on.
Tom's Hardware

Amazon backs a 7.65 GW Texas gas plant to power an off-grid AI campus

Permitting filings show Amazon is behind GW Ranch, a 7.65-gigawatt, 35-turbine natural-gas plant in Pecos County, Texas, built to power a new off-grid AI data-center campus without waiting on grid interconnection. Authorized to emit up to roughly 30 million tons of CO2 a year, it would rank among the largest single emissions sources in the country. Amazon targets first power by early 2027, is evaluating on-site solar and battery storage, and filed three data-center building permits in early August.

Why it matters: The scale confirms hyperscalers will self-generate fossil power to keep AI buildouts on schedule, decoupling compute growth from grid and clean-energy timelines. PMs holding Amazon or ESG-screened portfolios should model the emissions and regulatory exposure this creates, and treat utility-scale gas procurement as a leading indicator of how far power supply has fallen behind chip availability.

Slant builds AI intake forms that advisors create in plain language

Slant, an AI-native CRM for advisors, launched Intake Forms that users generate by describing what they want in plain language, with client responses written back to the record in one click. The tool pre-fills known CRM data and extracts details from an uploaded tax return before the client is asked anything, treating the form as an output of the database rather than a separate app bolted on top. Why it matters: When the system of record can generate and populate its own forms, the busywork of re-keying client data starts to disappear — the practical win advisors actually feel. Firms evaluating CRMs should test how deeply AI reads from and writes to the underlying database, since a chat layer that cannot act on structured client data produces demos, not time savings.
