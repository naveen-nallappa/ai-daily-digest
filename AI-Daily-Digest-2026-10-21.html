Major AI Updates
Meta investor relations

Meta raises the floor of 2026 capex to $135 billion as revenue grows 26%

Meta reported third-quarter revenue of $64.6 billion on October 21, up 26% from a year earlier, and guided fourth-quarter revenue to $73–76 billion. It narrowed 2026 capital spending to $135–145 billion, lifting the low end from $130 billion, after $34.9 billion of capex in the quarter. Meta gave no 2027 figure but said it will keep investing aggressively. A $9.9 billion legal charge cut reported EPS to $3.83, against $7.40 excluding it.

Why it matters: Meta credits AI with lifting ad revenue now, which gives hyperscaler capex a measurable payback that PMs can track quarter by quarter. Its open-ended 2027 language keeps demand visible for Nvidia, memory and power suppliers. Advisors fielding “AI bubble” questions can point to revenue growth that is keeping pace with spending, at least here.
Super Micro Form 8-K (SEC)

Super Micro lifts quarterly revenue to $18.5–19.5 billion, about $4 billion above guidance

Super Micro Computer said on October 20 that preliminary revenue for its fiscal first quarter, ended September 30, will be $18.5–19.5 billion, against prior guidance of $14.5–15.5 billion. It attributed the increase to demand for AI platforms. Gross margin should be 12.0–12.5%, above the 10.4–10.8% it had guided, which it credited to customer and product mix. Full results and the earnings call are set for November 3.

Why it matters: A server assembler beating its own guide by roughly 27% is a near-real-time read on AI rack shipments, ahead of Nvidia’s November print. The margin gain matters as much as volume, since thin hardware margins were the bear case. PMs should still wait for the November 3 cash-flow and receivables figures before treating the beat as clean.
DatacenterDynamics

Nvidia launches DSX POD, a prebuilt Vera Rubin data-center module for early 2027

Nvidia unveiled DSX POD at GTC Berlin on October 21, a containerized Vera Rubin data-center design that is built and tested before it ships. Nvidia claims it cuts deployment from about six months to 14 days, uses up to 30% less power for the same output, and can eliminate evaporative cooling water. Partners include Dell, HPE, Foxconn, Schneider Electric, Vertiv and Eaton. First deliveries are scheduled worldwide for early 2027.

Why it matters: If prebuilt modules work as claimed, the bottleneck for AI capacity shifts from construction time to power hookups, which favors utilities and sites with grid access already in hand. PMs holding data-center REITs, builders or cooling suppliers should ask how factory-built modules change their backlog and pricing. The efficiency figures are Nvidia’s own and untested.
AI in Wealth Management
WealthManagement.com

$20 billion RIA Modera unveils Mira, an agentic AI platform built with OnTrade

Modera Wealth Management, a $20 billion RIA in Westwood, New Jersey, announced Mira on October 21, an in-house agentic AI platform live since July. It links advisor workflows and systems, and its AI layer, from OnTrade, runs agents for planning, proposals and investment research. Modera spent two years restructuring data and governance first, and a working group validates the data. President Joe McCuine said advisors will “earn back hours.” Terms and adoption figures were not disclosed.

Why it matters: Modera joins Mercer, Mariner and Connecticut Wealth in building a firm-owned AI layer rather than renting point tools, and it says Mira will speed onboarding of acquired advisors. Sellers and recruits should ask acquirers whether their data will be moved onto such a platform. Firms comparing approaches should note that Modera fixed its data before adding agents.
Banking Dive

FDIC’s Travis Hill says AI agents moving deposits is regulators’ top concern

FDIC head Travis Hill said at Money20/20 in Las Vegas on October 20 that AI agents shifting customer deposits is “the big thing that we’re thinking about,” and that some movement is certain. He said the revised model risk guidance from the FDIC, OCC and Fed does not “work well” for agentic and generative AI. The FDIC is considering a public request for information on supervising them. No date was given.

Why it matters: Agents that move idle cash to the best yield would squeeze sweep income, a large profit line at Schwab, Merrill and other brokerage platforms. That helps explain why banks and Schwab now block outside agents. Firms should model sweep balances under faster rate-shopping and plan to comment if the FDIC opens a request for information.
AdvisorHub

M&A banker warns AI could erode RIA valuations now at a record 11.6 times EBITDA

Allen Darby of Alaris Acquisitions told AdvisorHub’s Advisors to Watch conference in New York, reported October 21, that AI may undercut record RIA prices. Median valuations reached 11.6 times EBITDA in 2025, per Advisor Growth Strategies, and deals hit a record 142 in the first quarter. Darby cited Carson data showing advisors who use AI serve 18% more households. He said some buyers view clients with up to $2 million as suited to AI agents.

Why it matters: If buyers start pricing mass-affluent books as automatable, multiples for practices concentrated below $2 million per client could compress first. Owners planning a sale should document how their AI tools add capacity, since that is now part of the case for value. Acquirers should test whether a target’s households-per-advisor figures already reflect AI gains.
AI in Asset Management
BlackRock ETF Trust Form 497K (SEC)

BlackRock launches RBTX, an active robotics ETF charging 0.65%

BlackRock launched the iShares Future Robotics and Related Technologies Active ETF (RBTX) on NYSE Arca on October 21, at a 0.65% expense ratio. Tony Kim and Reid Menge, who run more than $50 billion in AI and technology strategies, manage it against the MSCI ACWI. BlackRock’s BAI, the largest US AI ETF, holds over $14 billion. Reuters counted at least six AI-linked ETF launches in the past month, including Defiance’s AI infrastructure bond fund.

Why it matters: Issuers are slicing AI into narrower sleeves: robotics, language models and now AI-issuer bonds. Advisors adding a thematic fund should check how much it overlaps with Nvidia and the megacaps clients already hold through index funds. At 0.65%, RBTX costs about six times a core equity ETF, so the active manager has to earn it.
Hedgeweek (citing Bloomberg)

Citadel’s Ken Griffin says AI is “almost certainly” in a bubble

Citadel founder Ken Griffin said at the Future of Global Markets conference in New York on October 20 that AI is “almost certainly” in a bubble, Hedgeweek reported, citing Bloomberg. He said corporate America has been “very uneven” in getting value from AI models, and that radical economic change is generally “intertwined with a bubble.” He still called the coming period a potential “golden era for entrepreneurs.” He did not describe Citadel’s positioning.

Why it matters: Griffin’s point is uneven value capture, not a call that AI fails, which argues for owning companies showing AI revenue rather than the theme broadly. Coming a week after crowded AI trades unwound at hedge funds, it gives allocators a reason to check how much multi-strategy and long-short exposure overlaps with their equity AI holdings.
Hedgeweek (citing Bloomberg)

CME, ICE and Nodal await CFTC approval to list AI compute futures

CME Group, Intercontinental Exchange, Nodal Exchange and Architect Financial Technologies are waiting for CFTC approval to list compute-linked derivatives, Hedgeweek reported on October 21, citing Bloomberg. Brokers ADM Investor Services, BGC Group and Nanhua USA are preparing to offer them, and hedge funds are showing interest. BGC set up a division for secondary trading in compute and memory capacity in June. In August, the CFTC barred listings until a public comment period ends.

Why it matters: A listed price for GPU-hours would let funds hedge or bet on compute rents directly, rather than through Nvidia, CoreWeave or data-center credit. That would also give PMs a market check on neocloud revenue assumptions. Managers with AI infrastructure exposure should follow the CFTC comment period, since approval would create a new hedge.
