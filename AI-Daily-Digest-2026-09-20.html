Major AI Updates
CBS News

Trump vows an 'AI Force' and a new AI czar, rejecting any slowdown

President Trump said on Truth Social on September 19 that he will form an "AI Force" modeled on the Space Force and will soon name an AI czar, a post vacant since David Sacks left in March. He called superintelligence fears a hoax, said the administration will not hinder the industry and will police bad actors through existing law, and projected AI could reach 25% of US GDP. The post is his fourth rebuke of Dario Amodei's slowdown call, two days after King Charles pressed Nvidia and OpenAI for controls.

Why it matters: A dedicated federal AI body run on a growth mandate lowers the odds that the Senate duty-of-care bill advances before the November 3 midterms, and the czar appointment will signal whether export controls or data-center permitting move first. PMs should treat US policy risk to AI capex as receding into 2027, and advisors can point clients to a clear executive-branch position.
Nscale Pressroom

Nscale files for NYSE IPO with a $103 billion contracted order book

London-based AI cloud builder Nscale filed a public S-1 on September 18 to list on the NYSE as NSCL, with Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley leading 23 underwriters and a raise of up to $3 billion reported by Bloomberg. The prospectus shows first-half revenue of $140.6 million, up 1,252%, against a $1.02 billion net loss, and $56.4 billion of remaining performance obligations anchored by a $44.6 billion Anthropic contract for 460 megawatts in West Virginia. Nscale also issued $3.1 billion of convertible notes, at least $1 billion to Nvidia.

Why it matters: This is the third AI-infrastructure listing in the pipeline behind Crusoe and Firmus, and the first whose order book is dominated by a single lab customer, so underwriting hinges on Anthropic's own October IPO clearing. Allocators should model NSCL as a levered Anthropic credit rather than a CoreWeave comp, and note the Nvidia convertible as vendor financing that will recur across neocloud filings.
Anthropic

Anthropic and Accenture commit $1 billion each to embedded model evaluators

Anthropic named Accenture as its first embedded evaluator on September 18, with each expecting to invest at least $1 billion over five years. Accenture's Faculty unit will red-team models, run alignment assessments and test safeguards with access comparable to an employee's, including visibility into training and deployment decisions. The deal delivers a commitment from Amodei's "We Must Pace the Frontier" essay and the June Advanced AI Framework. It is non-exclusive: Anthropic is piloting similar work with METR, will name more evaluators in coming weeks, and Accenture may serve other labs.

Why it matters: Embedded evaluation is now a line item with a price tag, which gives IPO investors a concrete governance disclosure to underwrite in Anthropic's prospectus and a template OpenAI will be pressed to match. Compliance teams at firms deploying Claude should ask vendors whether evaluator findings will be published, since those reports will become the reference point for model-risk documentation under SEC and FINRA review.
The Wall Street Journal

Researchers used Claude Opus 5 to reach OpenAI's internal GitHub in one day

Researchers at startup Hacktron AI used Claude to breach OpenAI's employee forum and pivot into its internal GitHub, the Journal reported September 18. Claude Opus 4.8 built the initial exploit for an unpatched libheif image bug on June 23; Opus 5, released June 24, defeated OpenAI's memory randomization the same day, and a single sign-on misconfiguration exposed an employee account. OpenAI patched within 14 hours of the report. Separately, AIR disclosed Plugin4Shell, a zero-click flaw in Claude Code, Codex, Copilot and Gemini CLI; Anthropic and OpenAI have patched, Microsoft has not.

Why it matters: A frontier model closed the gap from proof-of-concept to a working exploit against a hardened target in 24 hours, which resets the assumed patch window for every firm running Discourse, Slack or any libheif-dependent image pipeline. CISOs at custodians and RIAs should confirm Claude Code and Codex versions are current, freeze Copilot plugin auto-updates, and add model-assisted exploitation to third-party risk questionnaires.
AI in Wealth Management
Cerulli Associates

Only 12% of RIAs rate as AI leaders, and governance beats budget

Cerulli's State of Wealth Management AI Adoption study, built with Vista Equity Partners from 68 RIAs managing about $1.2 trillion, sorts half of firms into an "exploring" tier, 38% scaling and 12% leading. Firms with centralized or hybrid AI governance scored more than 2.5 times higher, and 75% of leaders run formal training versus 21% of explorers. AI is set to rise from 8% to 15% of technology budgets in 2026, while 73% of firms plan to hire junior advisors over two years against 23% adding administrative staff.

Why it matters: The maturity score gives RIA acquirers and lenders a benchmark to price AI readiness into deals, and shows a named AI owner and written use policy move firms up the tiers faster than spend. Firms evaluating Claude for Financial Advisors or Cyan should document governance first, since 54% still cite compliance as the top barrier and examiners will ask for the policy before the productivity data.
InvestmentNews

Domain Money cuts half its planning team as AI lifts capacity to 150 clients

Flat-fee virtual RIA Domain Money, founded by venture capitalist Adam Dell, laid off about half of its planning team, with Dell telling InvestmentNews that automation now lets one advisor serve up to 150 clients. The cuts landed the same week as the Future Proof Festival in Huntington Beach, where Wedbush's Dan Ives argued AI will create more jobs than it removes and Luminary founder David Barnard said firms deploying AI well will win the wealth transfer. Domain Money is the first publicly reported RIA to tie advisor layoffs directly to AI.

Why it matters: A 150-client load at a flat-fee shop sets a visible productivity marker that acquirers and PE sponsors will use to challenge staffing ratios at traditional RIAs, where 80 to 100 households per advisor is still the norm. Firm owners should decide now whether AI capacity funds growth hires or margin, because the Cerulli data suggests the market is currently rewarding the former.
The Daily Upside

Advisors move to cap client AI exposure ahead of the Anthropic and OpenAI IPOs

A September 20 Advisor Upside survey of wealth managers finds the AI conversation has shifted from stock picking to exposure control. Arena Private Wealth's Christian Francaviglia starts client meetings by quantifying AI concentration already held through index funds and pushes "stopping the accumulation" rather than exiting. Sovereign Financial Group's Charles Failla warned that two of the three largest tech IPOs ever could price within months, so total-market index holders will buy them automatically. Opto Investments' Jake Miller called China risk overstated, with Chinese models trailing US frontier models by roughly seven months.

Why it matters: Client conversations in Q4 will center on how much Anthropic and OpenAI paper flows into cap-weighted funds after listing, and advisors need a rule for handling that passive drift before it happens. Practical steps: report AI look-through exposure alongside sector weights, pre-agree a concentration ceiling with each household, and pair any AI overweight with the equal-weight or EM tilts BlackRock and AllianceBernstein flagged this week.

NBER Digest

NBER study finds AI hedge funds' 50-basis-point monthly edge vanished after 2017

A National Bureau of Economic Research paper by Shuang Chen, Clemens Sialm and David Xu, featured in the September Digest, classified 116,000 SEC adviser disclosures from 2012 to 2024 and 7,896 US hedge funds by how they use AI. Funds whose strategies explicitly rely on AI beat non-AI peers by about 50 basis points a month before 2017, but the gap has since been statistically indistinguishable from zero. AI funds still show far lower return correlation with each other, 0.021 versus 0.124, and held only about $12 billion in 2024.

Why it matters: This is the first large-sample evidence that AI alpha in liquid macro and systematic strategies is arbitraged away on the same timeline as any other factor, which undercuts the premium pitch behind new AI-native launches from Magnetar and others. Allocators should demand sibling-fund comparisons and post-2017 track records, and treat low crowding, not headline outperformance, as the durable reason to hold AI-driven managers.
Bloomberg

Tech ETFs took $52 billion since March against $4 billion for everything else

Investors have put $52 billion into information-technology ETFs since the S&P 500 bottomed in late March, versus $4 billion into funds tracking the other ten sectors combined, according to Baird Strategas data reported by Bloomberg on September 14. That 13-to-1 ratio is the most lopsided sector flow on record and preceded the September 14 chip-index rout triggered by the frontier-lab slowdown calls. First Trust and ETF Action flow monitors for early September show semiconductor funds still leading inflows even as broad large-cap blend products began drawing money back.

Why it matters: Flow concentration this extreme means any de-rating of AI names now feeds directly into ETF redemptions and forced basket selling, amplifying moves in the underlying stocks. Model-portfolio teams should stress-test tech sleeves against a two-week outflow reversal, and PMs benchmarking against sector-neutral indices should note that the marginal ETF buyer, not fundamentals, has set semiconductor multiples since March.
Yorkville America (PR Newswire)

Yorkville lists a 2x leveraged ETF on a basket holding OpenAI, Anthropic and SpaceX

Yorkville America launched the 2X Long MANGOS Plus Daily Target ETF (MNGZ) on September 17, targeting 200% of the daily return of its MANGOS Plus Index ETF (FRUT) via swaps. FRUT, listed August 31 at a 0.50% fee, holds the six "MANGO" names, Meta, Nvidia, Alphabet, SpaceX, OpenAI and Anthropic, plus a "Parabolic 7" hardware sleeve led by Broadcom, Micron and AMD, with private-company exposure obtained mainly via total-return swaps and direct private holdings capped at 5%. Bloomberg's ETF desk flagged FRUT among three new launches to watch this week.

Why it matters: Daily-reset leverage stacked on swap-based exposure to unlisted labs is a new risk shape: the swap counterparty marks OpenAI and Anthropic, and MNGZ compounds that mark twice a day, with no primary-market liquidity behind it until the IPOs price. Due-diligence teams should keep these tickers off model and advisory platforms, and compliance should flag them under leveraged-product suitability rules.
