Major AI Updates
Anthropic

Anthropic’s $64 billion IPO is said to be several times oversubscribed

Bloomberg reported on November 11 that Anthropic’s IPO is multiple times oversubscribed, a day after the roadshow opened at $70 to $78 a share. AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC are buying a concurrent $26 billion private placement at the IPO price, lifting combined proceeds to about $90.4 billion at the top of the range, above SpaceX’s June record. Pricing is expected November 18 and Nasdaq trading as “ANT” on November 19. Bloomberg also reported a separate $3–3.5 billion Japan offering.

Why it matters: Heavy oversubscription makes a top-of-range or raised price likelier and means retail tranches will be cut back sharply. Advisors should tell clients to expect partial or no allocations and discourage chasing the stock on its first day. PMs should note that seven chip and cloud suppliers buying in at the IPO price deepens the circular financing already worrying AI credit investors.
Google

Google releases Gemini 4 Carbon at $2 per million input tokens

Google made Gemini 4 Carbon generally available on November 10 through its API, Gemini for business and Google AI Ultra. Introductory pricing of $2 per million input and $10 per million output tokens rises to $4 and $20 after November 30. Google says Carbon matched the best model on Hebbia’s end-to-end financial agent evaluation and ranked first of 23 configurations on Balyasny’s General Financial Intelligence benchmark. It passed 92% of a 375-task agentic honesty test, against 70% for Gemini 4 Argon.

Why it matters: Benchmarks run by a hedge fund and a financial research vendor make Carbon a direct contender for research and document workflows at banks and asset managers. Firms renewing model contracts should test it before the December 1 price step. A price fight among frontier labs during Anthropic’s IPO week also argues for multi-model contract terms rather than single-vendor lock-in.
OpenAI

OpenAI tells investors third-quarter revenue reached about $10 billion, up 50%

OpenAI told investors on November 10 that third-quarter revenue was roughly $10 billion, about 50% above the second quarter, The Information reported. Growth had slowed to about 17% in the second quarter; executives credited renewed demand for recent models and enterprise products. Business sales are reportedly overtaking consumer sales for the first time. The same day, OpenAI opened a Business Hub beta with Business Agents for eligible US e-commerce and online-service companies.

Why it matters: The figures give investors a private-market yardstick for Anthropic’s IPO pricing and show enterprise AI demand recovering after a slow first half. That matters more for hyperscaler capex, and the stocks clients own, than any single listing. Firms choosing AI vendors should note OpenAI’s push into business agents widens the field beyond Anthropic-led advisor tools.
AI in Wealth Management
Charles Schwab Pressroom

Schwab pilots an MCP server letting clients’ own AI tools reach their accounts

Charles Schwab said on November 12 it is piloting its own Model Context Protocol server with a select group of retail clients using Anthropic’s Claude. Once authorized, a client’s AI can pull market data and research, analyze positions, summarize portfolios and set alerts; thinkorswim-enabled accounts can prepare, place and manage trades with the client confirming. Schwab plans wider access and more AI platforms over coming months. It also said a thinkorswim Assistant is due soon and Charley continues its phased rollout.

Why it matters: Schwab is replacing the unauthorized agent logins it blocks with a channel it controls, including trade placement. Advisors with self-directed Schwab clients should expect AI-built portfolio views in reviews. RIAs on Schwab Advisor Services should watch whether their read-only Claude connection gains similar write access, because that would change supervision and trading-authority procedures.
Fidelity Investments

Fidelity sets a $25,000 asset minimum for clients seeking Anthropic IPO shares

Fidelity published terms on November 10 for retail customers seeking Anthropic shares: at least $25,000 in eligible Fidelity assets, requests from 25 to 1,000,000 shares in 25-share steps, and an offer period closing at 4 p.m. ET on November 17. Shares are allocated by tiering, which ranks customers by assets and revenue. Selling within 15 calendar days counts as flipping and bars future Fidelity IPO access for six months, then one year, then permanently.

Why it matters: Fidelity’s tiering rewards its largest and most profitable households, so most retail requests will be filled partially or not at all. Advisors with clients at Fidelity or other brokers should set expectations now and confirm cash for T+1 settlement. Firms running their own IPO programs should document allocation and flipping rules in writing before November 17.
Robinhood Investor Relations

Robinhood ranks Cortex among top priorities at Wolfe Research on November 11

Robinhood’s November 11 investor presentation for Wolfe Research listed “Cortex, Social” third among its 2026-and-beyond priorities, under the goal of leading next-generation wallet share. Its product timeline included Agentic Trading, Robinhood Agents and Agent Apps. As of September 30 it had 28.7 million funded customers, up 1.9 million, $396 billion of platform assets, up 19%, and 5.2 million Gold subscribers. October net deposits were about $15 billion. The deck gave no AI usage figures.

Why it matters: Robinhood is pairing AI research and agent trading with fast asset growth among younger investors, the heirs advisors hope to keep. Practices serving multigenerational families should ask whether heirs already use Robinhood’s AI tools and offer comparable digital research. Watch for Cortex usage figures with full monthly metrics; none were disclosed at the conference.
AI in Asset Management
Hedgeweek

Point72’s Cubist alleges an analyst used Claude Code and Codex to compile research

Point72’s Cubist Systematic Strategies alleges in court filings that former analyst Xin Jin ran 4,117 queries across 2,416 sessions of Anthropic’s Claude Code and OpenAI’s Codex between February and May 2026 to compile colleagues’ confidential research, Hedgeweek reported on November 12. Cubist fired him for cause two days after he said on September 16 that he had joined a rival. Jin says Cubist is withholding more than $30 million in investments and pay; his lawyers call the claims without merit.

Why it matters: Coding assistants open a new route for moving proprietary research, and their session logs are now courtroom evidence. Managers should confirm AI tools log prompts, limit repository access by role and appear in employment and IP agreements. Allocators should add AI-tool governance to operational due diligence questionnaires for quant and multi-manager funds.
Equable Institute

Equable finds the largest US public pensions hold up to $707 billion of AI stocks

Equable Institute estimates the 25 largest US public pension systems held $578 billion to $707 billion in AI-related companies as of June 30, PLANSPONSOR reported on November 9. About 85% sits in 15 companies, and the Magnificent Seven account for 63.3%. Colorado PERA has the highest share at 18.7%, followed by Minnesota SBI at 17.4%; CalPERS holds the most in dollars, $65.2 billion. The estimates use 13F filings and exclude private equity and private debt.

Why it matters: Public pensions are concentrated enough in AI that a sustained selloff would reach funded ratios and contribution rates. Consultants and OCIOs should report look-through AI concentration, including the private holdings this study leaves out. Advisors with public-sector clients should count pension exposure when judging how much more AI risk belongs in personal portfolios.
Hedgeweek

More hedge funds shorted Amazon, Apple, Nvidia and Meta in October

Data Insights’ October crowding report, covering about 16,000 securities and more than 700 hedge funds, shows more funds shorting Amazon, Apple, Nvidia and Meta, Hedgeweek reported on November 12. Tesla was the only Magnificent Seven stock with fewer short holders, and Apple’s long-to-short ratio fell to 0.65. AMD flipped to a short bias as its ratio fell to 0.94 from 1.18, while Nvidia stayed the most crowded semiconductor long, ahead of Broadcom and Applied Materials.

Why it matters: Rising shorts beside still-crowded longs raise the risk of sharp moves in both directions, as CoreWeave’s 20%-plus jump after earnings showed. PMs should check crowding in their AI holdings before adding to them. Advisors using active tech funds should ask managers how much of their AI exposure is hedged and how they size crowded names.
