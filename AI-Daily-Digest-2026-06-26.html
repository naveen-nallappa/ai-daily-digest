Anthropic's Most Powerful Models Stay Offline 13 Days After a US Export Order

As of June 25, Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 remain fully disabled — 13 days after a US government export-control directive ordered all access suspended for any foreign national, including Anthropic's own foreign-national employees. The company launched both models June 9 and pulled them June 12. After viral posts claimed access had returned, Anthropic staff said the firm is "serving exactly 0 traffic to Fable 5." The directive followed a Pentagon dispute over terms for military use of Claude.

Why it matters: Frontier-model availability is now a function of national-security policy, not just vendor roadmaps. Firms standardizing research or coding workflows on a single provider should keep a documented fallback, since a top model can vanish overnight by government order. Compliance teams should also note that export rules can bar foreign-national staff from specific AI tools, complicating global deployments.

China Drafts $295 Billion Plan for a National AI Grid on 80% Domestic Chips

China's National Development and Reform Commission is drafting a five-year, 2 trillion yuan ($295 billion) program to link thousands of data centers into a single national computing grid by 2028, with a mandate that at least 80% of the hardware — including AI accelerators — come from domestic suppliers. State carriers China Mobile and China Telecom would operate most facilities. The rule, if enforced, would effectively shut Nvidia and AMD out of China's AI buildout while favoring Huawei, Biren, and Moore Threads.

Why it matters: A state-mandated domestic-only buildout removes a large slice of addressable demand from US chipmakers while accelerating a parallel Chinese supply chain. Equity PMs with semiconductor exposure should reassess China revenue assumptions for Nvidia and AMD, and weigh second-order effects on memory and foundry names if self-sufficiency targets pull forward.
