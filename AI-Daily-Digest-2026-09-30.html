Major AI Updates
Bloomberg

OpenAI seeks at least $30 billion at a $1.4 trillion valuation as an IPO bridge

OpenAI is seeking at least $30 billion at a valuation of about $1.4 trillion, Bloomberg reported on September 29. The round would bridge the company to an IPO that Sam Altman has pushed from 2026 to 2027, citing safety. In March OpenAI raised $122 billion at an $852 billion valuation, a round then expected to be its last private raise. TechCrunch reported run-rate revenue of about $40 billion in August, up roughly 70% since July.

Why it matters: Two frontier labs now carry trillion-dollar private marks, and both are heading to public markets within about a year. PMs should size how index inclusion of Anthropic and OpenAI would dilute existing mega-cap AI weights, and advisors should expect clients holding private-market or interval funds to ask whether those funds own either company, and at what mark.
Bank of England

Bank of England warns AI valuations could face a sharper correction as debt financing grows

The Bank of England’s Financial Policy Committee, in its September 25 record published September 30, warned AI equity valuations remain at risk of a sharper correction after July’s fall. It cited a Morgan Stanley estimate of about $450 billion of global AI-related debt issued by early September, double 2025’s total, and a JPMorgan projection of $4.1 trillion of debt-financed AI capex from 2026 to 2030. About $700 billion of 2026-28 data-center capex could come from private credit. The countercyclical buffer stays at 2%.

Why it matters: A major central bank is now tracking AI financing as a systemic channel, not just an equity-valuation story. Credit PMs should map data-center and hyperscaler bond exposure across core and private-credit sleeves, and advisors should review how much AI capex risk sits in income products that clients treat as diversifiers from their growth-stock holdings.
AMD

AMD agrees to buy Fei-Fei Li’s World Labs for about $8.2 billion in stock

AMD agreed on September 28 to acquire World Labs, the world-model startup founded by Fei-Fei Li, in an all-stock deal valued at about $8.2 billion. Li becomes AMD executive vice president and chief scientist, reporting to CEO Lisa Su. The deal is a bet on physical AI, models that simulate and act in three-dimensional environments, and is expected to close by the end of 2026, subject to regulatory approval. Su said building compute platforms requires understanding how models evolve.

Why it matters: Chipmakers are buying model teams to differentiate against Nvidia, which shifts AI competition from hardware specs to full-stack offerings. Semiconductor PMs should weigh the dilution and integration risk against AMD’s positioning in robotics and simulation workloads, and thematic-fund analysts should watch whether world models become a separate index layer from language-model applications.
Law360

Third Circuit rules against fair use for AI training in Thomson Reuters v. Ross

The US Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit on September 29 affirmed Thomson Reuters’ summary-judgment win against Ross Intelligence, rejecting Ross’s fair-use defense for training its AI on Westlaw headnotes. It is the first US appellate ruling on fair use in AI training. Judge Tamika Montgomery-Reeves wrote the opinion, which is under a 10-day seal, so the court’s reasoning is not yet public. Ross shut down in 2021, citing litigation costs.

Why it matters: Licensed data is gaining legal value relative to scraped data, which favors owners of proprietary content such as Thomson Reuters, Morningstar and index providers. Firms building research or advice tools should confirm that vendors hold licenses for training data, and add copyright indemnities to AI contracts, since a model trained on unlicensed content becomes a liability for its users.
AI in Wealth Management
Charles Schwab

Schwab launches Charley, an AI assistant for retail clients rolling out from October

Schwab on September 30 unveiled Charley, a generative-AI assistant that answers questions by voice or text about transfers, trading, balances and statements, and completes routine tasks such as updating watchlists, adding beneficiaries and enrolling in dividend reinvestment. It hands clients to a live representative when needed. A phased rollout to eligible US retail clients on Schwab.com and the mobile app begins in October. In a Schwab survey of September 21-25, 65% of respondents said AI can handle routine tasks alongside human expertise.

Why it matters: The largest US custodian is putting an action-taking agent in front of retail clients, raising the service bar for every self-directed platform. RIAs custodied at Schwab should expect clients to ask for similar tools on their accounts, and should ask Schwab’s Advisor Services team at IMPACT in October whether Charley’s servicing features will reach advisor-held accounts.
Robinhood

Robinhood unveils in-app trading agents at HOOD Summit, with Loops coming by year-end

Robinhood unveiled Robinhood Agents at its HOOD Summit on September 29: named agents built into the app that research, build strategies and trade in a dedicated agentic account on OpenAI or Anthropic models. Manual trade approval is on by default. Robinhood says the agents are coming soon to eligible US customers, and Fortune reports access starting this week. Loops, which run standing instructions overnight, arrive by end-2026. Agent Apps add paid data from Nasdaq and others at $5-$30 a month.

Why it matters: Agentic trading is moving from a developer feature used by 150,000 customers to a mass-market product for about 29 million accounts. Advisors should expect clients with Robinhood side accounts to run automated strategies that conflict with their plan, and should ask about held-away trading activity in reviews. Compliance teams should watch how FINRA treats agent-initiated trades and suitability.
XY Planning Network

XYPN partners with Jump on AI tools for independent advisors, a week after Northwestern Mutual

XY Planning Network announced a partnership with Jump at XYPN LIVE in San Diego on September 29, giving members discounted access to Jump Meet, Grow and Onboard for meeting prep, notes, follow-ups, CRM updates and account opening. XYPN said AI efficiency tools were its most-requested partner category and Jump the most-requested product; hundreds of members already use it. Jump now cites more than 45,000 users and has raised $105 million, including an $80 million Series B in February.

Why it matters: Jump is becoming the default notetaker across both enterprise and independent channels, which narrows the field for smaller vendors. Independent RIAs should negotiate through a network or custodian program rather than alone, and check that Jump’s Schwab and Fidelity account-opening features are live for their custodian before counting on the onboarding time savings in capacity plans.
WealthManagement.com

Edward Jones builds an AI retirement-plan platform with Aboon for its advisors

Edward Jones is building an end-to-end workplace retirement-plan platform with Aboon, an AI-driven third-party administrator, and Retirement Plan Advisory Group, WealthManagement.com reported on September 30. It covers prospecting through plan servicing inside the advisor workstation and works with any recordkeeper or administrator. The prospecting module is in beta with advisors, with broader rollout expected early next year. Edward Jones advisors serve about 263,000 workplace plans with nearly $107 billion of assets. Edward Jones Ventures invested in Aboon’s $17.5 million seed round.

Why it matters: Small-plan 401(k) business is labor-heavy, and AI administration lets generalist advisors take it on at scale. Advisors competing for small-business owners should expect Edward Jones to pitch bundled plan and wealth relationships, and retirement-plan specialists should review whether their own recordkeeper and administrator stack can match the prospecting and servicing speed.
AI in Asset Management
Hedgeweek

Millennium gives all 7,000-plus employees a personal AI twin with its own audit trail

Millennium Management, the $97 billion multistrategy hedge fund, is rolling out a personal AI assistant, or digital twin, to more than 7,000 employees, Hedgeweek reported on September 30, citing Business Insider. About 1,600 are deployed, and 97% of the 150-plus pilot users used theirs daily. Each twin has its own identity, email address, permissions and audit trail, handles research, meeting prep and inbox triage, and cannot make decisions for its user. Millennium is now building team-level AI teammates.

Why it matters: Giving each agent its own identity and audit trail is a control model allocators can hold other managers to. Operational due-diligence teams should ask managers whether agents act under shared or individual credentials and how their actions are logged, and managers building internal assistants should copy the permission design before regulators ask.
Citi

Citi relaunches its custody Market Guide with AI search across 100-plus markets

Citi Investor Services relaunched its Market Guide platform at Sibos on September 29, adding AI-powered search, AI summaries and a redesigned interface. The post-trade tool consolidates local market practices, regulatory updates and settlement information across more than 100 custody markets, including 62 Citi proprietary branches. Clients can use it self-serve, through an API or white-labeled. It sits within Citi’s Custody+ suite. Chris Cox, global head of investor services, said AI is critical to Citi’s innovation agenda across Services.

Why it matters: Custodians are turning market-practice knowledge into queryable data, which cuts the manual research behind cross-border trading and settlement. Operations heads at global managers should test whether the API can feed their own agents and exception workflows, and compare it with BNY and State Street tools before custody reviews, since data access is becoming a selection criterion.
InvestmentNews

Arch launches an AI due-diligence tool for RIAs’ and family offices’ private-market deals

Arch, which runs private-markets portfolio monitoring for more than 650 allocators with over $600 billion of assets, launched Arch Investment Research on September 29, InvestmentNews reported. The tool reads offering documents, extracts key terms with links back to the source and flags items for review, then carries diligence files into monitoring after a deal closes. Early users reportedly cut diligence time by up to 50% per opportunity. Clients include four of the top 20 banks. It is sold standalone or bundled.

Why it matters: As RIAs and family offices add private funds, document review is the bottleneck that limits how many offerings a small team can screen. Alternatives teams should test the tool on recent subscription documents and check its term extraction against their own notes, since source-linked extraction is what makes an AI summary defensible in an investment-committee file.
