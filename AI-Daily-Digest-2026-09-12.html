Major AI Updates
Dario Amodei (Anthropic, essay)

Anthropic gives outside evaluators permanent access as Amodei urges labs to pace the frontier

Dario Amodei published "We Must Pace the Frontier" on September 12, committing Anthropic immediately to give independent evaluators such as METR permanent, employee-level access with the right to publish findings free of editorial control. The essay proposes common safety standards among frontier labs in democracies and government-to-government talks starting with a ban on AI-enabled bioweapons work. Sam Altman said OpenAI will match the evaluator access and Elon Musk posted "Dario is right," days after Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon resigned publicly.

Why it matters: For anyone underwriting Anthropic's expected fall IPO, this is a disclosed shift in the growth curve: slower capability releases in exchange for a lower catastrophic-risk premium. Model the revenue path on pricing and distribution rather than benchmark leaps. A cross-lab pacing pact would compress differentiation between labs — an argument for owning the compute and distribution layers instead.
Fortune

Altman rules out a 2026 OpenAI IPO, citing an 'ill-advised moment' on safety

Sam Altman told Fortune in an interview published September 12 that OpenAI will not go public in 2026, calling now "an ill-advised moment" given safety conditions and pointing to 2027 at the earliest. A listing had been discussed at roughly $1 trillion; the New York Times reported in June the company was leaning toward delay after SpaceX's $85 billion IPO spiked to $1.8 trillion and then fell. Altman said OpenAI has discussed pausing at new capability levels and may announce a cross-lab pact.

Why it matters: The 2026 mega-IPO calendar now rests on Anthropic alone, concentrating allocation demand and pricing power in one book. Wealth clients asking for OpenAI exposure are left with secondary tenders, SoftBank and Microsoft as proxies — review any private-market vehicle marketed on a near-term OpenAI listing, because its liquidity assumption just slipped a year.
Reuters

Senate negotiators draft duty-of-care bill letting Washington block unsafe frontier models

Reuters reported September 11 that Majority Leader John Thune, Commerce Chair Ted Cruz and Senators Amy Klobuchar and Maria Cantwell are negotiating legislation imposing a "duty of care" on developers of the most advanced models, with the government able to block a release subject to federal-court challenge. The draft would enlist national labs to test models for cyber, biological and nuclear uplift and would preempt state rules on those risks. There is no bill number or public text, and the House sits only one week before the November 3 midterms.

Why it matters: Federal pre-release review would formalize the two-tier access model Anthropic already runs and raise fixed compliance costs that favor the largest labs. Sector PMs should treat state-law preemption as the bargaining chip: a narrow federal standard that displaces California's rules is the outcome frontier-lab lobbying wants. Watch the lame-duck session for movement.
CNBC

Amazon can buy up to $60 billion of Qualcomm AI chips through 2036

Qualcomm said September 8 that Amazon Web Services can purchase up to $60 billion of custom AI inference chips, systems and optical connectivity through September 2036 under a multi-generation agreement. Amazon received warrants for 25 million Qualcomm shares at $161.26, worth about $4 billion, with 3.75 million vesting immediately and the rest tied to binding orders. Qualcomm shares rose about 10%, and the company reiterated a $15 billion data-center revenue target for 2029.

Why it matters: Hyperscalers are buying optionality on every non-Nvidia silicon path — Broadcom, Marvell, Positron and now Qualcomm — and paying with warrants rather than cash. For semis positioning, the equity-for-orders structure means customers, not just suppliers, capture upside from design wins; check whether AI-infrastructure sleeves hold the buyers of custom silicon as well as the sellers.
AI in Wealth Management
Envestnet (company announcement)

Envestnet to buy Vestmark, adding $2 trillion of wirehouse trading and tax accounts

Envestnet signed a definitive agreement on September 9 to acquire Vestmark, whose trading and tax-transition platform supports more than $2 trillion across five million accounts, with closing expected in the fourth quarter on undisclosed terms. Envestnet's own platforms carry roughly $8 trillion. No client migration is required: VestmarkONE, VAST, Tamarac and MoneyGuide continue on existing roadmaps, with incremental spending directed at AI workflows. The deal follows Envestnet's $35 million Tamarac investment within a $1 billion, five-year R&D plan and Vestmark's May launch of its Pulse AI portfolio-monitoring tool.

Why it matters: This puts institutional-grade tax management and direct indexing inside the platform a third of US advisors already use, and hands Bain-backed Envestnet a wirehouse client base it never had. RIAs on Tamarac should ask when Vestmark's tax-transition and Pulse tooling reach their contract; vendors pitching standalone tax overlays now face a bundled incumbent.
WealthManagement.com

Savvy Wealth raises $100 million Series C at a $600 million valuation

AI-native RIA Savvy Wealth announced a $100 million Series C on September 9 led by Halo Fund, the growth firm of Qualtrics founder Ryan Smith and Accel's Ryan Sweeney, with Thrive Capital, Goldman Sachs-owned Industry Ventures and Mark Casady's Vestigo returning. The $600 million valuation is 6.6 times the level of 15 months ago; total funding tops $200 million. Savvy manages $9 billion across 150 advisors, has recruited more than $4 billion in 2026, and is nearing $100 million of annual recurring revenue. Proceeds fund Savvy Intelligence agents built on OpenAI and Anthropic models.

Why it matters: Savvy is the clearest public mark on what an AI-native advisor platform is worth: roughly $67 million of equity value per billion of AUM, far above RIA-aggregator multiples. Recruiters at LPL, Raymond James and Ameriprise now compete against a venture-funded payout-plus-tooling pitch, and custodians Fidelity and Schwab gain a fast-growing integration client.
Luminary (company announcement)

BNY and Rockefeller back Luminary's $22 million round for AI estate data

Luminary closed a $22 million Series A on September 10 led by Ten Coves Capital, with BNY, 8VC, Fin Capital, Focus Financial Partners and Rockefeller Capital Management's FinTech Innovation Fund participating, taking total funding to nearly $32 million. The platform converts estate and ownership documents into structured data for scenario modeling, beneficiary and tax projections, exemption tracking and trust administration, and says it touches more than $500 billion of client assets. Proceeds go to AI capabilities, integrations, administration workflows and the commercial team.

Why it matters: Estate data is becoming distributor-owned infrastructure: Schwab backs Wealth.com, Vanguard and Edward Jones back Vanilla, Northwestern Mutual and UBS back Trust & Will, and now BNY and Rockefeller back Luminary. Advisors choosing an estate platform should weigh which custodian's ecosystem it will integrate with first, since strategic investors set the API priorities.
WealthManagement.com

Former LPL CEO Dan Arnold becomes executive chairman of AI-native Stirlingshire

Dan Arnold, removed as LPL Financial CEO in October 2024 for code-of-conduct violations, joined New York-based Stirlingshire Investments as executive chairman on September 11, with a remit covering advisor recruitment, operations and partnerships. Stirlingshire will launch Stirling One, an AI-native operating platform spanning onboarding, trading, rebalancing, tax optimization, compliance, CRM and reporting, at the Future Proof Festival next week. Advisors receive the platform free and keep 100% of payout; the firm runs RIA and broker-dealer entities with custody at Apex Fintech Solutions.

Why it matters: Arnold led LPL to a 537% shareholder return and knows which advisors are movable, so a 100%-payout, free-technology pitch with his name attached is a direct recruiting threat to independent broker-dealers. Due diligence will test Apex custody and an unproven platform, but expect LPL and Osaic to sharpen their own AI narratives at Future Proof.
AI in Asset Management
T. Rowe Price (investor relations)

T. Rowe Price puts Claude in front of portfolio managers and analysts

T. Rowe Price and Anthropic announced on September 10 that the $1.87 trillion manager is expanding Claude across its investment organization: portfolio managers and analysts use Claude and Cowork for research synthesis and multistep workflows, while developers adopt Claude Code to build investment tools. The rollout runs under the AI leadership model announced in August, with AI leads embedded in Investments and Global Distribution. CIO Eric Veiel said Claude lets analysts "cover more ground and go deeper." T. Rowe portfolios invest in Anthropic.

Why it matters: This is the first top-10 active manager to name a single frontier vendor for the research function itself rather than operations, and it lands after $7.9 billion of August net outflows. Expect T. Rowe to sell AI-augmented research as an active-management defense; peers without a named model partner will now get the question from consultants and platform gatekeepers.
Bloomberg (Odd Lots)

Bridgewater's Greg Jensen says AI already outperforms human equity analysts

Bridgewater co-CIO Greg Jensen told Bloomberg's Odd Lots on September 11 that AI systems now ingest filings and unstructured disclosures and produce forward estimates better than human equity analysts. An early backer of OpenAI and Anthropic, Jensen put the odds of a major AI-driven financial or physical disaster within a few years "way higher than anybody should be comfortable with," compared the moment to February 2020, and called for developer liability plus a "token tax" to offset Bridgewater's estimate that 18% of US jobs could be displaced within five years.

Why it matters: When the CIO of the largest macro fund says the analyst function is automated, sell-side research budgets and the junior-analyst pipeline are the assets being repriced. Allocators should ask managers how much fundamental coverage is now model-generated and how it is validated; Jensen's liability and token-tax proposals are also a policy risk for AI-heavy portfolios.
Defiance ETFs (company announcement)

Defiance lists equal-weight 'AI Magnificent 10' ETF spanning the chip supply chain

Defiance ETFs launched the AI Magnificent 10 ETF (Nasdaq: AIMG) on September 10, tracking a BITA index of ten equal-weighted names across accelerators, custom ASICs, foundries, memory, networking and photonics: Nvidia, Broadcom, Alphabet, TSMC, Samsung, SK hynix, Micron, Marvell, Lumentum and Coherent. The fund charges 0.61%, rebalances quarterly and may use options and swaps extensively to keep regulated-investment-company status. Defiance pitched the launch on Nvidia's $96.2 billion quarter and its estimate that the top hyperscalers will spend $1.3 trillion on capex next year.

Why it matters: A ten-stock, semis-concentrated fund with derivative overlays is a tactical vehicle, not core exposure, and equal weighting means the memory and optics names swing it as much as Nvidia does. For clients already holding QQQ or SMH, AIMG doubles down on the same names; its real use is expressing a hardware-over-software view without single-stock risk.
