Scope

This tracks how peers use AI in wealth management, split into internal (advisor productivity, compliance, operations) and client-facing (personalized insights, digital advice, agent access). It covers live production tools and announced pilots, with dates, so a line can be re-checked as things ship.

Three buckets: wirehouses and private banks (Morgan Stanley, UBS, JPMorgan, Bank of America/Merrill, Citi, Wells Fargo, with Goldman as the comparator), RIAs and independents (IBDs, aggregators, large RIAs, and the custodians and vendors they buy from), and fintechs pushing into wealth (brokerages, robos, AI-native RIAs, and the AI labs themselves). Every row links to the page the claim was taken from; rows marked "unverified" are hearsay or blocked pages.

A weekday scan at 7am ET updates the tables and logs each change in the change log; the executive takeaways are refreshed monthly by hand, not by the scan.

Executive Takeaways

The earlier read holds: meeting-workflow automation is table stakes across all three buckets, and client-facing AI is where differentiation now sits. Three things changed in the last six months that sharpen it.

1. Client-facing has moved from "none" to "two live and one open door." Citi is the most aggressive wirehouse: Portfolio Intelligence went live for Private Bank clients in April 2026 and reaches all Citi Wealth clients by year-end, and Citi Sky, a voice-and-avatar agent on Google DeepMind, is rolling to Citigold from summer 2026. Morgan Stanley took a different route: rather than build a client chatbot, it opened Shareworks and Equity Edge to clients' own AI agents via MCP (early access June 2026, full rollout 2027). JPMorgan has stated intent only; BofA, UBS, and Wells have nothing client-facing in wealth.

2. "Bring your own agent" is now a client expectation being set by brokerages, not banks. Robinhood (~100k agentic accounts, >$100M in custody by Q2 2026), Public, and five other US brokers let Claude or ChatGPT read and trade accounts through MCP. ChatGPT Finances (May 2026) and Perplexity already read Schwab, Fidelity, and Chase balances via Plaid whether or not those firms cooperate. Schwab and Fidelity have no official agent access. The question for any private bank is no longer whether to build a chatbot but whether to let the client's agent in, and on what terms.

3. The internal layer has converged on one pattern, and Goldman is behind it. Every wirehouse now has a notetaker plus a knowledge assistant on a single platform: Morgan Stanley (Assistant + Debrief, 98% of FA teams), BofA (Meeting Journey, ~15,000 employees, March 2026), Citi (CitiScribe + AskWealth), UBS (STAAT + Copilot, 95% of US advisors use some AI), Wells (AI Teammate, July 2026). Independents got there faster by buying Jump or Zocks; 70% of $1B+ RIAs use AI notetaking (Cerulli). No source describes a wealth-specific AI product at Goldman; the public record is GS AI Assistant, Banker Copilot, and the Anthropic ops agents.

Where the next differentiation is forming. Three vectors are visible: agentic execution behind a human approval gate (Morgan Stanley's "super agents," LPL Cyan, Dynasty's Claude-based AI OS, Mercer Aspen 2.0); growth and wallet-share analytics rather than notes (UBS STAAT's 20M opportunities in 2025, JPMorgan Coach AI's +20% gross sales claim); and the AI-native RIAs (Farther at $23B recruited assets, Savvy at $600M valuation, Compound at $5B+) winning advisors and their HNW books on margin, not clients on a persona. Vanguard's ~$4B acquisition of Altruist and Schwab's Claude for Financial Advisors deal (September 2026) mean custodians are becoming the AI distribution layer for the RIA channel.

Implication for Goldman. The internal layer is a catch-up exercise with a known recipe (notetaker plus knowledge assistant on one platform, measured by adoption). The open decisions are client-facing: a Citi-style owned agent for the mass-affluent tier, a Morgan Stanley-style open door for UHNW clients' own agents and family offices, or both. No firm in bucket 1 has yet paired either with the growth-analytics layer, which is the combination none of the six has shipped.

Vendor layer. Jump (35,000+ users, $80M Series B, 22.7% T3 share) and Zocks (5,000+ firms, $45M Series B) are extending from notes into account opening, scheduling, and book-scanning agents, which is why Salesforce answered with Agentic Advisor. Orion Denali AI, Envestnet's R3 2026 release (AI dashboard assistant, Tamarac Data Analyst Agent; Sept 18), eMoney CoPlanner, and Nitrogen Nucleus are the planning-and-platform layer; Holistiplan holds ~39% of tax-planning share.

Adoption. 63% of Schwab-custodied RIAs use AI, up from under 30% in 2023, but only ~10% call it fully integrated (Schwab 2026 RIA & AI Study). 42.9% of practices report an AI notetaker in the T3 2026 survey; 70% of $1B+ RIAs per Cerulli. 64% of wealth firms lack a unified data layer for AI (F2 Strategy), which is the real constraint on anything beyond notes.

Client-facing. Nothing in this bucket is client self-service. The closest are Ameriprise's AI-drafted client letters, advisor-mediated proposals from Dynasty, Mercer, and LPL Cyan, and Edward Jones' venture tools reaching clients through advisor pilots. Client-facing AI is a 2027 story here.

Bucket 3: Fintechs

This is where client-facing AI actually lives. The credible pressure on HNW and UHNW incumbents comes from three directions: brokerages normalizing agent access to accounts, AI-native RIAs pulling advisors and their books out of wirehouses, and custodians and AI labs bundling advisor AI into the RIA channel. Fully AI-only advice remains small and mass-affluent.

Row-per-tool detail with facing and segment columns: Tool detail.

Threat assessment. Credible near-term pressure on HNW incumbents: Farther, Savvy, and Compound (advisor and book migration on AI-driven margin, not client disintermediation); Vanguard plus Altruist (a low-cost incumbent owning advisor AI and custody for ~20k RIAs); Robinhood's ladder from Gold to "private banking" to the TradePMR RIA channel; and Morgan Stanley's open MCP door raising what HNW clients expect. Unproven at HNW: Range, Arta, and the SEC-registered AI advisors. Mass-market noise for UHNW purposes: SoFi Coach, Betterment, Boldin, Schwab Portfolio Insights, Fidelity Freya, and Ask IBKR, all informational and guardrailed.

The agent-access pattern. Read-only aggregation is mainstream (ChatGPT, Perplexity, SoFi over Plaid). Write and trade access via MCP is live at seven US brokers plus Scalable; Schwab and Fidelity have none. Brokerages disclaim supervision of the agent ("Public does not control, supervise, monitor, recommend, or audit any third-party AI agent"). Altruist's CEO is publicly warning that rivals will refuse API access, so data-access fights will decide who can deliver agentic advice.

Regulatory posture. The SEC withdrew the predictive-analytics rule in 2025; Chair Atkins (Sept 2026) treats AI as a tool under existing fiduciary and Reg BI rules with enforcement aimed at AI-washing. The FINRA 2026 report adds an agentic-AI section (scope, traceability, human-in-the-loop) but does not address AI recommendations to retail, a gap firms use via "information, not advice" disclaimers or segregated agent sub-accounts.
