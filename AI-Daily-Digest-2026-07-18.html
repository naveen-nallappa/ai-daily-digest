Bloomberg

Moonshot ships Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter open-weight frontier model

China's Moonshot AI released Kimi K3 on July 16, a 2.8-trillion-parameter multimodal reasoning model it calls the largest open-weight system shipped to date, with a one-million-token context window and always-on reasoning. It went live through Kimi's app and API at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output, with full open weights due by July 27. Bloomberg reported the model trails only Anthropic's Claude Fable 5 and OpenAI's GPT-5.6 on overall capability.

Why it matters: An open-weight model within reach of the US frontier resets the cost curve for any firm deploying AI internally. Technology teams weighing build-versus-buy should price self-hosted Kimi K3 against frontier API contracts, and allocators holding US-lab exposure should watch whether cheap, near-frontier open weights compress the pricing power baked into those private valuations.

Google delays flagship Gemini 3.5 Pro after a ground-up model rebuild

Google has slipped the launch of Gemini 3.5 Pro, its most powerful model, by months after internal testing showed weak coding and long-horizon reasoning, Bloomberg reported July 16. Quant funds surrender a quarter of their 2026 gains as crowded AI trades unwind

Systematic hedge funds were among the hardest hit in a late-June and early-July reversal, with average quant returns falling to 10.8% for the year from 14.4% on June 22, according to industry data reported by Hedgeweek. Losses clustered in heavily owned US equities and developed-Asian markets, amplified by sharp semiconductor swings and elevated leverage among South Korean investors. Why it matters: When many managers run similar models on the same crowded book, diversification you thought you owned can vanish in a drawdown. Allocators should look through headline strategy labels to underlying position overlap, stress-test how correlated their systematic sleeves are to the AI-momentum complex, and treat the speed of this deleveraging as evidence that crowding, not thesis, drove the losses.

Vanguard's $93 billion Strategic Equity Fund draws half its signals from machine learning

Vanguard's Quantitative Equity Group has rebuilt stock selection for its roughly $93 billion Strategic Equity Fund around an ensemble of dozens of machine-learning models tracking tens of millions of financial and economic interactions. Senior portfolio manager Cesar Orosco describes a blend in which half the inputs come from the team's decades-old fundamental approach and half from machine learning. The group frames the goal as replicating a good fundamental investor's judgment systematically and at scale across small- and mid-cap names.

Why it matters: A low-cost giant folding machine learning into a mainstream active fund signals the technique is migrating from hedge-fund alpha into everyday products retail investors already hold. Advisors doing due diligence should ask managers how much of a strategy's process is now model-driven, how those models are validated, and whether the machine-learning overlay changes the fund's factor tilts or turnover.
Bloomberg

Wall Street researchers warn AI could shrink a trading edge to eighteen months

A profitable trading signal could lose half its excess return in about 18 months, down from five to seven years before AI became widespread, according to research highlighted by Bloomberg on July 1. The authors argue each new AI entrant shortens the lifespan of every exploitable pattern at an increasing rate as models converge on the same trades. The same work found AI strategies vulnerable to manipulation, with one model's return dropping about 18 points after a single doctored headline.

Why it matters: If alpha decays this fast, the edge shifts from having a model to constantly refreshing it — and to guarding data inputs against adversarial manipulation. PMs building AI-driven strategies should budget for continuous retraining rather than treating a backtest as durable, and risk teams should add headline- and data-poisoning scenarios to model validation, since one corrupted input can swing returns materially.
