Nvidia's backstop for OpenAI's Ohio data center lands at $105 billion

Regulatory filings published August 18 fix Nvidia's financing commitment behind OpenAI's PORTS-Pike campus in Pike County, Ohio at $105 billion — roughly $145 billion below the ~$250 billion figure in early reports and under the sub-$120 billion level circulating last week. The credit supports an initial 4.25 gigawatts of computing capacity with an option for a further 3.75 gigawatts, under a 20-year OpenAI lease. SB Energy will build and manage the site, with capacity phasing in from 2028.

Why it matters: The negotiated shrinkage is the clearest data point yet that pricing power in AI infrastructure is shifting from chip suppliers to buyers, and Fortune reads it as a check on artificial chip demand. Anyone underwriting data-center paper or AI capex forecasts should treat final filed commitments, not headline announcements, as the demand signal.
European Central Bank

ECB economists say the AI stock boom is primed for a correction

An ECB blog published August 17, "The AI boom: rational enthusiasm or the next dot-com bubble?", argues a significant pullback is increasingly likely through two channels. Overconfident investors may bid prices past fundamentals and reprice sharply when sentiment fades. Alternatively, even if AI delivers, spreading adoption makes uncertainty economy-wide, pushing required risk premia — and therefore valuations — down despite robust profit growth. The authors cite the 1840s railway mania, the 1920s electrification boom and the late-1990s internet bubble.

Why it matters: The second path is the uncomfortable one for client conversations: a risk-premium repricing hits the whole index, so rotating between AI winners offers little protection. Advisors fielding client demands for more AI exposure now have a central-bank-authored, citable framework for position sizing that does not depend on calling the technology wrong.
OpenAI

OpenAI began its global rollout of ChatGPT for Teens on August 18

OpenAI started rolling out ChatGPT for Teens on August 18 to eligible users aged 13 to 17 on free and paid personal plans. The mode switches on automatically based on declared, verified or predicted age, and layers in reduced graphic content, restrictions on romantic or violent roleplay, and optional parental controls covering study hours, quiet hours, voice mode and memory. High-risk conversations can trigger a parental safety notification after review by trained personnel, though parents cannot read their teen's chats.

Why it matters: This is the template regulators will point to when they ask financial firms how client-facing AI gates access by identity: automated attribute prediction, feature-level restrictions and human-reviewed escalation. Firms building client chat tools should expect suitability-style gating — different capabilities for different client classes — to become the supervisory expectation, not an optional design choice.

FINNY ties pricing to advisor growth as LPL adds it to preferred vendors

AI prospecting platform FINNY announced a "Pay-as-You-Grow" model on August 17: advisors pay $50 a month for platform access plus an annual fee on assets from clients FINNY sources — about 20 basis points for the average LPL advisor, tiering down to 12.5 basis points at scale. The structure replaces flat subscriptions of $6,000 to $12,000 a year. LPL Financial has added FINNY to its preferred vendor list, opening distribution across its advisor network.

Why it matters: This imports the custodial referral-program economics advisors already know into AI vendor pricing, with the vendor now sharing growth risk. Advisors comparing prospecting tools should model the all-in cost at their expected asset velocity — AUM-based fees outrun flat SaaS quickly on large wins — and confirm how sourced-client fees are disclosed to those clients.

JPMorgan says AI concentration now spans credit benchmarks, not just equities

JPMorgan Asset Management strategist Gabriela Santos warned on August 18 that "AI tentacle" risk has spread beyond equities into fixed income. Hyperscalers funding infrastructure capex with debt are reshaping investment-grade credit benchmarks, lifting the AI-linked share of passive bond exposure. Santos said the super-cycle thesis remains intact but cautioned: "You can be very bullish on all things AI and still need to think very carefully on portfolio construction."

Why it matters: Fixed-income allocators using index products may now hold materially more AI-linked credit than their equity-side concentration limits would ever permit. PMs should quantify AI-issuer weight across aggregate and IG benchmarks, and consider issuer caps or active overlays — the diversification a bond sleeve is supposed to provide is quietly eroding as issuance mixes shift.

Jane Street leads $700 million round doubling chipmaker Etched to $21 billion

Inference-chip startup Etched raised $700 million on August 18 at a $21 billion valuation, up from $10.3 billion in its July 23 Series C. Quant trading firm Jane Street led the round, joined by Kleiner Perkins, Sequoia, Andreessen Horowitz, Tiger Global and Blackstone. Jane Street is also Etched's first customer: it received its first rack last month and is deploying the hardware in live workloads. Etched reports more than $1 billion in customer contracts, with its first prototype off TSMC's line this year.

Why it matters: A top quant firm is buying its inference edge at the silicon layer — leading the round, taking first delivery and running production workloads. Compute procurement is becoming a source of alpha in systematic trading, and allocators diligencing quant managers should start asking about hardware strategy the way they ask about data sourcing and execution.
