Major AI Updates
Akamai IR

Akamai signs $11.6 billion, 7-year Anthropic compute deal with equity warrants

Akamai and Anthropic said on September 25 that Anthropic has committed $11.6 billion over seven years for Akamai's CPU cloud capacity, an agreement expandable toward $20 billion. Akamai granted Anthropic warrants for up to 5% of its stock, 7.7 million shares at a $111.33 strike, tied to spending milestones. Akamai plans roughly $5.5 billion of capex to build the capacity and expects deal revenue to reach a $1.7 billion annual pace by the end of 2028. Akamai shares rose as much as 22% on the news.

Why it matters: A supplier granting equity to its customer is a new financing pattern for AI capex, and PMs holding Akamai should model dilution against the warrant schedule alongside the revenue ramp. The deal also confirms compute demand is spilling from hyperscalers into second-tier cloud providers, widening the universe of infrastructure names tied to frontier-lab spending decisions.
Official Microsoft Blog

Microsoft relaunches Copilot with a Code tool and Autopilot agent

Microsoft unveiled a revamped Copilot on September 25, adding a Code tool that builds apps and dashboards from natural-language prompts using GitHub Copilot technology, with early access at month's end and broader 365 Premium and Pro access later in 2026. Autopilot, an expanded version of the "Scout" agent announced in June, enters private preview at month's end with its own directory identity and customizable permissions. Word, Excel and PowerPoint are now embedded directly inside Copilot, alongside new tools for tracking AI usage costs.

Why it matters: An enterprise-grade identity and permissioning layer for an autonomous agent, plus built-in usage-cost tracking, is a governance template compliance teams should benchmark against the Claude and Gemini rollouts already inside wealth and asset managers. The Office-embedded workflow also raises the bar for advisor-facing copilots competing for the same drafting and analysis tasks.
Nextgov/FCW

Casar-Sanders bill would ban superintelligence and create a federal AI agency

Rep. Greg Casar and Sen. Bernie Sanders introduced the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act the week of September 22, which would create a Department of Artificial Intelligence, pause advanced AI development until it exists, and permanently ban systems capable of exceeding human cognitive performance across most domains. Sen. Ed Markey introduced a companion Cybersecurity and AI Board of Investigations Act modeled on the NTSB to probe AI-driven cyber incidents. Sens. Michael Bennet and Peter Welch expanded an earlier Federal Digital Commission proposal requiring pre-certification of frontier models, with penalties up to 15% of global revenue.

Why it matters: None of these bills has a plausible path through this Congress, but the 15%-of-global-revenue penalty and NTSB-style incident board are drafting templates likely to resurface in state legislation and future federal proposals. Compliance teams should track the Federal Digital Commission's pre-certification language specifically, since it would apply to any frontier model licensed for advisor use.
AI in Wealth Management
InvestmentNews

Northwestern Mutual gives 22,000 advisors access to Jump's AI suite

Northwestern Mutual is rolling out Jump's AI operating system, including meeting capture, proactive client-need alerts and field-practice analytics, to roughly 22,000 financial representatives across its home office and 85 regional offices. It is one of Jump's largest enterprise deployments, joining Cetera, Focus Financial, Principal Financial Group and Allianz as enterprise clients; Jump has raised $105 million total, including an $80 million Series B led by Insight Partners. Jump now counts more than 35,000 advisor users and roughly 22.7% share of the T3 tech-adoption survey.

Why it matters: A near-doubling of Jump's addressable advisor base through one enterprise contract strengthens its position as the default meeting-notes layer at broker-dealers and insurance-affiliated channels alike. Firms evaluating Jump against Zocks or Zeplyn should ask for Northwestern Mutual's rollout timeline and compliance-control specifics before their own renewal or RFP cycle.
InvestCloud

InvestCloud and Apollo launch Altic, an AI-native private-markets order network

InvestCloud said on September 23 that it is investing more than $50 million in Altic, an AI-native, rules-based network that routes, validates and manages the lifecycle of private-market orders, unifying public and private assets inside its $4.2 trillion APL managed-account platform. Apollo Global Management is the founding partner, with Apollo's Stephanie Drescher framing the network as expanding general partners' access to wealth-management distribution channels. Private-market assets on APL stand at $8 billion today; InvestCloud targets Altic's core services live by year-end 2026.

Why it matters: A major custodial-tech platform and a top alternative manager building shared plumbing for private-markets orders is the infrastructure layer that determines how fast wealth advisors can actually allocate to interval funds and evergreen vehicles at scale. Platform teams should ask whether their own custodian or TAMP has a seat at Altic before committing to a competing private-markets workflow.
Snappy Kraken

Snappy Kraken launches Claude-powered marketing coworker for advisors

Snappy Kraken launched Snappy AI on September 24, a Claude-powered, read-only assistant that analyzes an advisor's campaign activity and CRM-connection status, flags follow-up gaps and recommends next steps without executing campaigns or changing settings. It is included with monthly credits for qualifying subscriptions. Company research covering more than 9,000 advisors found only about 25% connect their CRM to the platform, but those who do see three to four times more client engagement.

Why it matters: The read-only, no-execution design is a template compliance teams should look for in marketing-AI tools generally, since it limits supervision burden versus agents that can send communications unsupervised. The CRM-connection gap Snappy Kraken cites also flags an adoption bottleneck worth raising with any advisor-facing AI vendor before assuming the tool gets used.
AI in Asset Management
Bloomberg

Citadel reports near-full AI adoption among its stock-picking teams

Citadel's chief technology officer Umesh Subramanian said the $71 billion hedge fund has reached close to full adoption of an internal AI chatbot across its fundamental equity teams, first rolled out in 2025 to extract filing details, summarize sell-side research and track executive keyword mentions. There is no mandate to use it, yet adoption is near-universal. Subramanian cautioned against "offloading human investment judgment to AI," calling it a research accelerant rather than a PM replacement. Peers including Balyasny, Man Group and Viking Global run similar internal tools.

Why it matters: Near-universal voluntary adoption at a top-five multi-strategy fund is a benchmark LPs can use against managers who still describe AI as experimental. The explicit "no offloading judgment" guardrail is also language allocators should ask every manager to put in writing before treating AI-assisted returns as repeatable alpha.
Bloomberg

Goldman lifts 2027 hyperscaler AI capex forecast to $1.2 trillion

Goldman Sachs raised its 2027 capital-expenditure forecast for the five largest US hyperscalers — Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft, Oracle and Meta — to $1.2 trillion, a 54% increase over 2026 and above prior Wall Street estimates. The bank separately projected more than a third of that 2027 spending will be debt-financed rather than funded from free cash flow, a shift from the mostly cash- and equity-funded capex of the last two years. The forecast covers data-center construction, chips and related AI infrastructure.

Why it matters: A shift toward debt-financed capex changes the leverage and credit-spread profile of mega-cap tech balance sheets that anchor most equity benchmarks. Fixed-income and multi-asset PMs should model hyperscaler credit spreads against this financing mix rather than treat the capex figure as a pure equity-multiple input, and should watch investment-grade issuance calendars into 2027.
The Information

Apollo-backed Stream in talks to lease Anthropic 1 gigawatt for $40 billion

Anthropic is negotiating to directly lease up to 1 gigawatt of data-center capacity from Stream Data Centers, majority-owned by Apollo Global Management, in a deal tied to at least $40 billion of associated investment, The Information reported September 25. The structure would bypass AWS and Google Cloud, Anthropic's two largest cloud partners, making it the lab's largest direct infrastructure commitment to date. It follows Apollo and Blackstone's roughly $35 billion private-credit deal in June to finance Anthropic's AI chip purchases.

Why it matters: Apollo underwriting both the debt and now the real-estate lease behind Anthropic's compute buildout concentrates AI infrastructure risk inside one alternative manager's balance sheet and its permanent-capital vehicles. Allocators in Apollo-sponsored credit and infrastructure funds should ask for AI-tenant concentration disclosure the same way they would for a single-tenant real-estate exposure.
