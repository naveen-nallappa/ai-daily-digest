OpenAI Model Autonomously Disproves an 80-Year-Old Geometry Conjecture

OpenAI said on May 20 that one of its internal general-purpose reasoning models autonomously disproved the Erdős unit-distance conjecture, a discrete-geometry problem open since 1946. The model produced a 125-page proof, approaching the question through algebraic number theory rather than the grid arrangements mathematicians long assumed optimal. Fields medalist Tim Gowers called the result "a milestone in AI mathematics," and Princeton's Will Sawin quantified the improvement in a companion paper. The model was not trained for the problem and received only the problem statement.

Why it matters: This is the first time an AI system has independently cracked a prominent open problem at the center of a mathematical field, not just a competition benchmark. PMs sizing AI exposure should treat genuine research-grade reasoning as a capability now demonstrated rather than promised — a data point that strengthens the bull case behind richly valued model labs and their pre-IPO marks.

Poisoned VS Code Extension Lets Hackers Steal 3,800 GitHub Repositories

GitHub confirmed on May 20 that threat group TeamPCP exfiltrated roughly 3,800 internal repositories after an employee installed a trojanized version of the Nx Console VS Code extension, which was live on Microsoft's marketplace for just 18 minutes on May 18. The malicious build silently harvested GitHub tokens, AWS keys, npm credentials, and developer secrets. The same supply-chain campaign also compromised developer devices at OpenAI and AI startup Mistral.

Why it matters: Supply-chain attacks now enter through the routine packages and extensions developers install, a layer traditional endpoint security barely covers. Technology and compliance leads at advisory and asset-management firms should audit which third-party developer tools and AI coding assistants touch internal systems, shorten credential lifetimes, and confirm vendor due diligence covers extension marketplaces, not just hosted software.

As elite quant firms embed AI across research, senior quants are warning that broad adoption may make markets faster without making them more efficient. Goldman Sachs Asset Management's Osman Ali argues that when many investors query similar models on similar data, the models generate correlated answers — and correlated trades. Citadel has launched an AI assistant for equities investors, trained on filings, transcripts, and brokerage research, while stressing that final investment judgment stays with humans.
