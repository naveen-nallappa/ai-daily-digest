SpaceX Strikes $60 Billion All-Stock Deal to Buy Cursor Maker Anysphere

On June 16, SpaceX confirmed a $60 billion all-stock acquisition of Anysphere, the startup behind AI coding tool Cursor — the most expensive AI software deal announced to date. Cursor had reached roughly $2.6 billion in annualized business revenue, and the transaction folds the code editor into xAI, which SpaceX absorbed in February. SpaceX said it will not use proceeds from its recent IPO to fund the purchase, which is expected to close in the third quarter of 2026.

Why it matters: AI coding is the first enterprise category turning models into hard revenue, and a $60 billion sticker resets takeout multiples across developer tooling. PMs holding enterprise-software or AI names should reassess acquisition premiums and how Musk's combined SpaceX-xAI entity, now among the most valuable US companies, reshapes the competitive map against Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic.

Magnetar Plans AI Fund That Replaces Analyst Teams With Hundreds of Bots

Magnetar Capital, an $18 billion hedge fund firm, is preparing a fund that uses hundreds of AI bots in place of traditional equity-research analysts, Bloomberg reported June 9. The bots source ideas, analyze companies and generate recommendations, while human portfolio managers retain final decisions and trade execution. Why it matters: An established multi-strategy manager — not a startup — industrializing AI research signals the buy side is moving from copilots to agent fleets that displace headcount. Allocators should ask incumbent managers how AI reshapes their cost base, capacity and key-person risk, and whether research-heavy fee levels still hold up once bots handle first-pass analysis.

Lumenai Readies First Institutional Hedge Fund Built on Fully Agentic AI

Lumenai Investments is preparing to launch what it calls the first institutional hedge fund built around a fully agentic AI architecture, with operations targeted to begin around June 1. The global equity long-short strategy inverts the usual setup: autonomous agents generate, evaluate and manage ideas continuously, while humans retain governance and risk oversight. Lumenai is developing the system with ETS Asset Management Factory, a quant research group that has operated since the late 1980s.

Why it matters: A fund that puts agents at the center of the process — not as a tool inside a human framework — is a live test of whether AI can run a strategy end to end. Allocators weighing AI-native managers should probe how Lumenai attributes performance, controls model drift and documents human oversight, since early track records will shape institutional comfort with delegating alpha generation to machines.
