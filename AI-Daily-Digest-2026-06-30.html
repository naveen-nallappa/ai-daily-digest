OpenAI Previews GPT-5.6 Sol, Terra, and Luna to Roughly 20 Vetted Partners

OpenAI on June 26 began a limited preview of GPT-5.6, a three-model family spanning the flagship Sol, the balanced Terra, and the low-cost Luna. Access is restricted to about 20 vetted organizations after OpenAI shared the models with the U.S. government, following a June 2 executive order on benchmarking new AI systems. Terra matches GPT-5.5 performance at roughly half the price; pricing runs from Luna at $1/$6 per million tokens to Sol at $5/$30. General availability is expected within weeks.

Why it matters: Tiered model families let buyers match cost to task rather than overpaying a flagship for routine work, and Luna and Terra undercut prior pricing sharply. Teams budgeting AI spend should map each workload to the cheapest adequate tier, but note the government-gated rollout means production access and vendor SLAs remain weeks away for most enterprises.

Colorado's First-in-Nation AI Act Reaches Its June 30 Effective Date

Colorado's AI Act, the first comprehensive U.S. state AI law, hit its June 30 effective date, requiring developers and deployers of high-risk systems to use reasonable care against algorithmic discrimination and to run impact assessments. Financial services — including credit, pricing, and account decisions — is an in-scope "consequential decision" domain. Lawmakers, however, passed SB 189 in May to repeal and replace the statute with a lighter, disclosure-based framework, leaving compliance teams tracking which obligations actually bind.

Why it matters: Wealth and lending firms using AI in client-facing decisions now face a live compliance trigger, even as the rules may soon shift. Compliance teams should inventory where models drive consequential decisions and document impact assessments, while avoiding over-building to a standard SB 189 could relax within months — keeping disclosure controls ready regardless of the outcome.

Asset Managers File First 'MANGOS' ETFs Tracking the AI Megacaps

Yorkville America and Corgi Securities filed with the SEC on June 15 for the first ETFs built around the "MANGOS" basket — Meta, Anthropic, Nvidia, Alphabet, OpenAI, and SpaceX — pitched as the AI era's answer to FAANG. Because Anthropic and OpenAI are private, the funds would capture them mostly by proxy and derivatives, using the up-to-15% illiquid-securities allowance for direct stakes. Yorkville's versions add seven chip and hardware names plus an options-income sleeve. Trading could begin as early as August.

Why it matters: Packaging private AI leaders into a 40-Act wrapper hands retail and advisors synthetic exposure to names they cannot otherwise buy — with basis risk, derivative costs, and illiquidity baked in. Due-diligence teams should scrutinize how much "OpenAI" or "Anthropic" exposure is real versus proxied, and treat tracking error as the core risk before allocating.
