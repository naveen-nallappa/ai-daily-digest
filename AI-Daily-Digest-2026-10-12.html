Major AI Updates
Bloomberg News (via Bloomberg Tax)

OpenAI’s run rate nears $50 billion, about $20 billion below figures investors heard

OpenAI’s annualized revenue was nearing $50 billion at the end of September, Bloomberg reported on October 8, following a Financial Times report. Some outlets had put the run rate near $70 billion last month. The gap largely reflects accounting: OpenAI books only its net share of sales through cloud partners, while Anthropic books gross. Bloomberg reported on October 9 that OpenAI still expects a run rate of $70 billion or more by year-end. The Nasdaq fell 1.25% on October 8.

Why it matters: Private AI revenue figures are not comparable across labs until accounting is standardized. PMs and advisors sizing exposure to OpenAI or Anthropic through private funds, SPVs or swap-based ETFs should ask managers which basis their marks use, and treat media run-rate figures as unaudited until the Anthropic IPO prospectus and any OpenAI filing set a common reference point.
Bloomberg

Nadella says firms must treat AI models as insider risks with an emergency brake

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella published an essay on October 10, “Models as Insider Risks in the Super Intelligence Era,” arguing that companies cannot outsource responsibility for what AI does on their behalf. He called for controls that sit outside the model: least-privilege access, tamper-proof activity logs, independent validation, more than one model for important outcomes, and an authorized person able to pause or stop a model mid-task. He also urged timely incident disclosure to affected parties.

Why it matters: The essay maps directly onto supervision duties firms already carry. Compliance and technology teams deploying advisor copilots or agents should confirm each agent has its own scoped credentials, that action logs are kept outside the vendor’s system, that a named person can halt it, and that a second model or a human checks client-facing output before it goes out.
Bloomberg

Goldman’s Sneader says new hires will supervise AI agents from day one

Kevin Sneader, head of Goldman Sachs’ Asia Pacific business, told the Milken Institute Asia Summit in Singapore on October 8 that new finance hires will manage AI agents from the start of their careers, with direct reports that are software, Bloomberg reported. Chief information officer Marco Argenti has argued junior staff must learn to specify, hand off and oversee AI tasks. Goldman has given about 12,000 engineers Cognition’s Devin coding agent and cites productivity gains above 20%.

Why it matters: The apprenticeship model is changing at a top-five firm. Wealth teams that rely on associates and paraplanners learning by doing should redesign training around reviewing and correcting agent output, and decide which tasks juniors must still do by hand so they can catch the errors an agent makes in client plans or proposals.
AI in Wealth Management
Reuters (via Khaleej Times), citing the Financial Times

HSBC plans to cut about 70% of UK wealth advisers in an AI push

HSBC plans widespread job cuts in its UK wealth management business, the Financial Times reported on October 7, with reductions of roughly 70% among financial advisers and about half of management and specialist roles. The bank is consulting staff, and affected employees are expected to leave by the end of October. HSBC said it is evolving to deliver “more digitally enabled products and journeys.” HSBC UK targets £100 billion of assets under management by 2028, up from £62 billion at end-2025.

Why it matters: A global bank is betting it can grow wealth assets by 60% with far fewer advisers. US firms should watch whether HSBC’s client retention holds and test their own capacity models against it: which planning and servicing tasks AI can absorb, and where affluent clients still expect a named adviser. Recruiters should expect experienced UK advisers to come on the market.
InvestmentNews

Alaris CEO warns AI could lower valuations for RIAs slow to adopt it

Allen Darby, CEO of RIA M&A adviser Alaris Acquisitions, told InvestmentNews on October 7 that AI could let an advisor serve about 200 client relationships, up from 80 to 120 today, without hurting service. Buyers investing “tens of millions of dollars” in AI may treat less efficient sellers as overstaffed and price in two to three years of reduced profits to rightsize them. Orion’s survey of 571 advisors found 73% of firms use AI but only 6% run agentic workflows.

Why it matters: AI readiness is becoming a diligence item in RIA deals. Owners planning a sale in the next few years should document AI-driven capacity gains, clients per advisor and staffing ratios now, and buyers should model integration costs for low-adoption targets explicitly rather than assuming headcount savings will arrive for free after closing.
ThinkAdvisor

Fitch says wealth managers face more AI disruption risk than annuity issuers

Fitch Ratings analysts said annuity issuers should be safer than wealth managers from AI-driven disruption to business models, ThinkAdvisor reported on October 9. AI could make professionals more productive but also bring new competition, they said, with pressure on wealth managers spanning financial planning, portfolio construction, tax strategy and estate planning. In a March report, Fitch ranked retail advisory as the US financial segment most exposed to AI, while saying it expected no near-term rating effects.

Why it matters: A rating agency now formally ranks advice as the most AI-exposed part of financial services. Firms should expect analysts and acquirers to ask how their revenue mix holds up, and advisors may find that insurance, annuity and complex estate work, which rely on licensing and underwriting, are harder for AI tools to replicate than allocation and tax-loss harvesting.
AI in Asset Management
Google Cloud (press release)

Balyasny deploys Google Gemini across 200 investment teams for agentic research

Balyasny Asset Management and Google Cloud said on October 8 that Balyasny will use Gemini models inside its proprietary research platforms, including BAMAgent, its internal application for orchestrating models and agents. Gemini supports more than 200 investment teams and connects to more than 80 internal databases and tools, searching earnings transcripts, central bank statements and filings. Chief AI officer Charlie Flanagan cited Gemini’s throughput for high-volume document search. Balyasny manages over $37 billion.

Why it matters: Leading multi-managers are going multi-model rather than standardizing on one lab. Asset managers building research agents should design their orchestration layer to swap models by task and cost, and negotiate early-access and data-isolation terms, as Balyasny did with Google’s VPC controls, instead of locking their analysts into a single provider.
Bloomberg

JPMorgan and Goldman underwrite only $1.3 billion of Volta’s $5 billion AI loan

JPMorgan and Goldman Sachs, arranging a $5 billion loan for AI infrastructure startup Volta, are underwriting only a $1.3 billion term loan and leaving $3.7 billion of chip loans unguaranteed, Bloomberg reported on October 12. Banks normally underwrite whole financings. The unrated deal yields about 11%, among the highest costs in the leveraged loan market, and the missing rating typically keeps CLO buyers out. The loan is backed by an Anthropic lease.

Why it matters: When lead banks will not hold the risk on AI-hardware debt, end investors carry it. Credit PMs and allocators to private-credit and loan funds should check exposure to unrated GPU-backed loans, scrutinize lease counterparties and chip-collateral assumptions, and confirm an 11% yield prices obsolescence risk rather than just a shortage of willing lenders.
Wall Street Journal (via investingLive)

Broadcom seeks over $50 billion from Apollo and Blackstone to finance OpenAI chips

Broadcom is arranging more than $50 billion of financing for the custom AI chip it is building with OpenAI, the Wall Street Journal reported on October 7, with Apollo and Blackstone in talks to lend. The deal could cover several gigawatts of OpenAI capacity and is expected to close before year-end, though its size could change. Separately, Oracle is discussing a chip-leasing vehicle with Apollo and Goldman Sachs, and SpaceX has discussed $40 billion of financing for Nvidia chips.

Why it matters: Private credit is becoming the balance sheet for AI labs that cannot buy hardware outright. Allocators in Apollo and Blackstone credit vehicles should ask how much is concentrated in single-lab chip leases, and advisors placing clients in semi-liquid private-credit funds should check those funds’ AI-hardware exposure before the next redemption window.
