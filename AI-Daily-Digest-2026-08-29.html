Major AI Updates
CNBC

Federal judge strikes down the Pentagon's blacklisting of Anthropic as unlawful

U.S. District Judge Rita Lin ruled on August 28 that the Pentagon's designation of Anthropic as a national-security supply chain risk was "illegal and baseless," finding in a 59-page order that the government violated the company's First Amendment and due process rights. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth imposed the designation after Anthropic refused contract terms overriding its prohibitions on autonomous weapons and domestic surveillance. The government can appeal, and separate Anthropic litigation continues in Washington.

Why it matters: The ruling gives AI vendors legal cover to keep usage restrictions in place while still competing for government work — a precedent that reduces the discount investors were applying to labs with published red lines. For portfolios holding government-adjacent AI exposure, the procurement retaliation risk that surfaced in March just got a judicial check.
Marvell Investor Relations

Marvell posts a record $2.74 billion quarter but Google revenue waits until 2029

Marvell reported record fiscal Q2 revenue of $2.74 billion on August 27 with adjusted EPS of $0.94, guided the next quarter to $3.15 billion, and raised full-year outlooks for fiscal 2027 and 2028 on what CEO Matt Murphy called "exceptional AI-related bookings." Shares still fell roughly 8% Friday after Murphy said revenue from the custom-chip agreement with Google — worth up to $120 billion through fiscal 2033 — becomes substantially meaningful only in fiscal 2029.

Why it matters: The market is no longer valuing signed AI contracts as near-term revenue; it is discounting them by delivery date. PMs holding custom-silicon names should re-underwrite each backlog headline for timing, and the same discipline applies to data-center and power names whose valuations lean on multi-year commitments rather than shipped product.
Data Center Dynamics

Nvidia pauses its GPU cloud revenue-share backstop two months after launch

Nvidia has paused the financing program announced in July under which it acted as a credit backstop for AI cloud providers in exchange for a share of revenue and a commitment to rent back unused GPUs, according to reports on August 28. Early adopters included Firmus, deploying 170,000 GPUs in Batam, Indonesia, and Sharon AI with 40,000 GB300s. Employees flagged antitrust exposure and partners pushed back on control terms; Nvidia says the model "continues to evolve" and disputes that it is shelved.

Why it matters: Smaller neocloud financings were being underwritten partly on Nvidia's backstop. If it stays paused, lenders to second-tier GPU operators lose a credit support they may have priced in — worth checking before adding data-center or neocloud paper, and a reminder that the AI credit complex still depends on a single counterparty's policy choices.
Andreessen Horowitz

Andreessen Horowitz raises $1.1 billion for a dedicated AI hardware fund

Andreessen Horowitz announced its Machine Age Fund on August 28, a $1.1 billion vehicle targeting chips, memory, networking, storage, data centers, and robotics — the firm's first fund dedicated to hardware infrastructure. a16z argues every layer of the hardware supply chain is capacity constrained, with an industry accustomed to 20–30% annual growth facing triple-digit demand. It is a notable pivot for the firm that declared "software is eating the world" fifteen years ago.

Why it matters: Venture capital formally chasing the physical buildout confirms where the bottleneck — and the margin — has moved. For allocators, expect a wave of hardware-focused venture and growth vehicles seeking commitments, and expect private valuations in components, cooling, and power to firm up before the public comps reflect it.
AI in Wealth Management
CIBC (Q3 2026 results)

CIBC pairs 15% profit growth with an AI tool halving advisor paperwork

CIBC reported third-quarter net income of $2.41 billion on August 27, or $2.47 per share, up 15% from a year earlier, its ninth straight quarter of double-digit EPS growth. Wealth management fee-based assets under administration and management both rose more than 20% year over year. On the AI front, the bank introduced AdvisorAssist AI Efficiency, which automates meeting notes and summaries and cuts advisors' administrative documentation time by up to 50%.

Why it matters: A 50% documentation-time claim from a Big Six bank is a benchmark advisors can now cite when evaluating their own firm's note-taking stack against vendors like Zocks or Jump. It also shows earnings calls remain the highest-signal source for deployed — not piloted — advisor AI, so keep reading the wealth segment commentary during print weeks.
TD Bank Group (Q3 2026 results)

TD runs ahead of its C$200 million AI value target for 2026

TD reported record third-quarter results on August 27, with adjusted earnings of C$4.7 billion and revenue up 8%. Management said the bank is tracking ahead of its target to deliver C$200 million of AI-driven value this fiscal year — roughly C$195 million captured through three quarters — against a medium-term ambition of C$1 billion in annualized value. Wealth management net income rose 25% to C$653 million on record segment revenue and assets, with AI deployed across credit, software development, and contact centers.

Why it matters: TD is reporting realized AI value against a stated target, not an ambition — a step beyond the forward goals RBC set a day earlier. Canadian banks are converging on quantified AI disclosure, which gives advisors and analysts a comparable metric across platforms; U.S. wirehouses have yet to publish an equivalent number.
WealthManagement.com

State Street hires Altruist veteran Pete Dorsey to sell its custody rebuild

State Street named Pete Dorsey chief revenue officer for wealth custody and clearing on August 28, a newly created role. Dorsey was previously chief strategy and revenue officer at Altruist and head of institution services at LPL, and joins from Wing Financial, the RIA app he co-founded. State Street has been hiring Altruist alumni to build a digitally native, AI-enabled RIA custody platform, five years after exiting the business — and the hire lands two days after Vanguard's $4.6 billion Altruist acquisition.

Why it matters: With Vanguard absorbing Altruist, a custody seat is opening for RIAs who want an AI-forward alternative to Schwab and Fidelity, and State Street is staffing to take it. Advisors reassessing custodial relationships after the Altruist deal should put State Street's build on the diligence list — and watch how much Altruist talent it keeps attracting.
Practifi

Practifi launches Sentir, an AI-native CRM built around 16 embedded agents

Practifi launched Sentir on August 27, an advisor CRM with intelligence built into the architecture rather than layered on top. Sixteen named AI agents work across client records and interactions, generating daily briefings, drafting next-step recommendations, and pulling context from a knowledge base of more than 200 primary sources spanning industry guidance and legislation. Practifi's existing configurable platform was rebranded Naya and remains available for firms that prefer a non-agentic CRM.

Why it matters: The CRM layer is where advisor AI consolidates: Salesforce, Practifi, and Envestnet are all now shipping agents that sit on the book of record. Firms mid-contract should test whether their incumbent's agent roadmap matches an AI-native rebuild before renewing — and ask any vendor demoing agents how recommendations are sourced and audited for compliance review.
AI in Asset Management
CNBC

DeepSeek nears a $7.4 billion round at $74 billion ahead of 2027 IPO

DeepSeek is in talks to raise at least $7.4 billion at a $74 billion valuation, with the round expected to close by the end of August ahead of a Shanghai Star Market listing targeted for 2027, CNBC reported August 28. The lab's first 50 billion yuan round consumed more than 60% of the assets of founder Liang Wenfeng's quant fund High-Flyer, which manages roughly 80 billion yuan and is now taking allocations in China's hard-tech IPO wave to chase returns.

Why it matters: A frontier lab outgrowing the hedge fund that spawned it marks the end of quant-subsidized AI research — Chinese labs now need institutional capital and public listings like their U.S. peers. For EM and China-tech allocations, a DeepSeek Star Market debut would create the first directly investable frontier-model pure play outside U.S. private markets.
Bloomberg

PIMCO says $220 billion of AI debt is pushing Treasury yields higher

PIMCO's Marc Seidner, CIO of non-traditional strategies, said on August 28 there is "too much, too fast" AI-related bond issuance, causing "indigestion" in fixed-income markets. Hyperscaler debt issuance has reached $220 billion in 2026 against $12.5 billion in the comparable period last year, per BNP Paribas data through August 10. PIMCO says the supply wave very possibly helped push the 10-year Treasury yield to about 4.75% earlier this month, while arguing the resulting concessions set up decent longer-term returns.

Why it matters: This is a duration and crowding-out call, not just a credit-quality warning: AI supply is now a variable in rate forecasts and rebalancing math for any bond-heavy allocation. PMs can treat new-issue concessions on quality AI paper as an entry point, but should track the hyperscaler issuance calendar the way they track Treasury refunding.
SEC filing (REX ETF Trust)

REX files an AI Growth & Income ETF as the product wave rolls on

REX filed a registration statement with the SEC on August 28 covering four Growth & Income ETFs — spanning FANG and innovation names, AI, and crypto — that becomes effective 75 days after filing. The filing caps an August of aggressive AI product formation: Harbor listed five actively managed AI-ecosystem ETFs on August 13 built around the OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, and SpaceX supply chains, and Pictet's AI-run PQUS has gathered roughly $100 million since its February launch.

Why it matters: Issuers are now segmenting the AI trade — income overlays, ecosystem baskets, model-managed stock selection — rather than selling one thematic basket. Advisors fielding client requests for "AI exposure" should map which layer each product actually owns, and press issuers on overlap: many of these funds hold the same ten names under different wrappers.
