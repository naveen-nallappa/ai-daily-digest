Major AI Updates
Anthropic (company blog)

Anthropic folds Glasswing into a three-tier Cyber Verification Program for vetted defenders

Anthropic said on October 6 it merged Project Glasswing into its Cyber Verification Program, now offering three tiers: Defense Access for SOC and incident-response teams, Red Team Access for authorized penetration testing, and Specialized Access for operators of grids, telecoms and market infrastructure, reviewed with the US government. Each tier includes Claude Mythos 5.1 with reduced safeguards. Glasswing partners found at least 129,000 verified vulnerabilities from April to July, more than 33,000 rated critical or high.

Why it matters: Defenders can now get the same frontier tools attackers are using, but only through a vetting queue that takes days to weeks. CISOs at wealth and asset managers should apply for Defense Access now, and ask custodians and core vendors whether they hold Red Team Access for their own testing before the next due-diligence cycle.
The Next Web

Meta and Sierra publish Personal Agent Protocol for AI agents logging in to businesses

Sierra and Meta unveiled the Personal Agent Protocol on October 6, an open, OAuth-based standard for how personal AI agents authenticate with companies. An agent can browse as a guest, then gain read-only or write access once the customer signs in, connecting through a firm’s website, its APIs over MCP or OpenAPI, or the firm’s own agent. Genesys, Rocket, Shopify, Stripe and Walmart are founding partners. A v0.1 specification is due later in October; payments are deferred to later versions.

Why it matters: Client agents such as Meta’s Muse will soon ask to read accounts and act on them, and PAP gives firms a standard way to say yes or no. Digital and compliance leads should decide what read-only and write scopes they would grant, and how agent sessions get logged for books-and-records, before clients start asking.
Mistral AI (company blog)

Mistral previews Large 4, a trillion-parameter open-weight model with weights due this month

Mistral released a preview API for Mistral Large 4 on October 6, a multimodal mixture-of-experts model with about one trillion total and 49 billion active parameters. Preview pricing is $1.36 per million input tokens and $4.18 per million output. Open weights follow by the end of October, and Mistral will run a European deployment end to end. Mistral reports 82% on a vulnerability reproduce-and-patch test, while its own human evaluation ranked it behind Claude Opus 5 on coding quality.

Why it matters: Together with Reflection’s Beam, two large Western open-weight models land this month. Firms with EU data-residency rules gain a frontier-class option run entirely in Europe. Model-risk teams should benchmark both on internal document and research tasks, since self-hosting can cut per-token costs for high-volume workloads such as meeting notes and surveillance.
Reuters

Google contracts 3.6 gigawatts from Constellation, including 890 megawatts of new nuclear

Google signed power agreements with Constellation Energy on October 6 totaling about 3.6 gigawatts, Reuters reported. A 15-year deal covers 2,700 megawatts from existing generation. A separate 20-year agreement funds upgrades at 11 nuclear units that add 890 megawatts, with more than $4.3 billion invested and first deliveries in 2028. The same day, New York Governor Kathy Hochul proposed ending data-center tax breaks and charging data centers higher electricity rates.

Why it matters: Hyperscalers are locking up existing nuclear output for decades, which supports long-dated contracted cash flows for Constellation and peers. PMs in utilities and infrastructure should weigh that against rising state pushback on data-center power costs, which could shift who pays for grid upgrades and squeeze returns on new merchant capacity.
AI in Wealth Management
Bloomberg

Dimon says AI cyber risk rose tenfold after Anthropic’s Mythos

JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon told Bloomberg Television on October 6 that “risks from AI went up 10-fold after Mythos,” adding that AI exposed vulnerabilities the bank had not known about. He declined to call the threat existential and said JPMorgan is “rolling up our sleeves” to fix what it finds. Cyber insurance has not repriced: Marsh data show US cyber rates fell 2% in the second quarter, and global rates fell for a twelfth straight quarter.

Why it matters: When the industry’s best-resourced bank calls AI its biggest cyber worry, regulators and clients will expect the same vigilance from smaller firms. Wealth firms should review cyber coverage while pricing is still soft, check vendor patch timelines, and prepare advisors for client questions after the recent AI-assisted bank breaches in South Korea.
ThinkAdvisor

eMoney launches Financial Assessment to win prospects who now ask AI first

eMoney Advisor launched Financial Assessment, a self-guided, firm-branded tool that gives prospects a one-page summary of their spending, education and retirement position in a few minutes, ThinkAdvisor reported on October 6. Advisors send email invitations; the data stays in eMoney and carries into a full plan if the prospect converts. It is available now to all eMoney Premium Client Portal users. eMoney product head Chad Porche tied the launch to more people using AI tools to answer financial questions themselves.

Why it matters: Planning vendors are now building for prospects who arrive with chatbot answers, not blank questionnaires. Firms on eMoney can test the assessment as a lead magnet against AI apps such as M1 Advisor, but should track conversion rates and confirm the summaries carry the disclosures compliance requires for pre-engagement materials.
PYMNTS (citing Citi research)

Citi projects Meta’s Muse agent will earn $27 billion a year by 2030

Citi said on October 6 that Meta’s Muse agent could bring in more than $27 billion a year by 2030: about $23 billion from transaction-related revenue and $4.5 billion from subscriptions, Reuters reported. Muse, launched September 8, has passed 6.6 million downloads and 1.8 million daily active users, and Citi called personal agents the new “front door to the internet.” Muse’s launch knocked more than 6% off Schwab and LPL shares on September 22.

Why it matters: A transaction-led model means Muse earns most when it moves money for users, which puts cash sweeps, subscriptions and simple advice in its path. Advisors should expect clients to delegate comparison shopping to agents, and firms should decide whether to let such agents connect through standards like the Personal Agent Protocol.
AI in Asset Management
Bloomberg (via The Next Web)

BlackRock and MGX in talks to anchor a $30 billion OpenAI round at $1.4 trillion

OpenAI is in talks with UAE funds led by MGX and with BlackRock to anchor a private round of at least $30 billion at a fixed $1.4 trillion pre-money valuation, Bloomberg reported on October 5. The UAE syndicate may invest up to $10 billion; the University of California endowment has also held talks, and no lead is set. OpenAI raised $122 billion at $852 billion in March. MGX and BlackRock already co-run the AI Infrastructure Partnership with Microsoft and Nvidia.

Why it matters: BlackRock moving from AI infrastructure into OpenAI equity would give its private-markets clients a direct route to pre-IPO AI. Allocators should ask how such stakes would be valued and offered in BlackRock vehicles, and compare a $1.4 trillion entry price with OpenAI’s roughly $70 billion revenue run rate and Anthropic’s planned listing.
S&P Global (press release)

S&P Global extends ChatIQ into Excel models, slides and portfolio return attribution

S&P Global released a Capital IQ Pro update on October 6 led by expanded ChatIQ, its conversational assistant over S&P company, credit, market and economic data. ChatIQ Spreadsheets builds and reviews Excel models with refreshable Capital IQ Pro formulas, and ChatIQ Presentations, still in Labs, drafts PowerPoint slides from the data. A beta Portfolio Insights tool links portfolio returns to news, events and earnings. The update also adds With Intelligence private-market benchmarking and new DCF tools.

Why it matters: Data vendors are building AI into the analyst’s spreadsheet, competing with general assistants such as Claude in Excel. Research heads already paying for Capital IQ Pro should pilot ChatIQ Spreadsheets against their current copilot on a live model, and check whether Portfolio Insights can replace in-house attribution commentary for client reports.
AdvisorHub

BofA’s Subramanian says easy money from the AI capex trade is ending

Bank of America strategists led by Savita Subramanian said in an October 5 note that outsized returns from AI capital spending are getting harder to earn because the trade is already priced in. Long-only active funds hold near-record-low positions in AI “disruptees” such as IT services, software and consumer finance, and near-record-high positions in industrials relative to consumer discretionary. The strategists suggested a selective pivot toward consumer stocks, arguing US consumer demand is underestimated.

Why it matters: With active managers crowded into AI hardware and underweight the perceived losers, a rotation could hit both sides at once. PMs should stress-test portfolios for a reversal in software and consumer finance, and advisors with concentrated AI-capex sleeves should review rebalancing rules before third-quarter earnings test the spending story.
