Major AI Updates
Reuters

OpenAI seeks about $30 billion at over $1.4 trillion before Anthropic’s IPO

OpenAI is speeding up a funding round of about $30 billion that could value it above $1.4 trillion, Reuters reported on October 19, citing unnamed sources. SoftBank, Abu Dhabi’s MGX and other existing backers are in talks, and OpenAI met long-only and sovereign funds last week. The aim is to lock in commitments before Anthropic’s expected mid-November listing. OpenAI was valued at $852 billion in March and runs at about $50 billion of annualized revenue, one source said.

Why it matters: Two private marks above $1.4 trillion within weeks will reset the reference prices that late-stage funds, interval funds and tech-heavy SMAs use. Advisors with clients in pre-IPO vehicles should check how those positions are marked and how lock-ups work, and should set allocation expectations for the Anthropic deal before demand from clients arrives.
BleepingComputer

DHS orders DeepSeek removed from federal civilian IT, including third-party products built on it

The Department of Homeland Security ordered federal civilian agencies to remove DeepSeek technology and block new purchases, in a Federal Register notice reported on October 20. The order covers DeepSeek’s own products, its affiliates’ products and third-party services built with its technology. Removal is required immediately, and agencies have 60 days to seek exceptions, which CISA will review. DHS cited the risk that Chinese authorities could reach US government data. The order does not apply to private or consumer use.

Why it matters: The third-party clause is the part to watch, because vendors often embed open-weight models without naming them. Firms that serve government plans or federal employees should ask advisor-tech and research vendors to confirm in writing which models sit underneath their tools, and add model provenance to vendor due-diligence questionnaires before regulators or clients ask.
Meta Engineering

Meta details its Ohio Prometheus supercluster, targeting about one million GPUs

Meta published details on October 19 of Prometheus, its gigawatt-scale AI supercluster in New Albany, Ohio, which trains its Muse models. It runs hundreds of thousands of Nvidia GPUs across several buildings today and targets about one million at full scale. The first phase is complete, with GB200 and GB300 systems live and Vera Rubin systems next. Meta reports training goodput above 85% and says interruptions fell 99% from their peak. It did not disclose the cost.

Why it matters: A million-GPU target confirms Meta’s capex path runs through Nvidia’s next two product cycles, despite the chip index selloff. PMs can use the reliability figures as a read on how much purchased compute is actually used, a key input for judging whether hyperscaler spending converts to model output or sits idle.
AI in Wealth Management
Zocks press release (Business Wire)

Zocks becomes available to LPL’s 32,000-plus advisors, syncing notes to WealthVision

Zocks said on October 20 that its AI meeting assistant is now available to LPL Financial’s more than 32,000 advisors and about 1,100 partner institutions. It writes meeting prep summaries, post-meeting notes, action items and follow-up emails. It captures client data without recording calls and syncs it to LPL’s WealthVision Premium planning tool and the advisor’s CRM. Zocks says more than 5,000 firms use it. The release quotes no LPL executive, and pricing was not disclosed.

Why it matters: LPL already lists Jump in its curated AI menu, so its advisors can now choose between the two leading notetakers. The WealthVision link matters most: meeting data that flows straight into a plan shortens plan delivery. Practices should compare how each tool writes to their CRM and what LPL supervision requires before choosing.
The Paypers (citing the Financial Times)

Citi bars third-party AI agents from logging in, joining JPMorgan and BofA

Citi said it does not let third-party AI agents or assistants log into customer accounts or transact on its digital platforms, the Financial Times reported, as summarized by The Paypers on October 19. Certain Open Banking providers keep read-only access. Bank of America is limiting agent access to deposits while it sets liability rules for agent-approved payments. Wall Street executives have contacted the Federal Reserve about agent use, according to a person familiar with the talks. The Fed declined to comment.

Why it matters: The three largest banks now agree that outside agents can read but not move money, which sets the bar for brokerage and custody platforms. Firms planning MCP or agent access should copy that line: read-only by default, human approval for transfers, and a written policy on who bears losses when an authorized agent errs.
WealthManagement.com

Jump links to FMG to suggest pre-approved content from meeting topics

Jump has integrated with FMG’s marketing platform through FMG Connect, WealthManagement.com reported on October 19. Based on what a client discusses in a meeting, Jump suggests FMG content that compliance has already approved, such as a retirement-income guide, for the advisor to review and add to follow-up emails. Jump is one of seven early-access partners building on FMG Connect. FMG is owned by GTCR. Availability and pricing were not disclosed.

Why it matters: Pairing meeting transcripts with a pre-approved library keeps AI-drafted follow-ups inside content compliance has already reviewed, which eases supervision of client communications. Firms using both tools should confirm how the suggestion is logged for books and records, and whether sending FMG material from Jump counts as a new communication needing review.
AI in Asset Management
MSCI investor relations

MSCI says AI agents cut custom-model builds from weeks to hours

MSCI reported third-quarter revenue of $892 million on October 20, up 12.4%, with new recurring subscription sales up 25.3%. On the call, executive Alvise Munari said MSCI has built agents for client portfolio construction and can now build a new model in a couple of hours instead of several weeks. CFO Andy Wiechmann said new products’ share of new sales rose more than 50% from a year ago. CEO Henry Fernandez cited the AI-native Vantager platform in a large US bank deal.

Why it matters: If custom indexes and risk models take hours to build, direct indexing and custom benchmarks get cheaper for mid-sized managers and wealth platforms. PMs should ask index and risk vendors for agent-built pricing. Munari also said most clients only have Microsoft Copilot, which signals that vendor data will reach LLM workflows slowly.
Hedgeweek

Default-swap trading on AI borrowers tops $9 billion a week

Credit default swaps tied to debt from Oracle, SpaceX, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Broadcom and Nvidia averaged over $9 billion of weekly trading in September, up from under $2 billion in July, Hedgeweek reported on October 20, citing the Financial Times and Morgan Stanley Research. AI-linked borrowing has reached about $466 billion this year, against $101 billion a year earlier. Five-year spreads hit records for Meta at 108 basis points and Nvidia at 101. Oracle reached 322.

Why it matters: Investors are using credit protection as a cheaper hedge than equity puts, so CDS spreads now give an early read on AI stress. Fixed-income PMs should check how much hyperscaler and neocloud debt sits in core and investment-grade funds, and advisors should note that “safe” bond sleeves may carry AI exposure.
Hedgeweek

Balyasny moves its applied-AI research head into a new autonomous-strategies role

Balyasny Asset Management has moved Peter Anderson from head of research for applied AI to head of autonomous strategies in its fundamental equities division, Hedgeweek reported on October 20, citing eFinancialCareers. Gearoid Murphy, formerly a senior researcher at Google’s X, replaces him in San Francisco. Itamar Kestenbaum joins from Meta as a senior AI engineer in New York. Balyasny’s systematic trading division remains separate under Francine Fang. The firm has not described what the new strategies will trade.

Why it matters: Moving an AI research lead into a trading role suggests multi-manager platforms want AI-run books inside discretionary equities, not only research help. Allocators to multi-strategy funds should ask how these books are sized, how risk limits apply to them, and how their returns are reported separately from human portfolio managers.
