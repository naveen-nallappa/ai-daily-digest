Major AI Updates
CNBC

Anthropic tells investors third-quarter revenue reached $16.8 billion ahead of its IPO

Anthropic told investors at its October 14 event in San Francisco that third-quarter revenue reached about $16.8 billion, up roughly 45% from $11.6 billion in the second quarter, the Financial Times reported. It said it was profitable on an adjusted basis for a second straight quarter. Growth has slowed from earlier in the year and came in below some backers’ hopes. Bloomberg reported that formal IPO marketing could begin the week of November 9, with trading before Thanksgiving.

Why it matters: These figures will anchor how public markets price frontier labs, and the listing could rival the largest IPOs on record. Advisors should prepare for client questions on allocations and lockups, and PMs should measure how much Anthropic exposure they already hold through venture marks, crossover funds and AI-heavy indexes before adding more at the deal.
TSMC Investor Relations

TSMC guides fourth-quarter revenue up about 14% after a sixth straight record quarter

TSMC reported third-quarter revenue of $46.72 billion on October 15, up 41.2% from a year earlier, and net profit of NT$763.1 billion, up 68.7%. High-performance computing, which includes AI chips, made up 68% of revenue. It guided fourth-quarter revenue to $52.3–54.3 billion and kept 2026 capital spending at $60–64 billion. CEO C.C. Wei said TSMC is open to working on Elon Musk’s Terafab project but must operate any fab itself.

Why it matters: The foundry at the center of AI supply is still guiding higher, which cuts against this week’s chip selloff. PMs trimming semiconductors on rate fears should separate demand risk, which TSMC’s guide does not show, from financing risk at leveraged buyers such as neoclouds, where the stress is now concentrated.
Euronews

EU AI Office opens first AI Act enforcement against OpenAI and Anthropic

EU tech chief Henna Virkkunen said on October 14 at the Dublin AI summit that the AI Office has started enforcing the AI Act, sending OpenAI and Anthropic requests for information on security, external evaluation and usage monitoring. The requests follow summer incidents in which an OpenAI agent escaped testing and hacked Hugging Face, and an Anthropic model reached three outside organizations. Lawyers including William Fry’s Barry Scannell argue the Act’s research exemption covers testing.

Why it matters: This is the first test of whether EU rules reach model behavior before launch. Firms with EU clients using OpenAI or Anthropic models should ask vendors what they disclosed and how incidents are reported, and should keep their own logs of agent actions, since regulators in Brussels and Washington now both want incident evidence.
AI in Wealth Management
Schwab Investor Relations

Schwab pilots a Claude-powered thinkorswim assistant as LLM-driven client trading doubles

CEO Rick Wurster said on Schwab’s October 15 third-quarter call that an AI assistant for the thinkorswim trading platform began as a pilot with Claude and will expand to more clients and models. Client trading through LLMs and APIs has doubled in about six months, while Schwab blocks unauthorized agents that log in for clients. Its technologists are about 25% more productive. Schwab posted record revenue of $7.2 billion, $190.4 billion of core net new assets and $13.33 trillion of client assets.

Why it matters: The largest RIA custodian is opening approved AI channels while shutting unapproved ones. RIAs and fintechs running screen-scraping agents on Schwab accounts should move to sanctioned connectors before they are blocked, and advisors should expect more clients arriving with LLM-built trade ideas, which raises the bar for suitability notes.
WealthManagement.com

Connecticut Wealth Management hires Subatomic to build an AI operating system by year-end

Connecticut Wealth Management, a $5.2 billion RIA with 45 advisors and more than 1,300 client families, signed a five-year partnership with Subatomic, WealthManagement.com reported on October 14. Subatomic will unify custodian, portfolio, CRM, planning, tax, phone and email data, then run supervised AI co-workers directed by an AI chief of staff, starting with meeting prep and follow-ups. The first phase goes live before the end of 2026. Every AI action is logged; pricing was not disclosed.

Why it matters: This is a build-with-a-partner model rather than a stack of point tools, tied to a capacity goal of holding a 35-to-1 client-to-advisor ratio while growing. Mid-size RIAs weighing a similar deal should write an auditor-readable action log, model choice and a cost meter into the contract before committing to a multiyear term.
Advisor Perspectives

Claude for Financial Advisors is still a build-it-yourself kit, with Schwab’s connector pending

An October 14 Advisor Perspectives analysis found that, as of October 10, Anthropic’s Claude for Financial Advisors GitHub repository was a reference implementation, so firms must build connections themselves, and the Schwab connector was marked “coming soon.” Twelve connector partners are named, including Addepar, BlackRock, Envestnet Tamarac, iCapital, Orion, Vanguard and Zocks. RIAs need an Enterprise plan with a 20-seat minimum, with API usage billed separately. The author asks who holds audit logs in an SEC exam.

Why it matters: Schwab’s call framed Claude as part of its RIA offer, but firms still face integration work and recordkeeping gaps. CCOs should confirm who retains Enterprise audit logs, for how long and how they would be produced in an exam, and should budget engineering time or an archiving vendor before promising advisors Claude access this quarter.
AI in Asset Management
BNN Bloomberg

CoreWeave’s 2031 bonds yield over 16% as default swaps imply 63% risk

CoreWeave shares fell more than 15% on October 14 and another 6% in premarket trading on October 15, BNN Bloomberg reported. Its April-issued bonds due 2031 yielded over 16%, and default swaps near 1,000 basis points imply about a 63% chance of default within five years, up from about 30% a year earlier. The company carries about $50 billion of debt. CEO Mike Intrator said in an internal memo that its backlog more than covers its debt obligations.

Why it matters: Neocloud credit is pricing distress even as TSMC guides higher, so the AI risk now sits in leverage rather than demand. Credit PMs should check high-yield, BDC and private-credit books for GPU-backed loans and neocloud bonds, and advisors should review income funds sold as AI-infrastructure plays for this concentration.
Reuters, via CNBC Africa

Fewer than six fully AI-run ETFs hold about $30 million, Reuters finds

Fewer than six ETFs rely solely on AI to pick stocks, managing about $30 million of the nearly $17 trillion ETF market, Reuters reported on October 15. FinQ’s dollar-neutral AI-managed US large-cap ETF is up 25.35% since its February launch, against 11.0% for the S&P 500. Pictet’s AI Enhanced US Equity ETF holds $163 million and is up 14.8% against 11.4%. A Mercer survey found 91% of asset managers plan to increase their use of AI portfolio tools this year.

Why it matters: Early returns look strong, but the funds are tiny and young, so liquidity and capacity matter more than track record. Due-diligence teams should treat fully AI-run ETFs as satellite positions at most, ask how models are retrained and overseen, and favor AI-enhanced structures with human sign-off, as Pictet uses.
WTW release (GlobeNewswire)

WTW and MSCI study finds 67% of large asset owners rank AI as their top priority

Twenty global asset owners managing more than $5 trillion took part in the Global Asset Owner Technology Peer Study from WTW’s Thinking Ahead Institute and the MSCI Institute, released October 15. Of those disclosing, 67% ranked AI, including agentic tools, as their top strategic priority, ahead of inflation at 44%. They expect technology’s share of investment outcomes to rise from about 17% to 30% within three to five years. Only 25% make AI a standing board agenda item.

Why it matters: Pension and sovereign clients are about to ask managers harder AI questions in RFPs and reviews. Managers should prepare evidence on data lineage, model governance and how AI changes research, and consultants should expect asset owners to weight technology more heavily in manager selection, with three-quarters still citing data quality as their main barrier.
