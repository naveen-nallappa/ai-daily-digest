Financial Stability Board (chair's letter)

FSB tells G20 that AI-enabled cyberattacks are the most immediate stability risk

FSB Chair Andrew Bailey wrote to G20 finance ministers and central bank governors on August 31, ahead of their meeting, naming AI-enabled cyberattacks the most immediate financial-stability risk from frontier models. Bailey, who also governs the Bank of England, said advanced systems change the speed, scale and economics of attacks, and urged authorities to prioritise safe-release practices for the most capable models. The same letter repeated the FSB's warning that markets remain vulnerable to a disorderly correction.

Why it matters: The FSB has now ranked AI cyber above model risk and market concentration in its own hierarchy, which is the ordering supervisors will carry into 2027 examination cycles. Firms should pull forward third-party concentration reviews: the same few cloud and model providers now sit underneath most advisory, custody and trading stacks, so one vendor compromise is a multi-firm event.
The Information

OpenAI buys tens of thousands of Macs to train computer-use agents

OpenAI has purchased tens of thousands of Mac mini and Mac Studio machines to run reinforcement-learning workloads for computer-use agents, according to reporting that circulated August 30 and 31, with Anthropic separately renting Mac mini capacity through AWS for the same purpose. The labs are using Apple's unified-memory systems for memory-bound agent training — editing code, navigating software, completing multi-step desktop tasks — not as a substitute for Nvidia clusters. Apple's Mac revenue rose 29% in its fiscal third quarter.

Why it matters: Agent training is developing a second hardware supply chain outside the GPU complex, which cuts against the assumption that AI capex accrues almost entirely to Nvidia and the hyperscalers. PMs expressing the AI trade through semiconductors alone are missing a demand source already visible in Apple's hardware line; lengthening lead times on high-memory Mac configurations are the observable tell.
European Commission (announcement)

Brussels designates ChatGPT a very large online search engine under the DSA

The European Commission on August 31 designated ChatGPT as a Very Large Online Search Engine under the Digital Services Act, alongside Reddit and Roblox as Very Large Online Platforms. ChatGPT's search function averaged roughly 159 million monthly active users in the EU over the six months to March 2026, well past the 45 million threshold. OpenAI has four months, to January 2027, to meet systemic-risk assessment, transparency and minor-protection obligations. Non-compliance carries fines of up to 6% of global annual revenue.

Why it matters: This is the first generative assistant anywhere pulled into platform-grade regulation, and the reasoning — a chatbot retrieving live web results is a search engine — travels to every agent that browses on a client's behalf. European wealth platforms exposing order or planning workflows to third-party assistants should assume their AI counterparty now carries an EU systemic-risk file that supervisors can read.
TechCrunch

SpaceX will cast its own turbine blades in Bastrop to speed data-center power

Elon Musk confirmed on August 29 that SpaceX is building a blades-and-vanes foundry near its Starlink plant in Bastrop, Texas, after job postings and roughly 830 acres of land purchases surfaced. Hot-section blades must be cast as single crystals and survive temperatures above the melting point of their own alloys; only a handful of foundries worldwide can do it, and order books at suppliers including GE Vernova run into 2030. Musk said in-house casting could bring gas turbines online up to 18 months faster.

Why it matters: Turbine castings, not chips, are the binding constraint on new AI capacity, and a vertically integrated competitor changes the scarcity premium embedded in power-equipment names. Anyone underwriting data-center credit or holding the picks-and-shovels basket should stress the assumption that turbine backlogs stay full to 2030 — that backlog is the whole valuation case for several suppliers.
AI in Wealth Management
InvestmentNews

Brookwood picks Amplify to rebuild its $1.5 billion platform on one data layer

Phoenix-based RIA Brookwood Investment Group has selected AI-native wealthtech Amplify Technology to build a single data platform spanning onboarding, trading, reporting, billing and governance, the firms said August 31. Brookwood has grown from $515 million to more than $1.5 billion in assets in two years and now supports over 75 advisors and about 6,200 clients. The deal follows Amplify's Custody Command Layer built with Goldman Sachs Custody Solutions and a turnkey portfolio partnership with Ned Davis Research.

Why it matters: Fast-growing RIAs are buying a data foundation before they buy agents, which inverts the usual procurement order and is the more defensible sequence — agents deployed on fragmented custodial and CRM data inherit the reconciliation problem. Firms evaluating AI tools should first ask what single source of truth the vendor writes to, and whether it survives a custodian change.
InvestmentNews

Two-thirds of Americans want retail access to AI's private-market gains

A DealMaker survey of more than 2,000 US adults, released August 31, found 67% believe everyday investors should be able to invest in AI's growth, rising to 78% among those 65 and older. Sixty-three percent said retail buyers risk entering names like OpenAI and Anthropic at inflated valuations after insiders capture the gains, and 57% said prolonged private status damages the economy. Even so, 36% said they would buy OpenAI pre-IPO given the chance. The INVEST Act, which would modernise the accredited-investor definition, sits before the Senate.

Why it matters: Client demand for pre-IPO AI exposure is arriving before most advisors have a suitability framework or a vehicle for it, and the 65-plus cohort driving that demand holds roughly 54% of US equities. Advisors should settle position-sizing, liquidity and valuation-marking policy for private AI now, rather than during the next mega-listing window.
Zeplyn (announcement)

Zeplyn agents cut not-in-good-order rates 80% on Schwab account opening

Zeplyn's integration with Schwab Advisor Center lets its AI agents assemble client data from meetings, emails, CRM records and documents, complete Schwab's digital account-opening fields and hand a finished draft to an advisor for review and submission. Early pilots cut not-in-good-order submissions roughly 80% and saved advisors more than 12 hours a week, the firm said. The same integration pulls live Schwab holdings and transactions into Zeplyn's pre-meeting client briefs.

Why it matters: NIGO is the cleanest number in advisor operations, and an 80% reduction is a harder ROI claim than time saved on notes. Operations leaders should demand the same metric from every agent vendor. The wider signal is that Schwab is letting third-party agents write into its account-opening workflow, which makes custodian API openness a real platform-selection criterion.
AI in Asset Management
Benzinga

Yorkville lists the first MANGOS ETF, using perpetual futures for OpenAI exposure

Yorkville America listed the Yorkville America MANGOS Plus Index ETF on the NYSE on August 31, its first fund outside the Truth Social brand. The portfolio pairs the MANGOS names — Meta, Alphabet, Nvidia, SpaceX, Anthropic and OpenAI — with AI infrastructure beneficiaries including Micron and SanDisk. For the two private labs, Yorkville plans to use perpetual futures rather than wait for a listing. The firm is also close to acquiring an SMA-focused institutional manager, with the deal expected to close in September.

Why it matters: Synthetic exposure to private AI companies inside a 1940 Act wrapper puts basis risk, funding cost and mark-to-model valuation into a daily-liquidity product. Diligence should focus on the futures counterparty and the reference price for Anthropic and OpenAI, not the ticker's headline basket — and on how the fund reprices if either lab lists at a discount to its last round.
CFTC (press release)

CFTC opens 60-day consultation on compute futures as CME targets October 5

The CFTC is taking 60 days of comment on how derivatives exchanges should structure contracts referencing AI computing power, focusing on settlement price indexes and the standardisation needed to list them. CME Group's product page gives October 5 as a planned launch date for contracts representing one month of GPU rental, subject to review. ICE plans a cash-settled contract on the NATIVX COIL Index, which normalises tokenised compute and connectivity by energy use, later in 2026. Architect Financial Technologies is building a competing venue.

Why it matters: A listed compute curve would give allocators a direct hedge on the input cost driving AI capex, and a forward price that can be compared against the growth embedded in hyperscaler multiples. Managers running AI-infrastructure books should start modelling how a public compute strip changes their marks well before October 5, since early liquidity will be thin and the basis wide.
Bank for International Settlements (Bulletin 134)

BIS shows LLMs can flag AT1 prospectus divergences supervisors previously missed

A BIS bulletin published August 26 sets out a procedure using large language models to review bank prospectuses against capital rules and rank potential divergences for supervisory attention. The authors applied it to Additional Tier 1 prospectuses from European global systemically important banks, then validated it on anonymised Credit Suisse and Yes Bank documents, recovering the rule-versus-prospectus differences that later drove disputes over AT1 loss absorption. The same approach could compare national rules against the international standards they implement.

Why it matters: Credit teams holding AT1 and other loss-absorbing paper can now run this screen themselves rather than wait for a resolution event to reveal the gap between documentation and rule. Expect supervisors to apply it first, which shortens the window in which a mispriced clause stays mispriced — a reason to re-read subordinated holdings' terms before the market does.
