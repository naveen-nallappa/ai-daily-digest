OpenAI's unreleased Astra model solves ten decade-old math problems with verified proofs

EU AI Act transparency and penalty rules take effect for AI-generated content

The EU AI Act's transparency obligations became enforceable August 2, requiring providers to tell users when they are interacting with AI and to add machine-readable marks that let AI-generated or manipulated images, audio, video, and text be detected. Systems placed on the market on or after August 2 need the marking from day one; pre-existing systems have until December 2. The general-purpose AI penalty regime also took effect, with fines reaching €15 million or 3% of worldwide turnover.

Why it matters: Coming a day after California's own transparency law, the EU regime hardens a global baseline: any firm generating synthetic media or chatbot text for EU clients must now embed provenance marks and disclose AI use. Compliance teams should map where AI touches client-facing content, confirm vendor tooling supports the required marking, and write these duties into third-party contracts before enforcement bites.
