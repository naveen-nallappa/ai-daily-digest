Major AI Updates
Anthropic Form S-1/A (SEC)

Anthropic’s amended IPO filing shows $17 billion of third-quarter revenue

Anthropic’s amended S-1, filed November 2, reports about $17.0 billion of revenue for the quarter to September 30, more than 12 times a year earlier, and a $23.3 billion net loss. Growth over the second quarter slowed to 47%. Nine-month revenue was $33.24 billion. The filing lists about $895 billion of compute and lease commitments, roughly $447.5 billion of it through 2030. Anthropic plans to list on Nasdaq as ANT and aims to price before Thanksgiving.

Why it matters: This IPO tests public appetite for frontier-lab economics: fast revenue growth against commitments of roughly 50 times quarterly sales. PMs should plan for a likely Nasdaq-100 fast-entry addition about 15 trading days after listing, which forces index-fund buying. Credit PMs holding Google, Amazon or Microsoft bonds should note Anthropic is a major counterparty on their compute contracts.
Marvell Technology Form 8-K (SEC)

Marvell agrees to buy Lumentum for $115 billion in an all-stock optics deal

Marvell Technology agreed on November 2 to acquire Lumentum in an all-stock deal valued at $115 billion including debt, or $102.5 billion of equity. Lumentum holders get 4.5 Marvell shares each, worth $1,132 a share, a 23.9% premium to the October 28 close, and will own about 33% of the combined company. Closing is expected in the first half of 2028. Marvell fell more than 10% and Lumentum more than 15% in intraday trading.

Why it matters: The deal bets that optical links, not chips alone, will limit how far AI data centers scale. Reuters puts the price at about 33 times Lumentum’s forward core operating profit, double the peer median. Semiconductor PMs should expect a long antitrust review and an 18-month overhang on both stocks. AI-thematic ETF holders should check their combined weight, since both names often sit in the same baskets.
Bloomberg

Oracle files to raise $20 billion in stock and bonds for its AI build-out

Oracle filed on November 2 to sell $10 billion of new shares and $10 billion of investment-grade bonds in six tranches maturing from 2029 to 2046, led by BofA Securities. At its October 28 investor day, Oracle capped new capital through fiscal 2030 at $20 billion, split evenly, and called it the final amount for this cycle. It spent $55.7 billion on data centers in the year to May 2026. Shares fell about 5%.

Why it matters: Oracle is pulling its whole remaining funding plan forward into a weak tape, choosing certainty over price. Fixed-income PMs should compare the new-issue concession with recent Meta and Alphabet deals as AI bond supply builds. Equity holders take dilution well before free cash flow turns positive, which Oracle expects in the second half of fiscal 2028.
AI in Wealth Management
American Banker

Morgan Stanley advisors have tried to build nearly 50,000 of their own AI agents

Morgan Stanley lets advisors build agents in plain language on its AI@MS dashboard, wealth AI head Koren Maranca told American Banker on November 2. Advisors have made nearly 50,000 build attempts, though most are not in regular use, and about half of advisor inquiries are now first handled by an AI agent. Firm-wide agents include a morning briefing, a call-center Q&A agent and a savings-income projector. FA Assist, which picks agents from a prompt, is in development.

Why it matters: Morgan Stanley is turning advisors into builders, with review gates before an agent spreads. Rival firms should expect recruits to ask for the same freedom. Compliance teams can copy the model: accuracy and completeness checks when a team adopts an agent, wider testing before firm-wide release, and review after use. The firm still gives no hours-saved figure, so treat productivity claims cautiously.
RIABiz

Schwab requires DocuSign for RIA account agreements from November 2

Schwab stopped accepting paper agreements on November 2 for RIA accounts that can be opened digitally, RIABiz reported; accounts with private assets or complex legal structures can still use the old process. Nevis, the AI wealth platform Schwab backed in its $70 million round, will bring digital onboarding to Schwab advisors in the first quarter of 2027. RIAs will pay Nevis separately through Schwab’s Advisor Center marketplace. Schwab’s $4,800 Claude credit offer closes December 1.

Why it matters: Onboarding is the top pain point for 72% of advisors in an F2 Strategy survey, and the paper fallback is now gone. RIAs on Schwab should clean up client data before Nevis arrives, since each system still keeps its own client records. Firms weighing Claude seats should decide before December 1 and check whether Nevis duplicates tools they already pay for.
WealthManagement.com

Wealth firms urge clients to keep Anthropic IPO stakes small and be patient

Advisors at Arena Private Wealth, Premier Path Wealth Partners and Birch Financial Group told WealthManagement.com on October 30 that client demand for Anthropic shares trails SpaceX. At Birch, about 10% of clients asked about Anthropic, against a quarter for SpaceX. They expect a pop and then a slide, as SpaceX ran to $226 and fell to $127 within a month. Some point cautious clients to ETFs that hold Anthropic, such as Tema’s LAZR at about 10.5%.

Why it matters: Hot-IPO allocations go mostly to the largest relationships, and the stock may fall soon after. Advisors should set position limits before pricing, document suitability for any IPO order, and explain lock-ups of up to 180 days. Clients buying exposure through thematic ETFs should know they hold a concentrated private-market mark, not a diversified AI basket.
AI in Asset Management
PYMNTS (citing Bloomberg)

Goldman limits Claude Code and GitHub AI coding tools to its 12,000 engineers

Goldman Sachs has restricted Anthropic’s Claude Code and Microsoft’s GitHub coding tools to engineers, after letting bankers and traders use them for months, Bloomberg reported November 2, citing internal guidance issued in October. Some traders had used the tools to monitor client trading flows and to build written and graphic materials. Spokesperson Nick Carcaterra said the firm is moving from experimentation to a more selective approach that weighs productivity and risk management.

Why it matters: A leading markets franchise is pulling agentic coding back from front-office staff, a sign risk teams see trader-built automation as a control gap. Buy-side COOs should inventory the scripts and tools PMs and traders built with AI, and route anything touching order flow or client data through engineering review. Expect vendors to pitch governed no-code tools to these desks.
TechCrunch

Venture LPs warn funds are piling into OpenAI and Anthropic rounds

Limited partners Beezer Clarkson of LGT Capital Partners and Amit Bhatti of TrueBridge told TechCrunch on October 30 that venture managers are crowding into OpenAI and Anthropic, often within the same fund. Putting 20% of a fund into one large round, such as Anthropic’s, pushes it ahead of deployment pace. Managers now return to raise every 12 to 18 months, against a norm of 24 to 36, and seed checks have roughly doubled.

Why it matters: Allocators may own the same two labs several times over through different venture managers, and again through public and crossover funds once Anthropic lists. CIOs should run look-through exposure to OpenAI and Anthropic across venture, growth and public books before IPO marks reset. Faster fundraising also squeezes diligence, so slow down commitments to managers coming back early.
