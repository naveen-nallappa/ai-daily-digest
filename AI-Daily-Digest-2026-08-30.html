Major AI Updates
The Information

Anthropic weighs letting insiders sell shares in a late-September IPO

Anthropic is working on a plan, reported August 28, to combine newly issued shares with a secondary component letting existing holders sell into its IPO — a break from this year's primary-only SpaceX and Cerebras listings. To offset supply-overhang concerns, it is weighing lockups longer than the customary 180 days for some shareholders. The prospectus goes public after Labor Day, with an investor day in mid-September and a listing targeted for late September or early October; bankers have discussed a valuation near $1.5 trillion.

Why it matters: Structure details now drive the trade. A secondary tranche plus extended lockups changes float, index-inclusion math, and the post-listing supply calendar — the variables that determined how SpaceX traded after its debut. Advisors fielding client demand for the offering should anchor expectations to the September prospectus, not the valuation headlines.
Federal Reserve (keynote remarks)

Warsh puts AI at the center of his first Jackson Hole keynote

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh used his August 28 Jackson Hole keynote to frame AI as potentially a new factor of production with direct consequences for monetary policy. He posed two open questions: whether AI will cause a significant, sustained rise in productivity — and when — and how much of the return on capital stays with owners of scarce assets like AI labs, chipmakers, energy producers, and cloud providers versus flowing to businesses and consumers. The Fed is standing up a task force on productivity and jobs to work through both.

Why it matters: The Fed chair just tied the rate path to the AI capex cycle in his most formal venue. If the task force concludes AI capex is inflationary before it is productive, that supports higher-for-longer — a direct input for duration positioning and for the discount rates underpinning AI-heavy equity valuations into the September FOMC.
CNBC

White House weighs extending chip tariffs to servers and laptops

The administration is considering a second round of semiconductor tariffs that would reach beyond chips to products built with them — laptops, gaming consoles, and the servers that fill AI data centers — according to reports on August 27. Commerce officials have signaled the exemptions attached to January's 25% chip tariff, covering data centers, R&D, startups, and consumer devices, may not carry over. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick favors capping duty-free imports at volumes pegged to each company's committed US production, with any rollout phased.

Why it matters: A tariff on assembled servers flows straight into hyperscaler and neocloud capex budgets in a way chip-only duties did not, pressuring the economics behind every data-center financing in the pipeline. Watch for guidance revisions from server OEMs and for the exemption decision — the January carve-outs are what kept AI infrastructure costs out of round one.
AI in Wealth Management
Scotiabank (Q3 2026 / AI Consortium release)

Scotiabank co-founds an AI control consortium as wealth earnings rise 23%

Scotiabank reported record third-quarter results on August 25, with net income up 17% to C$2.95 billion and wealth management earnings up 23%. During the quarter it joined Lightworks, Sun Life, and TELUS to launch the AI Consortium, a Canadian collaboration to build the control systems large regulated firms need to deploy AI safely. The bank also rolled out Scotia Intelligence knowledge agents to employees, and said staff used AI 17% more to answer customer questions than in the prior quarter.

Why it matters: Shared AI-governance infrastructure among competing regulated firms is a new model — if the consortium's control frameworks become a de facto standard, they will shape what compliance departments demand from advisor AI vendors. The quarter also completes the Canadian print sweep: all six big banks now report scaled AI programs alongside 20%-plus wealth growth.
BMO Financial Group (Q3 2026 results)

BMO puts hard revenue numbers on its AI stack as profit jumps 22%

BMO beat estimates on August 25 with adjusted EPS of C$3.96, up 22%, and wealth management earnings growth of 22%. Unusually, the bank quantified AI returns line by line: its AIM insights platform has driven more than 35,000 commercial client conversations with over 80% of insights leading to action, the Lumi frontline chatbot lifted new-employee productivity 15%, and AI-enhanced offer targeting produced a 3% sales-revenue lift. BMO also renewed its Vector Institute partnership and stood up an Institute for Applied AI & Quantum.

Why it matters: Most banks still describe AI in adoption percentages; BMO reported it in revenue lift and productivity deltas. Those disclosed metrics give advisors and analysts a concrete yardstick for interrogating any firm's AI claims — ask for the sales lift and action rate, not the pilot count — and set the disclosure bar for US wealth managers reporting in October.
InvestmentNews

Vanguard settled a founder lawsuit weeks before its $4.6 billion Altruist deal

Vanguard settled litigation with the founders of Just Invest on July 29, less than a month before announcing its $4.6 billion cash acquisition of AI-forward RIA custodian Altruist on August 26, InvestmentNews reported August 27. The founders of Just Invest — the direct-indexing firm Vanguard bought in 2021 as its first-ever acquisition — alleged the purchase was "an insurance policy rather than a good faith effort to generate new revenue," and had moved in June to depose former CEO Tim Buckley before the case settled on undisclosed terms.

Why it matters: Vanguard's only prior acquisition ended in founder litigation over post-deal neglect — the exact integration risk now hanging over Altruist, whose value depends on retaining the team building its AI custody stack. Advisors weighing the platform should watch founder retention and product velocity over the next few quarters, not just the deal headline.
CPA Practice Advisor

april pipes client-authorized IRS transcripts into advisor planning workflows

Embedded tax platform april expanded its offering for wealth management firms on August 28, giving advisors client-authorized access to up to seven years of IRS transcript data through an API. The feed runs through april's tax engine — which has processed more than 1.4 million federal and state returns in 2026 and holds full IRS e-file authorization — to surface planning opportunities such as Roth conversions, concentrated stock positions, and estimated tax payments directly in onboarding and planning workflows.

Why it matters: Authoritative IRS data beats client-uploaded PDFs as fuel for AI tax planning, and tax capability is becoming a measurable differentiator among top-performing RIAs. Firms comparing april against Holistiplan or Altruist's Hazel should weight data provenance and consent handling — a transcript API changes both the accuracy and the compliance posture of AI-generated tax advice.
AI in Asset Management
Blue Owl (press release)

Blue Owl leads a $2.4 billion GPU financing for IREN at 9%

Funds managed by Blue Owl led a $2.4 billion compute equipment financing for AI cloud operator IREN, announced August 28, split between a $1.2 billion senior secured term loan and $1.2 billion of senior secured notes, both priced at 9% and maturing about two and a half years after funding. PIMCO also participated. Proceeds fund Nvidia Blackwell Ultra GPUs for IREN's Mackenzie campus in British Columbia, with capital drawn in tranches as hardware is delivered and commissioned.

Why it matters: A 9% coupon on GPU-collateralized paper from a $319 billion manager sets a clean pricing benchmark for compute-backed private credit — several hundred basis points over hyperscaler investment-grade issuance. PMs holding private credit funds should note the short 2.5-year tenor matched to chip depreciation; that structure, not the headline size, is what makes the collateral bankable.
Hedgeweek

Two Sigma discloses two new founder arbitrations at the $80 billion quant

Two Sigma told investors it faces two fresh arbitration proceedings in the long-running governance fight between co-founders John Overdeck and David Siegel, Hedgeweek reported August 28 citing Bloomberg. Entities linked to Siegel's family had a claim referred to arbitration on August 17 alleging Overdeck breached fiduciary duties in handling a former employee's compensation dispute. Separately, Seth Platt — Siegel's pick for the two-person management committee — filed on August 18 to resolve whether his appointment makes him co-CEO alongside Carter Lyons.

Why it matters: Two Sigma runs $80 billion in machine-driven strategies, and unresolved control questions at the top are exactly what operational due diligence flags — the 2023-24 chapter of this dispute already drew SEC attention. Allocators with exposure should press for clarity on management-committee authority before the arbitrations conclude, since key-person clauses may hinge on the outcome.
Owner (press release)

Goldman Sachs Alternatives leads a $240 million round in AI platform Owner

Growth Equity at Goldman Sachs Alternatives led a $240 million financing for Owner at a $2.3 billion valuation, announced August 28, with Meritech, Redpoint, Headline, and Jack Altman participating. Owner, which launched in 2020 and has passed $100 million in annual recurring revenue, sells restaurants an AI-run marketing and technology stack — websites, online ordering, CRM, and AI phone ordering — and now powers more US locations than Domino's.

Why it matters: Goldman's growth arm paying roughly 23x ARR for applied AI in a decidedly unglamorous vertical shows where alternative managers see durable AI revenue: workflow replacement with measurable unit economics, not model-layer bets. For allocators, it is a useful comp for how growth-equity sleeves are marking AI application companies against the infrastructure trade.
