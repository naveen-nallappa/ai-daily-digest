Major AI Updates
Oracle press release

Oracle puts ChatGPT and Codex in its apps and adds Google TPUs from 2027

At Oracle AI World on October 27, Oracle said OpenAI models, ChatGPT and Codex will be built into Fusion Applications, NetSuite and its AI Database through a new AI Workspace. Separately, Oracle and Google said eighth-generation TPUs will be offered on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure in 2027, ramping over several years, the first time Google has placed TPUs in another hyperscaler’s cloud. CNBC reported that Cerebras systems and OpenAI’s Jalapeño inference chip will also join OCI.

Why it matters: Oracle is reducing its dependence on Nvidia supply while tying itself more closely to OpenAI, its largest backlog customer. PMs holding Oracle debt or equity should weigh more chip choice against more exposure to one counterparty. Firms on Fusion or NetSuite should expect ChatGPT inside finance and HR workflows and set data-access controls before switching it on.
Seoul Economic Daily

China’s STAR50 index falls about 40% in four months, erasing roughly $480 billion

Shanghai’s tech-heavy STAR50 index has fallen about 40% in under four months, erasing roughly $480 billion of market value, Seoul Economic Daily reported on October 27. It closed at 1,315.50 on October 26. Robot maker Unitree has lost about half its value since its August debut, memory maker CXMT fell more than 25% in a month, and optical suppliers Zhongji Innolight and Eoptolink are down about 60% from summer highs. The index still trades near 100 times earnings.

Why it matters: China’s AI trade unwound after regulators curbed leveraged and high-frequency trading, while the Nasdaq sits only about 6% off its high. PMs running global AI sleeves should check how much China exposure sits in these names, including through the new HKEX AI index. Advisors should flag concentration in single-country AI funds before year-end rebalancing.
OpenAI

OpenAI says an internal model disproved a core complexity-theory hypothesis

OpenAI said on October 26 that an internal model produced a disproof of the Exponential Time Hypothesis, a widely assumed limit on how fast Boolean satisfiability can be solved, and verified it in the Lean proof language. It also claimed proofs of the local smoothing conjecture and the existence of Yang-Mills theory in four dimensions. Independent experts, including the hypothesis’s co-author Russell Impagliazzo, are reviewing the claim unpaid. OpenAI gave $5 million to the Simons Foundation for related conferences.

Why it matters: If the claims hold, frontier models are producing original research, not just summarizing it, which supports the revenue assumptions behind AI-lab valuations ahead of Anthropic’s IPO. PMs should treat confirmation, expected over months, as a catalyst for AI-lab private marks. Research teams should note the machine-checked proofs, a template for auditing AI-generated analysis.
AI in Wealth Management
LPL Financial Q3 2026 results

LPL begins rolling out its Cyan AI agent, targeting all advisors by year-end

LPL said with third-quarter results on October 27 that it has begun a phased rollout of Cyan, its proprietary AI agent inside ClientWorks. CEO Rich Steinmeier said the Cyan Assist pilot covered several hundred advisors and that access will reach all advisors by year-end, with more capabilities planned for early 2027. The firm, which serves more than 32,000 advisors and $2.6 trillion in client assets, reported adjusted EPS of $5.97, up 15%.

Why it matters: Cyan is the largest broker-dealer rollout of an in-house agent so far, arriving as Zocks and Jump also plug into LPL’s stack. Recruiters competing for LPL advisors should expect a sharper technology pitch. Independent firms should ask whether their vendors can match an agent built into the core platform rather than a bolt-on assistant.
Reuters (via Euronext)

UBS bars external AI models from acting for clients as US banks block agents

UBS chief executive Sergio Ermotti said on October 27 that the bank does not allow outside AI models to carry out actions for clients, citing peers’ exposure to models that move or optimize deposits. American Banker reported the same day that JPMorgan, Citi, Bank of America, Wells Fargo and Morgan Stanley block third-party agents from customer accounts. Wells Fargo and Bank of America are design partners on Poppy, a Meta and Sierra protocol for agents to identify themselves and get customer permission.

Why it matters: Clients using ChatGPT or Claude agents will hit walls at most large banks and wirehouses, while Robinhood and Interactive Brokers invite them in. Advisors should expect questions about why a client’s agent cannot see or move money. Firms planning agent access should track Poppy, the likeliest route from blanket blocking to permissioned access.
Nevis release

Schwab joins Nevis’s $70 million round to run AI agents across its custody platform

Nevis said on October 26 that it raised a $70 million Series B led by EQT Ventures, with Charles Schwab joining as a strategic investor alongside Sequoia, ICONIQ, QuantumLight and Ribbit. The partnership aims to extend Nevis’s operational agents across Schwab’s custodial infrastructure, which serves more than 17,000 RIAs. Nevis, founded in 2024, says platform assets grew from $50 billion to over $700 billion this year and its agents have completed more than 100,000 tasks.

Why it matters: Schwab now backs both Wealth.com and Nevis while offering Claude, building a curated AI ecosystem rather than one in-house assistant. RIAs on Schwab should expect deeper Nevis integrations for account opening and servicing. Before signing, they should ask which Nevis agents get write access to custody workflows and how each action is logged for books and records.
Robinhood Q3 2026 results (SEC 8-K)

About 200,000 Robinhood customers have opened accounts that let AI agents trade

Robinhood’s third-quarter results, filed on October 27, show about 200,000 customers have opened Agentic Trading Accounts, which let their own AI agents trade, generating more than $1.6 billion in notional volume. That is roughly double the 100,000 accounts reported at the May beta. Robinhood Strategies, its managed-portfolio service, grew to 350,000 funded customers and more than $2 billion in assets. The company announced Robinhood Agents and Agent Apps at its September HOOD Summit.

Why it matters: Agent-driven trading is now a measurable retail channel, growing while large banks block agents. Advisors with younger clients should ask whether those clients run agents in separate accounts, since those holdings and trades sit outside the advisor’s view and risk models. Broker-dealer compliance teams should define supervision for agent-initiated orders before clients demand it.
AI in Asset Management
TechCrunch

Apollo commits about $40 billion to buy Nvidia chips and lease them to SpaceX

SpaceX disclosed in a regulatory filing on October 26 a binding commitment for roughly $40 billion of GPU financing, which it called the largest committed GPU financing. An Apollo-managed entity will buy Nvidia Vera Rubin chips and server hardware and lease them to SpaceX for about five years from delivery, starting in the first half of 2027. SpaceX expects closing by year-end. Its AI segment produced $7.6 billion of $14.6 billion third-quarter revenue.

Why it matters: Private credit is now financing AI chips directly through leases, not just data-center buildings. Investors in Apollo’s credit funds should ask how much exposure sits in GPU leases, whose collateral can lose value quickly as new chips ship. Allocators comparing private-credit managers should request look-through to AI-hardware collateral and lessee concentration.
Bank of England and FCA

Bank of England survey finds 80% of UK financial firms run AI agents

The Bank of England and FCA published their 2026 survey of AI in UK financial services on October 26. AI use rose to 86% of firms from 75% in 2024, and 80% of detailed respondents have deployed AI agents, mostly for decision support; only 2% give agents broad autonomy. Investment and capital-markets firms were the largest group, at 100 respondents. The top three cloud providers account for 79% of reported relationships, and third parties supply 53% of high-materiality systems.

Why it matters: Regulators now have a baseline to test whether firms supervise agents and vendors as they scale. Asset managers with UK entities should expect follow-up questions on model-provider concentration and agent permissions. US firms can use the same split, decision support versus execution, to inventory their own agents before SEC exams that now cover AI.
Hedgeweek (citing Bloomberg)

Quant firms pay AI PhDs up to $800,000 as they build in-house AI labs

Quant hedge funds and trading firms are expanding academic funding and dedicated AI labs to compete with technology companies for machine-learning talent, Hedgeweek reported on October 27, citing Bloomberg. Recruiters put first-year pay for machine-learning PhDs at $450,000 to $650,000, with some reaching $800,000. Qube’s QRT Labs sponsors more than 70 PhD and postdoctoral researchers, G-Research funds 36 researchers and 43 PhD students, and $35 billion CFM has set up a long-horizon machine-learning lab.

Why it matters: Talent costs are rising for every manager building AI in-house, and the largest quants are locking up the research pipeline. Allocators should ask smaller systematic managers how they will keep researchers as AI labs recruit from Two Sigma and Hudson River Trading. Fundamental managers without that budget should plan to buy, not build, most AI tooling.
