Major AI Updates
AMD Investor Relations

AMD guides to about $17 billion of fourth-quarter revenue as data-center sales double

AMD reported third-quarter revenue of $13.4 billion on November 3, up 45% from a year earlier. Data-center revenue nearly doubled to $8.5 billion, up 97% year over year. The company guided fourth-quarter revenue to about $17 billion, plus or minus 3%, with non-GAAP gross margin near 55%. Results included a $1.1 billion change in the fair value of warrants issued to certain customers, the structure AMD used in its OpenAI supply deal.

Why it matters: The guide implies about 27% sequential growth, a sign that a second GPU supplier is now scaling with OpenAI and other buyers. Semiconductor PMs should treat the warrant line as a recurring earnings swing that grows with AMD’s own share price. Allocators heavy in Nvidia through AI ETFs should check whether their funds hold enough AMD to capture any share shift.
Bloomberg

Meta sells €9 billion of debut euro bonds on a record order book

Meta priced about €9 billion of euro bonds on November 4 in six tranches running from 4 to 38 years, its first deal in the currency, led by Citi and Morgan Stanley. Orders peaked near €53 billion, a record for a euro corporate bond. The sale followed a $15 billion five-part dollar offering a day earlier. Together the two deals raise at least $20 billion for AI infrastructure, after S&P moved Meta’s outlook to negative.

Why it matters: Hyperscalers now tap every major bond market to fund capex, and European buyers are paying up for scarce high-grade tech paper. Credit PMs should watch whether new-issue concessions widen as Alphabet, Oracle and Meta keep returning. Investment-grade index funds take on more AI-capex exposure by default, so advisors using them should know their tech-issuer weight is rising.
Fortune

Ohio voters back data-center limits in at least 14 towns as CoreWeave bonds slide

Ohio voters decided 21 data-center ballot measures across 18 municipalities on November 3, more than half of the 41 such measures nationwide, and passed bans or other limits in at least 14, Fortune reported. Trenton approved a ban 80% to 20%. In Texas, Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa called for a moratorium on new AI data centers. On November 4, CoreWeave’s unsecured notes due 2031 fell to about 79.5 cents on the dollar, Bloomberg reported.

Why it matters: Local permitting is now a priced risk for AI infrastructure, alongside power and chips. High-yield PMs should stress-test data-center credits for sites in states with new governors or local bans. Infrastructure and private-credit allocators should ask managers how many pipeline projects still need zoning or referendum approval, and whether lease terms protect lenders if construction stops.
Apple Form 10-K (SEC)

Apple’s 10-K flags AI agents that act in unpredictable ways as a new risk

Apple’s annual report, filed November 3 for the fiscal year ended September 26, adds autonomous AI agents to its risk factors, warning they may behave in “unintended or unpredictable” ways. On November 4, Bloomberg reported that Apple cut some engineers’ daily internal AI budget from $300 to $200, with cuts to as low as $100 planned. Apple also opened Siri to outside developers through App Intents in its iOS 27.3 betas.

Why it matters: When the largest consumer platform writes agent misbehavior into its risk factors, compliance teams gain cover to demand the same controls from vendors. Wealth firms piloting client-facing agents should map who carries liability when an agent acts outside instructions. Equity analysts should also note that Apple is capping internal AI spend while peers raise capex, a sharp contrast in cost discipline.
AI in Wealth Management
Nitrogen press release (GlobeNewswire)

Osaic opens Nitrogen’s Nucleus AI engine and full suite to nearly 10,000 advisors

Nitrogen said on November 3 that Osaic has approved its full product suite, including all Nucleus agentic AI features, for Osaic’s network of nearly 10,000 advisors. Advisors get six products: the Risk, Research, Income, Tax, Legacy and Insurance Centers. Nucleus can turn client statements into portfolios, build retirement income illustrations and turn tax returns into visual summaries, each with advisor approval. Nitrogen CEO Dan Zitting and Osaic chief AI officer Sayee Bellamkonda announced the deal.

Why it matters: Osaic is adding a planning and proposal agent on top of the Jump and Zocks notetakers it already offers, the next layer of AI at independent broker-dealers. Osaic advisors can now automate proposal prep from uploaded statements but still own the recommendation. Rival broker-dealers should expect recruiting pitches to cite this, and supervision teams should check how Nucleus outputs are archived.
Financial Planning

Bill Harris’s Evergreen.ai advice app will end its beta on November 11

Evergreen, the AI advice venture of former Personal Capital CEO Bill Harris, is shutting down, Financial Planning reported on November 4. The firm said its Evergreen.ai beta will end on November 11 and all user data will be deleted then. Clients of its RIA, Evergreen Wealth, will move to other advisors or custodians by year-end. The app launched in September. Its August survey of 1,981 adults found 29% acted on AI money advice without checking another source.

Why it matters: A well-known founder closed a free AI advice app seven weeks after launch and gave no reason, a sign that direct-to-consumer AI advice remains hard to monetize even with an RIA behind it. Advisors should use the survey as a prompt: ask clients what they have asked AI, especially on tax and equity-compensation decisions, before they act on it.
InvestmentNews

eMoney beta-tests its CoPlanner Chat assistant ahead of a 2027 launch

Fidelity-owned eMoney Advisor is beta testing CoPlanner Chat, an AI assistant built on CoPlanner, the rules-based plan generator it launched in March, InvestmentNews reported on November 2. Advisors will be able to query client data, such as real estate holdings, and model tasks like Roth conversions, and the chat will become an interface to the wider platform. Brandon Heid of eMoney described the beta at Schwab IMPACT in Boston. Launch is expected in 2027.

Why it matters: eMoney sits on a large share of advisor desktops, so a chat layer there could make planning queries routine rather than a specialist task. Firms on eMoney should test this beta before buying a separate planning chatbot. Compliance teams should ask how chat-generated Roth and tax scenarios are logged, since they can feed directly into client recommendations.
InvestmentNews

Large RIAs name ChatGPT, Copilot and Claude in job posts over advisor tools

In the third quarter, 19.4% of independent RIAs posting jobs, 39 of 201, named an AI tool, up from 1.1% two years earlier, according to a Caddi study shared with InvestmentNews on November 4. Over the 12 months to September, Microsoft Copilot appeared at 29 firms, ChatGPT at 27 and Claude at 26, up from three. Zocks, at seven firms, was the only advisor-specific tool among the most named. Caddi sells back-office AI agents to RIAs.

Why it matters: Large RIAs are hiring for general-purpose models, not just buying advisor apps, which shifts vendor leverage toward Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic. Eight of the 10 largest independents posted AI jobs, while firms under $2.5 billion posted none. Ops leaders at smaller firms should decide whether to build these skills or rent them, and budget for data-governance roles.
AI in Asset Management
Hedgeweek

D.E. Shaw reaches a record $125 billion and adds a 0.12% technology fee

D.E. Shaw’s assets reached a record $125 billion, up from $98 billion at the start of 2026, Bloomberg reported on November 3. The quant firm uses AI-powered research systems that read corporate information and alternative data to find shorter-term trades. It added a 0.12% technology surcharge for investors in its flagship Composite fund, on top of 3% management and 35% performance fees. Composite returned 15.3% and Oculus 30% through September.

Why it matters: A technology surcharge passes AI compute costs straight to LPs, on top of fees already well above industry norms. Allocators should expect other quant and multistrategy funds to test similar line items, and should ask managers to break out compute spend and show how it lifts returns. Fee committees should decide now whether tech charges count as pass-through expenses.
Apollo Q3 2026 earnings release

Apollo puts its AI exposure at about 3% as Athene holds $12.9 billion

On its November 3 third-quarter call, Apollo president Jim Zelter put the firm’s total AI-related exposure at about 2.5% to 3%. Athene’s $12.9 billion of digital-infrastructure investments is about 3% of its gross invested assets and about 92% investment grade, including $3.6 billion in chips and $3.6 billion of secured debt with investment-grade counterparties. Zelter said hyperscalers have grown from about 1% to 6% of the investment-grade index. Apollo reported record quarterly origination of $114 billion.

Why it matters: Apollo is signaling discipline to insurance and wealth clients who worry private credit is over-lent to AI. Advisors selling interval funds or annuities backed by Apollo or its peers should ask for the same split of chips, leases and energy. Fixed-income PMs should note Zelter’s view that index weights could keep climbing, forcing passive buyers deeper into AI debt.
HKMA Global Financial Leaders’ Investment Summit

Wall Street CEOs split on AI financing risk at Hong Kong’s investor summit

At the Hong Kong Monetary Authority’s Global Financial Leaders’ Investment Summit, held November 2 to 4, Pimco CEO Emmanuel Roman said AI financing could reach about $10 trillion over five years and warned that a downturn will eventually break things, Bloomberg reported. Morgan Stanley CEO Ted Pick put AI capex at $10 trillion to $15 trillion over the next decade. Blackstone president Jon Gray said some of today’s skepticism is probably healthy.

Why it matters: Pimco’s CEO is warning about AI debt while Morgan Stanley’s CEO projects up to $15 trillion of spending, and both agree the financing need is enormous. PMs should size AI exposure across equity, investment-grade, high-yield and private credit together, not sleeve by sleeve. Advisors should expect clients to ask whether AI is a bubble and prepare a position-level answer.
