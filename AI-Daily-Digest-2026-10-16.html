Major AI Updates
Axios

FTC sends OpenAI and Anthropic 50-page demands over AI safety incidents

The Federal Trade Commission issued civil investigative demands to OpenAI and Anthropic on October 14, Axios reported, each running more than 50 pages. They seek records on security incidents, safeguards, marketing materials and privacy policies, and the agency plans to compel top executives to testify and to send demands to other AI firms. The same day, eight states including Arizona, Iowa and Kansas sued OpenAI, alleging it misled users, including children, about ChatGPT’s safety.

Why it matters: US consumer-protection enforcement now runs alongside the EU AI Office requests reported this week. Firms deploying OpenAI or Anthropic tools with clients should expect vendor disclosures to change and should ask for incident-notification terms in contracts. Compliance teams should also keep their own records of model outputs shown to clients, since regulators are asking how products were marketed as safe.
Semafor

White House superintelligence task force starts a 120-day AI risk review

The White House Super Intelligence task force adopted its charter at its first meeting on October 15, Semafor reported, starting a 120-day window. It asked the President’s Council of Advisors on Science and Technology for a risk report on cybersecurity, biosecurity and model control. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton chairs the group, Defense Undersecretary Emil Michael will draft breach-reporting protocols for labs, and FTC Chair Andrew Ferguson leads enforcement coordination. How compliance will be enforced remains unclear.

Why it matters: The 120-day clock points to federal rules on lab breach reporting by about mid-February. Treasury Secretary Bessent sits on the group, so financial-sector expectations could follow. Firms should map which vendors are frontier labs, confirm how those vendors would notify clients of a breach, and expect export controls to be cited as a lever over labs that resist.
Meta AI blog

Meta releases Muse Spark Open, an 816-billion-parameter open-weight model

Meta released Muse Spark Open on October 15, a natively multimodal model with 816 billion total parameters, 42 billion active per request and a one-million-token context window. Meta calls it the most capable US open-weight model to date and says it targets coding and agentic work. Weights are on Hugging Face, Amazon SageMaker, Google Cloud and Microsoft Foundry under the Muse Model License, which limits commercial use. It runs locally on two Apple M5 Ultra Mac Studios.

Why it matters: A strong open model that runs on in-house hardware gives firms a way to keep client data off third-party APIs. Technology teams should check the license’s commercial limits before any build and test the model against current vendors on their own tasks, since Meta’s performance claims are self-reported and open weights shift security work onto the firm.
Microsoft AI

Microsoft finishes pre-training MAI-Base-2, a 2.8-trillion-parameter in-house model

Microsoft AI said on October 15 it completed pre-training MAI-Base-2, a mixture-of-experts model with 2.8 trillion total and 80 billion active parameters, trained on 16,000 GB200 GPUs over 38 days in August and September. Microsoft says pre-training was 4.2 to 12.3 times more compute-efficient than DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Base and that the model beat every open-weight model it tested. It is now in post-training, and Microsoft plans to license weights to trusted partners through Foundry, with no date given.

Why it matters: Microsoft is building a credible alternative to OpenAI inside its own stack, which could change pricing and model choice in Copilot and Azure. Firms standardized on Microsoft should ask which model sits behind each Copilot feature and how changes are disclosed, since a model swap can alter outputs that compliance teams have already reviewed and approved.
AI in Wealth Management
Focus Financial Partners release

Focus Financial picks Nevis as its enterprise AI platform across its business units

Focus Financial Partners, with more than $500 billion in client assets, said on October 15 that Nevis will be the enterprise AI platform for Focus Partners Wealth and its other business units, automating advisors’ administrative work. Nevis deployment teams will work directly with Focus technologists and advisors. Focus made an undisclosed investment in Nevis, which says platform assets grew from $50 billion to $700 billion this year and has raised $40 million from Sequoia, ICONIQ and Ribbit. No rollout date was given.

Why it matters: Focus already runs Jump for 800 advisors, so this adds an operations layer rather than replacing notetaking. Aggregators weighing a firm-wide AI vendor should ask how one platform handles partner firms with different custodians and CRMs, who owns the data and what deployment support is included, since Focus chose a vendor that embeds its own staff.
RIABiz

TD Bank names Schwab’s former head of firmwide AI to run its US wealth unit

TD Bank announced on October 13 that Hardeep Walia, Schwab’s head of firmwide AI for about 18 months, became head of U.S. Wealth on October 5, RIABiz reported on October 15. At Schwab he chaired the AI Working Group and sat on the AI Council before leaving in August, a month before Schwab’s September 14 Anthropic deal. Walia founded Motif Investing and led a 250-person engineering group at TD Ameritrade. TD’s U.S. wealth arm has about $16 billion in assets under administration.

Why it matters: A bank choosing an AI executive to run a wealth business signals that technology now drives the growth plan, not just support. Advisors at TD should expect faster tool rollouts. Competing RIAs and custodians should note that AI leaders are now recruited as business heads, which raises the bar for their own AI talent planning.
InvestmentNews

MyComplianceOffice adds AI surveillance linking employee chats to prediction-market bets

MyComplianceOffice, which serves more than 1,500 financial firms, about half of them RIAs, extended its personal-trading controls to prediction markets, InvestmentNews reported on October 14. The update adds AI-assisted surveillance of employee messages on Telegram and Slack and combines messages, trades, conflicts, disclosures and nonpublic-information exposure into one review. MCO sorts bets by whether they relate to securities or company events. Combined Kalshi and Polymarket monthly volume reached $53 billion in July, up from $26 billion in May.

Why it matters: Prediction markets give staff a new route around insider-trading controls, and Goldman, Point72 and Balyasny have already restricted them. CCOs should update codes of ethics to cover event contracts, decide whether Slack and Telegram fall inside monitored channels, and ask surveillance vendors how AI flags are reviewed and documented before relying on them in an exam.
AI in Asset Management
Hedgeweek

Stock-picking hedge funds lose 2.9% in a week as crowded AI bets unwind

Global fundamental long-short hedge funds lost 2.9% in the week to October 15 but remain up 14.3% for the year, Goldman Sachs prime brokerage data cited by Reuters show. Managers sold global AI stocks for six straight sessions. Asia-focused stock pickers fell 6.8% in the first half of October as Korea’s KOSPI dropped 8% on October 15. Goldman said fundamental, systematic and multi-strategy funds all fell more than 80 basis points on the same day three times this year.

Why it matters: Diversification across hedge fund styles failed on the days that mattered, because many managers held the same AI theme. Allocators should ask managers for AI-related gross and net exposure, not just sector weights, and stress-test multi-manager portfolios for a joint drawdown. Advisors using liquid alternatives to hedge tech-heavy equity books should check for that overlap.
Reuters (syndicated)

Some Anthropic investors now discuss a $1.5 trillion IPO valuation, down from $2 trillion

Attendees at Anthropic’s October 14 investor day discussed a valuation of about $1.5 trillion for its planned November share sale, below the roughly $2 trillion previously expected, three of four sources told Reuters on October 15. Some were disappointed that third-quarter revenue grew 45% to $16.8 billion after nearly doubling in the second quarter. The prospectus shows a 2025 loss of $42 billion and at least $518 billion of committed cloud and compute spending over a decade.

Why it matters: This week’s revenue figures are now feeding directly into price. Because the offering is a bellwether for a possible OpenAI listing, PMs should treat the final valuation as a signal for private AI marks across crossover and venture funds. Advisors fielding allocation requests should explain that a lower price reflects slower growth, not a failed deal.
Bloomberg

Nscale’s $35 billion IPO plan meets a six-month delay at its Norway site

Nscale, planning a New York listing valued at up to $35 billion, has its flagship Norway data center about six months behind schedule, with 6.4 of a planned 30 megawatts running, Bloomberg reported on October 15. Cooling leaks, local rules, construction delays and a fire caused the setback, which reportedly triggered penalties owed to Microsoft and ByteDance. The prospectus cites $103 billion of contracted backlog, against first-half revenue of $140.6 million and a $1.02 billion net loss.

Why it matters: Neocloud backlogs depend on delivery, and missed megawatts become penalties before they become revenue. IPO allocators should check build progress against contracted start dates rather than headline backlog, and note how concentrated revenue is. Credit PMs holding CoreWeave-style paper should apply the same test, since construction risk is now visible across the pipeline.
