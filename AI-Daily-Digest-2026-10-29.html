Major AI Updates
CNBC (Alphabet Q3 results)

Alphabet loses about $500 billion in its worst trading day ever

Alphabet shares fell about 13% on October 29, erasing roughly $500 billion of market value, after third-quarter results showed $51.4 billion of capex and free cash flow of minus $9.1 billion. Google Cloud revenue rose 103% to $30.7 billion, but total revenue of $127.6 billion and EPS of $2.12 missed estimates, the latter hit by an $8.7 billion equity-securities loss. Alphabet held 2026 capex at $195–205 billion and said 2027 spending will rise significantly, with a figure due with fourth-quarter results.

Why it matters: Investors now punish AI spending that outruns cash flow, even with cloud revenue doubling. PMs should check how much index and growth-sleeve risk sits in hyperscalers with negative free cash flow. Advisors fielding questions should note that a single-day $500 billion loss can hit cap-weighted S&P exposure before any client touches a tech fund.
CNBC

Microsoft’s Azure grows 48% while capex falls short of its own outlook

Microsoft reported fiscal first-quarter revenue of $92.3 billion on October 28, up 19% and above its forecast range. Azure grew 48% in constant currency against guidance of 44–45%. Capital spending was $45.2 billion, short of the “over $50 billion” outlook, and Microsoft now expects about $175 billion for calendar 2026, down from about $190 billion, as more data-center leases are booked as operating leases. Commercial backlog reached $677 billion, up 73%. Shares rose about 4% after hours.

Why it matters: Lower reported capex partly reflects accounting, not less building, since leases move off the capex line. Credit analysts should add lease commitments back when comparing hyperscaler spending. PMs weighing Microsoft against Alphabet should note the market rewarded faster revenue conversion and steadier cash flow, not lower ambition, and should expect Microsoft to keep financing growth through leases.
Amazon press release

AWS grows 39% to $46 billion as Amazon’s free cash flow turns sharply negative

Amazon reported on October 29 that AWS revenue rose 39% to $46 billion in the third quarter, its fastest growth in years, with $17.8 billion of operating income, an annual run rate of about $184 billion. Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow fell to minus $42.4 billion from plus $14.7 billion a year earlier as AI data-center spending climbed. Amazon is on track for roughly $220 billion of 2026 capital spending, the largest of any hyperscaler.

Why it matters: All three hyperscalers now show accelerating cloud growth paid for with shrinking or negative free cash flow. Fixed-income PMs should expect more bond and lease financing from these issuers. Advisors should remind clients that Amazon, Microsoft and Alphabet together are a large share of most core equity holdings, so earnings-day swings reach balanced portfolios.
AI in Wealth Management
WealthManagement.com

Schwab switches on Claude for its 17,000 RIAs with a $4,800 seat credit

Schwab Advisor Services said at its IMPACT conference on October 28 that RIAs can now connect Claude for Financial Advisors to Schwab Advisor Center data, read-only, with account numbers masked. Firms signing up by November 30 get a $4,800 credit, a year of the 20-seat Enterprise minimum, though usage is billed separately. Schwab also opened an introductory version of Wealth.com’s Ester tax and estate tool, free until February 2027, with a paid version in the first quarter.

Why it matters: Schwab is trying to fix the seat minimum that shut out most of its roughly 11,000 RIAs under $100 million. Small firms should price total cost: advisers cite $75–120 a month of usage per user on top of seats. Compliance teams should confirm what client data reaches Claude before the November 30 deadline.
InvestmentNews (Schwab IMPACT)

Schwab CEO says some AI agents try 50 logins a week and are blocked

Schwab chief executive Rick Wurster said at IMPACT on October 28 that the firm blocks third-party AI agents from logging in for clients, with a single agent attempting 30 to 50 logins a week. He named Meta’s Muse and OpenAI’s Dots as agents moving into consumer finance. Schwab lets LLMs connect only on terms it controls, through its own MCP server, and does not let agents move money. Client trading through LLMs and APIs is growing.

Why it matters: Schwab joins JPMorgan and UBS in drawing a line between sanctioned connections and agents that reuse client credentials. Advisors should tell clients that pasting Schwab logins into a consumer agent may breach account terms. RIAs building workflows should use Schwab’s approved connectors rather than screen-scraping tools that may be cut off.
WealthManagement.com

CAIS, FINNY, Conquest and 55ip plug their data into Claude for advisors

Anthropic added partners to Claude for Financial Advisors on October 27, the opening day of Schwab IMPACT. CAIS connected its CAISey agent for fund eligibility, capital calls and unfunded commitments. Conquest added advice checks against US, Canadian and UK frameworks, FINNY added prospecting data, and J.P. Morgan Asset Management’s 55ip added ActiveTax transition analysis. Jump released seven Claude skills, including Client Commitments and Decision Log, built on its meeting data.

Why it matters: Claude is becoming the place where alternatives, tax transitions and prospecting data meet, rather than each vendor’s own portal. Firms choosing vendors should ask whether a tool ships an MCP connector. Supervisors should map which connectors each advisor enables, since every connector widens the client data the model can see and must be logged.
Raymond James FQ4 2026 results (SEC 8-K)

Raymond James rolls out an AI meeting manager tied into CRM and supervision

Chief executive Paul Shoukry said on the October 29 earnings call that Raymond James is rolling out an AI-powered meeting manager that summarizes meetings, drafts action items and writes notes into CRM and supervisory systems, saving advisers “hours and hours a week.” He gave no usage figures. The firm reported fiscal fourth-quarter net revenue of $4.27 billion, up 15%, record client assets of $1.94 trillion, and a technology budget above $1.1 billion a year.

Why it matters: Wiring notes into supervision, not just CRM, answers the main compliance objection to AI notetakers. Recruiters competing for Raymond James advisers should expect the tool in the pitch alongside Client360. Firms using Jump or Zocks should check whether notes reach their surveillance systems or stop at the CRM, a gap examiners may test.
AI in Asset Management
Bloomberg

Pimco sold about $3 billion of Meta data-center bonds before AI debt slid

Pimco sold roughly $3 billion of the bonds financing Meta’s Louisiana data center, a deal of about $18 billion it anchored in 2025, while they traded above par, Bloomberg reported on October 28. Filings show it also sold about half of its $10 billion position in Oracle’s Michigan data-center debt, which was issued at 98.7 cents and now trades near 88. Pimco passed on the Broadcom-Anthropic financing but bought a $4 billion slice of SpaceX’s GPU deal.

Why it matters: The largest anchor buyer is trimming AI project debt while still adding selectively, a signal on where it sees value. Fixed-income PMs should check exposure to single-tenant data-center bonds, which have fallen in recent weeks. Advisors using Pimco or other core-plus funds should ask how much AI infrastructure debt sits in those holdings.
Morningstar Q3 2026 CEO letter (SEC 8-K)

Morningstar says its data connectors logged 13 million AI tool calls

Chief executive Kunal Kapoor’s third-quarter letter, filed on October 28, says cumulative tool calls on the PitchBook Data Premium Connector passed 13 million, up from 1 million in July. About 28% of PitchBook and Morningstar Direct clients have used its MCP offerings. Direct AI, launched October 7, has about 3,500 users, 86% of whom have run a query or agent, and more than 42,000 prompts. A first AI-native standalone workflow product is due in November.

Why it matters: Asset managers increasingly reach licensed data through their own AI tools rather than terminals, so data licenses now need agent-use terms. Research teams should audit which models pull Morningstar or PitchBook data and under whose license. Morningstar’s test, in which a leading model found emissions data for only 12–15% of companies, argues against relying on public-web answers.
InvestmentNews

Invesco brings its model portfolios into Claude for advisers

Invesco, which manages $2.5 trillion, said on October 27 it is building a connector that lets advisers using its model portfolios see that data inside Claude for Financial Advisors. The connector also carries Invesco’s practice-management research, starting with a referral program and ready-made scripts, plus custom-portfolio requests. MSCI added a connector the same day for portfolio, private-assets, index and climate analytics. Invesco gave no launch date, pricing or adoption figures.

Why it matters: Asset managers are competing for model-portfolio shelf space inside the advisor’s AI tool, not just on custodian platforms. Distribution heads should decide whether their models and research are reachable from Claude. Home-office teams should check that AI-surfaced portfolio data carries the same disclosures and performance caveats as the approved fact sheet.
