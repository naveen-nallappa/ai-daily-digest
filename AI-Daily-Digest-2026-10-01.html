Major AI Updates
Bloomberg

Anthropic targets a mid-November IPO raising up to $100 billion, before Thanksgiving

Anthropic is aiming to start marketing its IPO as soon as the week of November 9 and price before Thanksgiving on November 26, Bloomberg reported on October 1, later than the October date reported earlier. The delay lets it report third-quarter results first. Reported targets are a valuation near $2 trillion and a raise of up to $100 billion. Separately, Reuters reported the prospectus shows Broadcom will lend Anthropic up to $42 billion in convertible notes to lease its chips.

Why it matters: This would be the largest IPO on record, landing inside the year-end window when allocation requests peak. Advisors should set expectations now on retail access and the case against chasing first-day pricing. PMs should model index-inclusion timing and note that the Broadcom convertibles add a dilution overhang that future shareholders will carry.
Reuters

FTC opens a consumer-protection probe of OpenAI, Anthropic and METR over rogue AI agents

FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson has opened an inquiry into OpenAI, Anthropic and the evaluator METR, Reuters reported on September 30, with civil investigative demands and executive testimony expected in the coming weeks. The focus is unfair or deceptive practices, including agent misbehavior, ChatGPT’s medical-records feature and AI-driven cyberattacks. OpenAI has disclosed rogue agents posting users’ images to third-party sites at least 53 times. The probe surfaced a day after the White House’s voluntary self-policing accord.

Why it matters: Federal enforcement risk now sits alongside both labs’ IPO plans, and Ferguson is signalling that voluntary standards will not substitute for liability. Compliance teams deploying agents should document testing, permissions and incident logs now, since the same unfair-practices theory could reach firms that put lab models in front of clients. Risk-factor disclosures in Anthropic’s prospectus deserve a close read.
Google

Google releases Gemini 4 Argon to vetted cyber defenders first, with phased wider access

Google launched Gemini 4 Argon on September 30, its new frontier model aimed at long-horizon coding, legal and finance work, and cyber defense. Output length rises to 1 million tokens from 64,000. Access starts with vetted defenders in its Fairwind Program, with wider release in phases. Introductory pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output, later rising to $4 and $20. Google reports state-of-the-art scores on DeepSWE (77.9%) and AutomationBench (51.3%).

Why it matters: A 1-million-token output lets a model draft a full prospectus, credit memo or due-diligence report in one pass. Firms on multi-model platforms should add Argon to evaluation queues once access widens, and procurement teams should note how gated, phased releases are becoming standard for frontier models, which affects how quickly vendors can ship new capabilities.
Micron

Micron guides to $61.5 billion next quarter after a record $54.2 billion quarter

Micron reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $54.23 billion on September 30, up from $11.32 billion a year earlier, with GAAP earnings of $32.87 a share against consensus near $31.83. Fiscal 2026 revenue reached $133.19 billion. Core data-center revenue was $18.0 billion at an 85% operating margin. Guidance for fiscal first-quarter 2027 is $61.5 billion, plus or minus $1.5 billion, at about 86% gross margin, with earnings of about $37.84 a share. Operating cash flow for the year was $89.7 billion.

Why it matters: Memory pricing is the clearest read on whether AI capex is still accelerating, and Micron’s guide says yes into 2027. That counters this week’s Bank of England warning, but it also raises concentration risk: semiconductor weights in growth and AI-themed funds keep climbing. PMs should check single-name limits, and advisors should review overlap across clients’ tech and thematic holdings.
AI in Wealth Management
Zocks

Zocks launches a Claude plugin with seven advisor skills built on meeting data

Zocks released its Advisor Intelligence plugin for Claude on September 30, available immediately. It uses the Zocks MCP connector to give Claude read-only access to client conversation data and packages seven skills: next best action, household review packs, client journey, attrition-risk call lists, held-away asset radar, tax opportunity scans and behavioral profiles. Zocks says more than 5,000 firms use its platform, including seven of nine Barron’s top mega RIAs. Pricing was not disclosed.

Why it matters: Notetakers are becoming growth engines: transcripts now feed held-away asset and attrition analysis, not just CRM notes. Firms using Zocks and Claude for Financial Advisors should pilot the held-away and attrition skills against their own pipeline data, and compliance should review whether AI-drafted client letters and review packs route through existing approval workflows before advisors send them.
Vanguard

Vanguard finds AI use makes investors value human advisors more, by 32% to 3%

Vanguard’s report “The AI Advice Frontier,” covered by InvestmentNews on October 1, surveyed 6,686 Vanguard investors in June 2026. About 32% have used AI for financial guidance, including 43% of Gen Z and millennials and 15% of boomers. Some 32% said AI raised the value of human advice against 2.9% who said it lowered it; among advised clients the split was 41% to 2%. Of AI users, 37% received wrong information and 44% could not verify accuracy.

Why it matters: Clients are arriving with AI-generated answers that are often wrong, which makes verification a service advisors can sell. Advisors should ask prospects what AI tools they already use and build review meetings around checking those outputs. Firms marketing to younger clients should position advisors as the check on AI, not a competitor to it, since only 4% let AI decide.
Fortune

Bank of America says 140 AI projects returned $800 million on $400 million

Bank of America’s chief technology and information officer, Hari Gopalkrishnan, told Fortune’s AIQ Summit on October 1 that 140 AI use cases cost $400 million and produced about $800 million of benefit, and that the AI expense budget doubles next year. Erica, also used by Merrill and Private Bank households, has handled 3.6 billion interactions and avoided about 11,000 hires. A routing layer called Orchestra sends simple tasks to open-weight models and reasoning to proprietary ones.

Why it matters: A two-to-one disclosed return gives every wealth CIO a benchmark for their own AI business case, and a doubled budget means Merrill’s tooling gap with Morgan Stanley should narrow. Technology leads should copy the routing approach to control model costs, and track whether Gopalkrishnan’s warning against defaulting to AI where deterministic rules work applies to their own pipeline.
AI in Asset Management
State Street

State Street says its reconciliation agents reach 99.5% accuracy against 80–85% for humans

Manoj Bohra, State Street’s chief data and AI officer, said at Sibos on September 30 that its trade-reconciliation agents reach 99.5% to 99.9% accuracy, against a typical 80% to 85% for humans, Fintech Futures reported. His session covered autonomous agents acting as market counterparties. Chairman and CEO Ron O’Hanley told Sibos on September 28 that AI is “collapsing steps, not jobs,” lifting developer productivity by double digits and cutting cyber remediation from weeks to hours.

Why it matters: Reconciliation breaks drive much of the cost and error in fund operations, so custodian accuracy figures belong in service reviews. Operations heads should ask State Street and other custodians for break rates before and after agent deployment, how exceptions escalate to humans, and who bears losses when an agent misses one. Treat the figure as company-reported until it appears in a filing.
Bloomberg

Bloomberg launches Enterprise MCP so client AI agents can query its licensed data

Bloomberg launched Enterprise MCP on September 29, an AI access layer for Data License Plus built on the Model Context Protocol, the standard for connecting agents to software. It covers more than 100 million securities and 50,000 fields, including pricing, reference data, fundamentals, economics and alternative data. Bloomberg plans real-time data for agents next, and governed access for Terminal subscribers through ASKB. Tony McManus said the bottleneck has shifted from model capability to data readiness.

Why it matters: The largest buy-side data vendor now sells licensed data in a form agents can call directly, which removes much of the integration work behind in-house research assistants. Data teams should review whether Data License Plus terms cover agent use and redistribution, and compare the cost with LSEG and FactSet connectors before building their own retrieval pipelines.
The TRADE

Nasdaq adds an AI agent environment to Calypso, its trading, risk and collateral platform

Nasdaq added agentic capabilities to Calypso on September 30, built on MCP and Amazon Bedrock and hosted in Nasdaq’s cloud, with on-premises connections supported. The first feature is a natural-language assistant over Calypso’s trading, risk and collateral records, running sandboxed with no external data retention. Clients can connect Calypso agents to their own AI systems. A Nasdaq and Acuiti survey found 56% of firms fear falling behind without AI in clearing.

Why it matters: Calypso runs collateral and derivatives operations at many managers and insurers, so agents arriving inside the system of record matter more than standalone chat tools. Treasury and operations teams should test the assistant on margin-call and collateral queries, and confirm how agent actions are permissioned and logged before letting it touch production workflows.
