Major AI Updates
Bloomberg

OpenAI weighs a pre-IPO round above $1.2 trillion, pushing its listing past 2026

The Financial Times and Bloomberg reported September 15 that OpenAI has held early talks with investors about a private round valuing the company at more than $1.2 trillion, roughly 41% above the $852 billion post-money mark from its $122 billion March raise. The approach came from investors rather than the company, and terms remain fluid. Proceeding would let OpenAI defer the IPO that Sam Altman ruled out for 2026 on September 12, citing safety concerns, while Anthropic keeps its October Nasdaq target.

Why it matters: Clients holding OpenAI through SoftBank, venture feeders or secondary platforms should expect another year without public-market liquidity, and advisors should treat the new round as a mark, not a realizable exit. For IPO desks and pre-IPO fund sponsors, Anthropic becomes the only frontier-lab listing in the 2026 window, which concentrates allocation demand and pricing power in one book.
Axios

Trump calls AI doom a 'hoax' in third rebuke of the Amodei slowdown call

President Trump wrote on Truth Social September 14 that fears of AI destroying humanity are "a HOAX" and that the only guardrail AI needs is a "STRONG AND SMART (High IQ!) PRESIDENT," his third dismissal of the Amodei-led pacing call in as many days. He accused Amodei of "pretending to be a 'perfect little angel'" and described a "SICK conspiracy" against data centers that benefits only China. Former AI czar David Sacks backed the position, and China's Foreign Ministry spokesman Guo Jiakun separately called the essay fear-mongering.

Why it matters: The White House has now taken the opposite side of the labs on pacing, which lowers the odds of federal capability-threshold rules before the midterms and pushes the regulatory risk toward Congress and the states. Advisors fielding questions after Monday's chip selloff can point to policy, not enforcement, as the near-term variable; the Senate duty-of-care draft is the item to watch.
Google (company blog)

Google ships Gemini 3.8 Live as Apple opens its Gemini-powered Siri beta

Google released Gemini 3.8 Live and 3.8 Live Extended Thinking on September 15 across the Gemini API, AI Studio, Gemini Enterprise, Search Live and Workspace. The Extended Thinking model reasons and speaks simultaneously, switches among 97 languages mid-conversation and took the top spot on Artificial Analysis' speech-to-speech index at 82.6. The same day Apple opened the English public beta of its rebuilt Siri, built on custom Gemini models with processing split between the device and Private Cloud Compute, excluding the EU on several platforms and still on hold in China.

Why it matters: Voice is now the default interface on the phone most clients carry, and it runs on Google models, so firms building client-facing assistants should target Gemini and Siri entry points rather than a proprietary app. Compliance teams should assume AI-transcribed client calls become routine by year-end and confirm recording-consent and books-and-records policies cover voice agents.
AI in Wealth Management
Morgan Stanley (investor relations)

Morgan Stanley says AI-scaled wealth unit means 2026 is not a peak-earnings year

Co-President Dan Simkowitz told the Barclays Global Financial Services Conference on September 15 that Morgan Stanley does not view 2026 as a peak-earnings year, citing wealth management, a developing capital-markets recovery and continued AI-related financing demand. The wealth unit now serves more than 20 million households, up from 2.5 million in 2019, and oversees more than $8 trillion, with AI tools scaling service without matching growth in advisor headcount. He sized the US wealth market at roughly $60 trillion growing toward $100 trillion.

Why it matters: Morgan Stanley is underwriting growth on AI-driven advisor productivity rather than recruiting, which sets the benchmark every wirehouse and independent will be asked about when Q3 prints begin in mid-October. Advisors should expect households-per-advisor targets to rise; RIAs competing for workplace-plan participants should note the funnel is now 20 million households deep and AI-serviced by default.
Vanguard (press release)

Vanguard survey: 37% of advisors name compliance the top barrier to AI automation

Vanguard released a national survey of 549 US advisors on September 15, fielded in July through Escalent's Cogent Beat instrument. Advisors mainly use AI for administrative work: drafting emails (38%), research (35%) and meeting notes (27%). Compliance and home-office hesitance led the barriers at 37%, followed by lack of time (34%) and limited proficiency (31%); 22% worry AI undercuts their value. Some 72% want more time for prospecting. Lauren Wilkinson, CIO of financial advisor services, said firms must move from assisting tasks to automating them.

Why it matters: The 37% compliance blocker is the number Vanguard wants home offices to see, since it sells model portfolios as the 400-hours-a-year time saver. Broker-dealers should read it as a mandate to publish approved AI-use policies before the Claude and ChatGPT advisor suites reach their reps, and asset managers should expect model-portfolio due diligence to weigh time-saved claims alongside performance.
PLANADVISER

AI-native RIA Arca embeds Altruist's custodial API after passing $1 billion in assets

Arca Financial, the AI-native wealth manager that emerged from stealth in June with $64 million in funding, named Altruist its preferred custodian and technology partner on September 14 and embedded Altruist's Realtime Custodial API directly into its proprietary platform so its AI agents can run client and operational workflows on live account data. Arca crossed $1 billion in client assets after acquiring Sandbox Financial Partners in May. Altruist is itself being absorbed into Vanguard under the $4.6 billion deal announced August 26.

Why it matters: An AI-native RIA just chose its custodian for API depth rather than brand, weeks before that custodian becomes Vanguard-owned. Schwab, Fidelity and Pershing RIA teams should expect real-time API access to enter custody RFPs, and firms evaluating AI-native acquirers or roll-ups should ask which custodian the agents are wired to, because switching costs now sit in the integration layer.
WealthManagement.com

Pave Finance raises $15 million Series A at a $100 million pre-money valuation

Pave Finance, a New York AI-driven trading and portfolio management platform for RIAs, announced September 14 an oversubscribed Series A of more than $15 million at a $100 million pre-money valuation, following a $14 million seed in 2025. The platform represents $130 billion in assets across more than 300,000 accounts, tracks more than 50,000 securities and integrates with Schwab, Fidelity and BNY Pershing for onboarding. Investors included advisory firms, former financial-services executives and board members, and insiders; proceeds fund engineering and go-to-market hiring.

Why it matters: Portfolio construction, not note-taking, is where the next wave of advisor-AI capital is going, and Pave's $130 billion footprint at a $100 million mark looks inexpensive next to Savvy's $600 million valuation last week. Advisors on Orion or Envestnet should ask whether their rebalancer roadmap includes natural-language constraint entry, which Pave and FinTurk both now ship.
AI in Asset Management
Bloomberg

AllianceBernstein sees hyperscaler capex topping $1 trillion in 2027 despite slowdown calls

AllianceBernstein fixed-income strategists said September 15 that calls to pace frontier AI development will not stop hyperscalers and data-center operators from raising capital and spending, with senior investment strategist Thierry Taglione describing the financings as long-term plans. The firm projects combined hyperscaler capex will exceed $1 trillion in 2027, while flagging that growth should moderate in the years after, a deceleration it says could pose broader risks to US expansion. The note landed a day after the Amodei-endorsed slowdown call knocked 5.9% off the chip index.

Why it matters: For credit PMs the message is that AI-linked issuance, from hyperscaler bonds to data-center ABS and neocloud loans, keeps coming regardless of the safety debate, so spread discipline rather than supply scarcity is the risk. Allocators should stress-test a 2028 capex deceleration in infrastructure and private-credit sleeves that were underwritten on straight-line growth.
ABC News (Australia)

Nvidia-backed Firmus starts roadshow for an October ASX IPO of up to A$7 billion

Firmus Technologies began investor meetings in Sydney and Melbourne this week ahead of a planned October listing on the ASX that could raise up to A$7 billion (about US$5 billion), which would be Australia's second-largest float after Telstra. Reports put the target valuation as high as $30 billion, roughly double the $15.5 billion July mark, on top of a $10 billion Blackstone-led debt facility and an August $2 billion round joined by Nvidia, Coatue and Jane Street. Firmus signed a multi-year OpenAI deal last week for two Malaysian sites; no prospectus has been lodged.

Why it matters: This is the first pure-play AI data-center IPO of size outside the US, and it will price Blackstone's and Jane Street's private marks in public. PMs holding Asia-Pacific infrastructure or the Nvidia supply chain should get on the book early; retail-heavy Australian distribution and a co-CEO with a 2016 insider-trading conviction are the governance items for the diligence file.
Bloomberg

Z.AI raises $5 billion in Hong Kong through shares and zero-coupon convertibles

Z.AI, the Beijing model developer formerly known as Zhipu AI, said in a Hong Kong exchange filing on September 13 that it raised about $2 billion by placing 21.97 million shares at HK$714, a 10% discount, plus 20.14 billion yuan ($3 billion) of zero-coupon convertible bonds due September 2027 with a HK$892.50 conversion price. About 60% of proceeds fund next-generation models and a self-training system. The company listed in January and raised $4 billion in July, taking total capital raised to nearly $9.5 billion in nine months.

Why it matters: A zero-coupon 12-month convertible at a 25% premium is a bet that Hong Kong AI equities re-rate before September 2027, and EM managers holding Z.AI have now absorbed 10% placement discounts twice in two months. Allocators benchmarking China AI exposure should treat repeated follow-ons as the funding model rather than a one-off and size positions for further supply.
