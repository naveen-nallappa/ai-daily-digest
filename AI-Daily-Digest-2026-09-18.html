Crusoe raises $3.9 billion Series F at a $30.9 billion valuation

Crusoe announced a $3.9 billion Series F on September 17, co-led by Atreides Management, Mubadala Capital and Valor Equity Partners, with Founders Fund, GIC, Nvidia, Qatar Investment Authority and TPG participating. The round triples the $10 billion mark set last October and follows a five-year, roughly $13 billion GPU contract with Jane Street signed September 3. Proceeds fund the OpenAI-anchored Abilene, Texas campus and factory-built Spark modular data centers from plants in Colorado and Tulsa. Crusoe has met Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley about a near-term IPO.

Why it matters: The IPO pipeline for AI infrastructure now has a $30 billion name behind CoreWeave, and the Jane Street contract shows quant funds paying lab-scale prices for private compute. Private-markets desks should mark Crusoe exposure inside GIC, QIA and TPG feeder vehicles, and PMs should model modular capacity as a hedge against data-center moratorium risk.
NBC News

House votes 417-3 to make large data centers pay their own grid costs

The House passed the Ratepayer Protection Act 417-3 on September 16, amending the 1978 PURPA so state regulators must consider standards requiring data centers drawing 100 megawatts or more to cover the full cost of grid upgrades built to serve them. Sponsored by Reps. Gabe Evans and Kathy Castor, the bill drew only three no votes and is likely the last major measure before the November 3 midterms. Companion Senate bills exist but have not advanced, and Scottish lawmakers backed a temporary hyperscale moratorium the same day.

Why it matters: A near-unanimous House vote signals that data-center power costs are now a bipartisan campaign issue, which raises the odds of state-level tariff changes even if the Senate stalls. Utility and hyperscaler analysts should re-run capex models with data centers absorbing interconnection costs, and advisors fielding client questions on rising power bills now have a concrete legislative reference point.
Fortune / AP

King Charles presses Nvidia, OpenAI and DeepMind for AI controls at Scotland summit

King Charles III convened Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, Google DeepMind chair Demis Hassabis, OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar, an Anthropic representative and UK AI minister Kanishka Narayan at Dumfries House on September 17, telling them the industry needs sufficient means of control before it is too late. Buckingham Palace said delegates discussed whether governments and companies could agree on shared guiding principles but announced no agreement. Huang, speaking after the king, said safety responsibility belongs to individual companies rather than a coordinated pause.

Why it matters: This is the first head-of-state convening since Amodei's pacing essay and OpenAI's incident disclosures, and it leaves Huang publicly isolated from the slowdown camp alongside the White House. Expect the UK AI Security Institute to gain a larger evaluation role, and treat any principles document from this group as a likely input to the EU frontier-lab summit von der Leyen proposed on September 16.
Bloomberg

Modi doubles India's chip mission to $13.5 billion as Applied Materials pledges $5 billion

Prime Minister Narendra Modi opened SEMICON India 2026 on September 17 with Phase 2 of the India Semiconductor Mission, a $13.5 billion outlay over 12 years, up from $8 billion in Phase 1, plus rules allowing foreign firms to partner with Indian startups and design houses. Applied Materials committed $5 billion over 10 years including a 140-acre research park, Lam Research pledged about 10,000 crore rupees for its first Indian component fab, and Tata Electronics signed 16 supplier agreements. India still imports roughly 90% of its semiconductors.

Why it matters: India is now the third state-backed chip build-out after the US CHIPS program and China's 15th Five-Year Plan, and the Applied and Lam commitments give US toolmakers a second Asian demand base outside Taiwan. EM PMs weighing BlackRock's AI-supply-chain overweight should add Indian fabs and packaging to the Korea-Taiwan basket rather than treating India purely as a services play.

State Street goes live with Apex-powered RIA custody and an AI service layer

State Street said its RIA custody and clearing business went live the week of September 14, with a top US RIA slated as anchor client within two months and roughly 40 firms in the pipeline, according to wealth services head John Plansky. The offering runs on Apex Fintech Solutions, in which State Street took a minority stake last October, with a proprietary data, services and AI layer built on top. The firm targets RIAs above $1 billion and hired Altruist veterans Jennifer Stokes and Pete Dorsey.

Why it matters: A $50 trillion custodian re-entering RIA custody five years after selling to FNZ gives large RIAs a fourth institutional option alongside Schwab, Fidelity and the Vanguard-owned Altruist. Firms above $1 billion running platform RFPs should ask State Street for its AI data-layer roadmap, institutional financing terms for long-short strategies and whether it will waive the ETF platform fees legacy custodians charge.

Envestnet ships AI dashboard assistant and Tamarac data agent in third 2026 release

Envestnet's third platform release of 2026, detailed September 18, adds an interactive dashboard with configurable widgets, a conversational AI assistant that opens from any Insights view, and a UMP configuration portal now live for admin templates, with sponsor self-service due in the fourth release later this year. Larger clients can opt into the dashboard from mid-September. Tamarac gains an AI Data Analyst Agent for natural-language account queries and an AI migration tool for legacy reports. Conquest separately opened self-serve access for RIAs with 10 or fewer advisors.

Why it matters: With the Vestmark deal pending, Envestnet is now shipping AI at the interface layer rather than only in data services, narrowing the gap with Orion's Denali rollout. Tamarac firms should enroll in the mid-September dashboard opt-in and test the Data Analyst Agent against their own query logs before year-end, and treat Q4 sponsor self-service as the milestone for cutting UMP admin headcount.

$40 billion Allworth deploys enterprise Claude and ChatGPT and builds apps with Claude Code

Allworth Financial COO Pete Engelken disclosed on September 18 that the $40 billion RIA has rolled out enterprise Claude and ChatGPT to its entire organization and uses Claude Code to build custom proposal generation, API integrations and information-security bots. The firm pairs Jump for meeting automation, Hadrius for AI compliance monitoring, Regal for AI voice agents and StackAI for no-code workflows on top of Salesforce, Envestnet Tamarac and Schwab and Fidelity custody, fed by a decade-old proprietary data lake. Next investments target MCP expansion.

Why it matters: Allworth is the clearest public example of a large RIA treating frontier models as a build platform rather than a chatbot, with in-house engineers shipping production tools via Claude Code. Acquisitive firms should note the data-lake investment came first; AI vendor RFPs should ask whether tools expose MCP endpoints, since that is now the integration standard serial acquirers expect.

Hedgeweek

One-third of alternative-data buyers report quality decline as vendors lean on AI

Research from data consultancy Neudata, reported September 18, finds roughly one-third of investors buying external datasets say quality has deteriorated over the past two years, with buyers attributing part of the decline to vendors using large language models to generate, classify or interpret data with less human oversight. AggKnowledge co-founder Daniel Entrup said providers also cite AI as a reason to cut data-quality staff. Neudata research head Daryl Smith said funds with in-house data science increasingly take raw feeds and run their own processing.

Why it matters: Alt-data due diligence now needs a provenance step: how a vendor collected, labeled and processed the feed, and whether an LLM touched it. Allocators evaluating quant managers should ask for signal-decay attribution tied to vendor changes, and managers should budget for raw-feed ingestion rather than vendor-processed products to keep control of the transformation into signals.
Bloomberg

GMI Cloud seeks $300 million chip-backed loan at low-teens yields for Tencent compute

Nvidia partner GMI Cloud has approached banks and private credit funds for a $300 million loan to buy Nvidia chips for a Thailand facility hosted by STT GDC, with Tencent as end user of the compute, Bloomberg reported September 17. The proposed deal would pay low-teens yields and is secured by the hardware rather than the building. GMI recently closed a NT$13.9 billion, about $436 million, Taiwan loan backed by compute contracts, and Blackstone earlier anchored an $8.5 billion investment-grade chip financing for CoreWeave.

Why it matters: Chip-collateralized lending is becoming its own private-credit sub-asset class, with a spread ladder from investment-grade CoreWeave paper to low-teens neocloud loans backed by Tencent offtake. Credit PMs should underwrite GPU depreciation curves and offtake counterparty risk separately from real-estate exposure, and allocators reviewing private-credit funds should ask how much book is now secured by hardware.

Every S&P 500 sector set for Q3 earnings growth as AI capex spreads

Bloomberg Intelligence data published September 16 show all 11 S&P 500 sectors are expected to post third-quarter earnings growth, the first clean sweep since 2021, with technology and energy still leading at 62% and 111%. Barclays strategist Venu Krishna said hyperscaler AI capex is effectively revenue for storage, hardware, industrials and utilities, while Aramark raised guidance twice on $400 million to $500 million of data-center service contracts. Wells Fargo's Ohsung Kwon flagged data-center moratoriums and rising rates as the main risks.

Why it matters: The earnings breadth argument now rests on AI capex flowing to second-order beneficiaries, which supports equal-weight and industrials tilts over pure semiconductor concentration heading into October prints. PMs should pair that with the House ratepayer bill and hedge-fund Mag Seven trimming from earlier this week as evidence the financing and political risks sit with the spenders, not the suppliers.
