Major AI Updates
Nvidia Newsroom

Nvidia commits $250 million of UK compute to agentic AI safety research

Nvidia said on October 13 it will donate compute worth $250 million over five years, about $50 million a year, to UK research on agentic AI safety and security. The capacity runs through UK-based Nvidia cloud partners, with providers and terms still to be agreed. Most goes to the wider UK safety community, with a share for Oxford’s BOLD lab, UCL’s SOFAIR lab and Imperial College. The work covers secure agents, open-model security and safety benchmarks.

Why it matters: Agent safety is moving from lab policy to funded infrastructure and shared benchmarks. Firms deploying agents that touch client accounts should track the sandboxing and evaluation standards these labs publish, since supervisors are likely to cite them when asking how a firm tested an agent before letting it act.
TechCrunch

FBI arrests OpenAI engineer accused of planting a covert recording device in its office

The FBI arrested OpenAI product engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan on October 9, after the company found a recording device plugged into a television in its San Francisco office, according to reports on October 13. He is charged with intercepting oral communications and faces up to five years in prison. OpenAI said the device had encryption and a data-transfer mechanism built to evade monitoring, but it found no sign that model weights, source code or customer data were accessed.

Why it matters: Insider threats at AI labs now target strategy and model plans, not just code. Firms sharing confidential client data with AI vendors should add insider-threat and physical-security questions to vendor due diligence, including how labs detect covert devices and restrict who can reach customer data.
DealStreetAsia, citing the Wall Street Journal

Amazon in talks to buy real-time video AI startup Decart for about $7 billion

Amazon is negotiating to acquire Decart for around $7 billion, the Wall Street Journal reported on October 11. Decart was valued near $4 billion in May after a $300 million round led by Radical Ventures, with Nvidia joining. It makes Lucy, a model that edits live video in real time, and Oasis, which simulates environments for robotics and autonomous-driving training. Anthropic explored a $6 billion deal in August but dropped it in September after due diligence, Bloomberg reported.

Why it matters: Big Tech is paying close to double recent private marks for specialist model teams. Allocators in growth and venture funds should expect more premium exits for world-model and video startups, and should test which portfolio companies lack that scarcity, since Character.AI’s bankruptcy showed the downside this week.
AI in Wealth Management
Morgan Stanley Investor Relations

Ted Pick says AI agents will strengthen Morgan Stanley’s wealth scale advantage

CEO Ted Pick said on Morgan Stanley’s third-quarter call on October 14 that the firm’s wealth scale “will remain and even be aided by agents,” adding that the firm is now “double overweight” AI after early work with OpenAI. CFO Sharon Yeshaya said AI is cutting errors and checks in internal work. Wealth management posted record revenue of $9.1 billion, a 30.4% pretax margin and $232 billion of net new assets, with client assets of $8.4 trillion.

Why it matters: The largest wirehouse is framing agents as a moat, and it named no new advisor tool this quarter. Recruiters courting Morgan Stanley teams should expect AI to feature in retention talks, while RIAs pitching breakaways should be ready to show comparable meeting, research and servicing automation.
Bank of America Q3 2026 supplemental (SEC 8-K)

Bank of America expects AI benefits to reach $700 million next year

CEO Brian Moynihan said on the October 14 call that about 130 AI projects are live, each with a payback, and that their benefit should rise from about $250–300 million this year to $700 million in 2027. Wealth revenue rose 13% to $7.1 billion, with client balances above $5 trillion. Moynihan said outside agents must keep “the human in the loop before money is moved.” He told CNBC the bank hired 1,000 contractors to patch flaws exposed by models including Anthropic’s Mythos.

Why it matters: Banks are now putting dollar paybacks on AI, and cyber remediation sits on the cost side. Merrill advisors should expect productivity targets tied to these numbers, and fintechs building agent access should plan for human approval on any money movement, a line JPMorgan’s Dimon also drew on October 13.
InvestmentNews

Wealth Enhancement strategist warns AI exposure is hiding in diversified alternatives portfolios

Ayako Yoshioka, senior investment strategist at the $160 billion RIA Wealth Enhancement, said on a Goldman Sachs Asset Management webinar, reported October 13, that AI themes now run through public stocks, bonds, venture, private equity and private credit, creating a “false sense of diversification.” Goldman’s survey of 1,000 wealthy investors found 63% see alternatives as high risk, up from 56%, yet 38% plan to add. HUB’s Dennis Bielik said a private equity fund could hold 30% Anthropic exposure at current marks.

Why it matters: Advisors should map look-through AI exposure across every sleeve before adding private credit or PE. Clients whose wealth comes from tech employers are most at risk of stacking the same bet, so those households need a concentration review that counts data-center lending and AI-heavy venture holdings alongside stock.
AI in Asset Management
BlackRock Q3 2026 earnings release

BlackRock says AI is amplifying Aladdin as technology revenue rises 14% to $588 million

BlackRock reported third-quarter results on October 14 with $205 billion of net inflows, assets of $15.49 trillion and adjusted EPS of $15.93, up 38%. Technology services revenue rose 14% to $588 million and annual contract value grew 12%. Larry Fink said “AI is amplifying the value of Aladdin” and that the AI Infrastructure Partnership made its first deployment by closing the Aligned Data Centers deal. Precision ETFs, including AI-linked exposures, drew $41 billion.

Why it matters: Aladdin growth is holding near its low-to-mid-teens target as BlackRock layers AI on top, which strengthens its hand in platform renewals with managers and wealth firms. PMs should also note that $41 billion of thematic ETF inflows partly tracks AI, adding to crowding in the same names.
Citadel Securities Global Market Intelligence

Citadel Securities says retail bought $9.1 billion of stocks in one week, led by AI

Retail investors net bought $9.1 billion of US equities in the latest week, the most in 15 weeks and the third straight weekly rise, Citadel Securities’ Scott Rubner wrote on October 14. Semiconductors drew $2.6 billion, AI infrastructure $2.4 billion and neoclouds $700 million, the desk’s largest weekly buying in that group. Companies have authorized a record $1.3 trillion of buybacks this year, 43% from technology, with blackout windows lifting through early November.

Why it matters: Retail flows and corporate buybacks are converging on the same AI names into earnings. PMs managing crowding risk should size AI positions for a sharper reversal if results disappoint, and advisors should treat client requests to add semiconductors after this run as a prompt to rebalance rather than chase.
State Street Q3 2026 earnings release (SEC 8-K)

State Street wins a top RIA mandate for its AI-layered Apex custody platform

CEO Ron O’Hanley said on State Street’s October 14 call that the firm won a servicing mandate from one of the largest US RIAs for its new wealth services business, built with Apex Fintech Solutions; the client already uses Charles River’s front-office wealth platform. State Street wraps Apex’s custody APIs in its own data and AI layer. O’Hanley said AI is simplifying processes and speeding technology development. Revenue rose 13% to $4.0 billion and assets under custody reached $59.2 trillion.

Why it matters: Another large custodian is courting $1 billion-plus RIAs with an AI-ready data layer, giving firms more leverage when renegotiating with Schwab, Fidelity or Pershing. Managers and RIAs already on Charles River should ask how State Street’s data feeds their AI tools and whether integration lowers switching costs.
