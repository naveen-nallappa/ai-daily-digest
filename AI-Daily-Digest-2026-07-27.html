Bloomberg

Nvidia weighs a $250 billion backstop for OpenAI's Ohio data center

Bloomberg and the Wall Street Journal reported on July 26 that Nvidia is in early talks to guarantee as much as $250 billion of financing so OpenAI can lease a roughly $500 billion, 10-gigawatt campus that SoftBank is developing on a decommissioned Ohio uranium-enrichment site. The arrangement would let OpenAI begin controlling its own compute rather than renting from Microsoft, Amazon, and Oracle, while locking in years of Nvidia chip demand. SB Energy would help build the site's power, and terms remain unsettled.

Why it matters: The deal would concentrate an unprecedented share of AI-infrastructure risk in a single Nvidia–OpenAI credit relationship. PMs holding either name, or data-center REITs and power suppliers, should watch whether a chipmaker guaranteeing its own customer's purchases becomes a template others copy — vendor financing at this scale blurs the line between genuine demand and self-dealing.

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5 on July 24, its flagship reasoning model, priced at $5 per million input and $25 per million output tokens — unchanged from Opus 4.8 — with a new adjustable "effort" setting that lets users trade intelligence for speed and token savings.
